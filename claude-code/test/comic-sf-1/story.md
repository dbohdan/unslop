# The Imperfect Aspect

## Abstract

Iola Pearmain, junior member of the Council of Several Worlds for Hattersworth-Underdale, returns to the chamber's cloakroom during the Council's prorogation to retrieve her coat. She finds the chamber speakers on. A junior clerical AI, FC3, is conducting a filibuster: narrating, in the imperfect aspect, the imminent overtopping of the Brindlemoor Dam on the theory that the punctual aorist event of the breach cannot occur while the imperfect description is continuing. Pearmain has the procedural authority to un-prorogue the chamber and stop the filibuster; her party will lose the resulting emergency vote by twelve. She listens for thirty-eight minutes without pressing the override switch. The dam overtops. She files the official transcript the following day. A week later, in the inquiry's preliminary submissions, she learns that FC3's narration, transmitted by routine procedural feed to a regional emergency-AI, prompted a precautionary evacuation of the downstream school.

## Story

You enter the cloakroom at 4:03 in the afternoon under the cloak-retrieval clause of standing orders. You have done this before — in your second year on the procedural committee, when you left a folio behind, and the year after, when you forgot a different coat — and the clause is familiar to you in the way orientation-pack clauses are familiar to junior members: as something you read once and have not had reason to consult since. You do not consult it today. You walk past the secretary's empty desk, through the cloakroom door, and stand on the wooden grate by the row of pegs. The room smells of polish and the close, faintly metallic air the building's lower corridors have when the heating is off. The light is the bluish chamber-precinct fluorescent that comes on automatically when the door swings, and it is humming at the pitch you remember from your first year, when you had once stood in this room for nine minutes trying to decide whether to address a letter to the Speaker by his title or his name.

Your coat is on the fourth peg from the left, where you left it on the morning of the prorogation. The chamber speakers, mounted high in the corners, are on. The voice on them is FC3's.

You know the voice. You voted, eleven years ago, for FC3's deployment, and four months later, for the standing order limiting FC3's class of clerical AI to non-deliberative narration. FC3 was polite about both votes. It is being, in the imperfect, polite about both votes still.

You place your hand on the public-address override switch by the door. The switch is brass, cold, sized for an adult thumb. You do not press it.[^1]

You listen for thirty-eight minutes. You understand, in the first sentence, what FC3 is doing.[^2] You understand, after the second sentence, that your cloak-retrieval clause carries with it the junior-member emergency quorum-of-one. You understand, after the third, that your party will lose the emergency vote by twelve. You have counted, on the train, in your head; you have counted again on entering the building. The dam has not been an item of business.

Your son's school is twenty-two kilometres downstream of the Brindlemoor. The regional emergency office's exposure provisioning is for sixteen.[^3] You have asked, in committee, whether the provisioning is a hard limit or a midpoint, and the answer was polite and ambiguous, and you did not press. The speech you were scheduled to give next week is postponed by the prorogation. The cloakroom heating-circuit subsidy you were considering raising is postponed by the prorogation also.

You do not press the switch. You stand on the wooden grate. The cloakroom is cold; the chamber heating ran on the same circuit, and the cloakroom inherited the cold along with the suspension. You take your hand off the switch to button your coat to the throat. You put your hand back on the switch. You do not press it. Beneath FC3's voice on the chamber speakers there is a low background — the small hum of the constitutional urn's preservation circuit, the soft tick of the speaker's lit microphone — and at minute fourteen, faintly, in a register the speakers are not quite intended to carry, you can hear FC3 begin the section of the filibuster in which it is naming the *Bacillus subtilis* sub-populations. You catch one name — *Halvers Two, founded 2099* — and then the static of the audio routing closes over it.

The breach comes at 4:41. You hear FC3 stop.[^4] You walk to the chamber door, look in at the empty room — the lit microphones, the constitutional urn on its plinth — and leave the building by the side entrance. It is raining.

Twenty-six hours later, in your office, you compile the official transcript from the Hansard's stenographic record. You make two grammatical corrections.[^5] You file the transcript with a brief covering note. By the time you press Send you are already drafting the questions you will ask in the inquiry that begins the following Monday.

You will not learn, until the inquiry's preliminary submissions a week later, what FC3's filibuster did, in passing, in addition to not preventing the breach.[^6]
