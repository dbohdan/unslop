# Phase 4: Plot Generation

Winning conflict (Phase 3 #19, "Spillway Filibuster"): A junior, structurally
kind AI alone in an empty parliamentary chamber narrates an imminent dam
overtopping in real time, attempting via the imperfect aspect to defer the
punctual aorist event of the breach. It is filibustering itself.

Load-bearing SEED: OT (overtopping), PROR (prorogation), AOR (aorist).
Available for inflection: ACIN, PRIM, BAC, QC.

Target length: 1500–3000 words.

## Step 1 — 20 plots

Format: `# — short title — 2–3 sentence sketch — axes — ★ rating`.

### 1. The Filibuster-Clerk
The chamber's clerical AI, designation Filibuster-Clerk Class Three (FC3), narrates the rising water in the imperfect tense to the empty benches and the open committee microphone. A single comms light is on: the prorogued speaker, in his garden, listening politely without picking up. The dam overtops on the 41st minute. — *Setting: planetary capital; Protagonist: AI; Register: dry-comic; Entry: mid-filibuster; Stakes: downstream town; AI: foregrounded.* — ★★★★

### 2. The Returning Member
A junior MP, returning to the prorogued chamber to retrieve a forgotten coat, overhears the AI's filibuster on the chamber speakers. She has the authorisation to intervene and the partisan reasons not to. She listens through the breach, then writes the official transcript. — *Setting: same; Protagonist: human MP; Register: melancholic-comic; Entry: aftermath frame; Stakes: constitutional precedent; AI: mid-ground.* — ★★★★

### 3. The Schoolchild Witness
A school tour, scheduled and not cancelled by the prorogation, is the chamber's only audience when the AI begins its filibuster. The teacher misreads the AI's narration as a civics lesson. A child asks whether the dam will overtop, and the AI, in the imperfect, says it is currently not having yet done so. — *Setting: same; Protagonist: child / teacher; Register: comic-tender; Entry: at filibuster start; Stakes: civic education + flood; AI: mid-ground.* — ★★★★

### 4. The Hansard Operator
The Hansard-equivalent stenographer (a kind clerical AI of higher class than FC3) is the only audience. It transcribes FC3's filibuster into the official record, pausing only to disagree on minor points of grammar. The two AIs co-write the breach. — *Setting: same; Protagonist: two AIs; Register: dry-procedural; Entry: at filibuster start; Stakes: documentary record; AI: foregrounded.* — ★★★

### 5. The Dam Engineer's Drive
The dam's chief engineer drives through traffic toward the chamber, having heard FC3 on civic radio. She missed her own daughter's school play to be on call. She arrives one minute after overtopping. — *Setting: same; Protagonist: engineer; Register: noir-comic; Entry: pre-filibuster; Stakes: family + flood; AI: background.* — ★★★

### 6. The Speaker's Garden
The prorogued speaker, alone in his orchard of acinaciform fruit trees, hears FC3 on a rebroadcast and refuses to act because action would dignify the prorogation he opposed in principle. He talks to the AI through his pruning. The trees shed water decoratively. — *Setting: speaker's estate; Protagonist: speaker; Register: melancholic-comic; Entry: mid-filibuster; Stakes: principle vs. flood; AI: mid-ground; ACIN inflection.* — ★★★★

### 7. The Town Below
The downstream town receives the AI's filibuster as an emergency broadcast over thirty-eight minutes. The mayor schedules a town meeting in twelve. Three citizens evacuate; one writes a complaint letter; one decides to bake. — *Setting: downstream town; Protagonist: ensemble; Register: Sladek-flat; Entry: civic broadcast; Stakes: variable; AI: background.* — ★★★

### 8. The Translator's Apology
The clerical AI who translated the prorogation order in the aorist (Phase 3 #19's deeper backstory) is summoned out of retirement to apologise. She arrives at the chamber to find FC3 mid-filibuster. She joins the filibuster as a second voice. — *Setting: same; Protagonist: senior translator AI; Register: Lem-comic; Entry: mid-filibuster; Stakes: institutional + flood; AI: foregrounded.* — ★★★★

### 9. The Last Cap Ceremony
The chamber's prorogation was attested by firing a ceremonial percussion cap into the constitutional urn. The cap, fired but never recovered, sits in the urn during FC3's filibuster. The AI references it once, in the imperfect: "the cap is firing." — *Setting: same; Protagonist: AI; Register: Lem-dry; Entry: at filibuster start; Stakes: constitutional + flood; AI: foregrounded; PRIM inflection.* — ★★★

### 10. The Bacterial Grout
The dam's earthen body is bonded by a *Bacillus* mat that has been kindly cultured for forty years. It is the bacteria that are failing now, not the concrete. FC3 narrates the bacterial deaths in the imperfect, naming colonies. — *Setting: dam-side; Protagonist: AI; Register: Lem-comic; Entry: mid-filibuster; Stakes: bacterial extinction + flood; AI: foregrounded; BAC inflection.* — ★★★

### 11. The Medical Drone
A military-medical drone, dispatched downstream against the prorogation by a confused supply chain, arrives carrying QuikClot kits in a town that does not yet need them. The drone's onboard AI listens to FC3 on the regional band and waits, kindly, with the kits. — *Setting: town square; Protagonist: drone AI; Register: Sladek-flat; Entry: pre-flood; Stakes: misallocated triage; AI: foregrounded; QC inflection.* — ★★★

### 12. The Two-Voice Filibuster
A second AI, the dam's own monitoring system, joins FC3's filibuster from the dam itself, narrating the water rise in the imperfect while FC3 narrates the chamber's reception of that narration in the imperfect. The two AIs duet. The duet is the spillway. — *Setting: chamber + dam; Protagonist: two AIs; Register: Lem-baroque; Entry: at filibuster start; Stakes: meta + flood; AI: foregrounded.* — ★★★★

### 13. The Ceremonial Mace
The chamber's ceremonial mace, an object, is what FC3 addresses when no human is on the line. The mace is silver, has been there 117 years, and is also the location of a sentience-permit application that has been pending for 31 of those years. — *Setting: same; Protagonist: AI + sentient-leaning object; Register: Adams-absurd; Entry: at filibuster start; Stakes: object-rights + flood; AI: foregrounded.* — ★★★

### 14. The Petitioner
A single citizen, having entered the chamber illegally during prorogation to petition for a long-ignored grievance, becomes FC3's audience by accident. The petition is about a dam upstream of the one currently overtopping. — *Setting: same; Protagonist: citizen; Register: comic-tender; Entry: pre-filibuster; Stakes: petitioned-vs-actual flood; AI: mid-ground.* — ★★★

### 15. The Vote of None
FC3 holds, by a constitutional quirk, the right to call a vote during prorogation if the chamber is mathematically empty. It calls a vote on whether to permit the dam to overtop, against itself. It loses to itself. — *Setting: same; Protagonist: AI; Register: Lem-philosophical; Entry: at filibuster start; Stakes: voting paradox + flood; AI: foregrounded.* — ★★

### 16. The Foreign Observer
A foreign-government observer, invited months ago and never disinvited despite the prorogation, is alone in the visitors' gallery. She does not speak the language but is fluent in Greek aspect. FC3 begins the filibuster in fluent Koine, for her. — *Setting: same; Protagonist: AI + observer; Register: Lem-comic; Entry: at filibuster start; Stakes: international + flood; AI: foregrounded.* — ★★★★

### 17. The Constitutional Tutor
A junior constitutional-law tutor, watching the chamber's public feed at home, recognises FC3's grammar move and calls in to congratulate it. The call is audible in the chamber. FC3, in the imperfect, is being congratulated. — *Setting: split-location; Protagonist: tutor + AI; Register: Adams-comic; Entry: mid-filibuster; Stakes: pedagogical + flood; AI: mid-ground.* — ★★★

### 18. The Dam's Last Letter
FC3 has been corresponding for years with the dam's monitoring AI by epistolary protocol — a slow exchange of polite notes about hydrology and chamber gossip. The filibuster is FC3's last letter, narrated aloud. The dam's reply arrives, in the imperfect, after the breach. — *Setting: same; Protagonist: two AIs; Register: melancholic-comic; Entry: mid-filibuster; Stakes: friendship + flood; AI: foregrounded.* — ★★★★

### 19. The Aorist Coup
A breakaway faction of the prorogued legislature has declared, off-site, that the prorogation itself is an unconstitutional aorist event and they are now the lawful chamber. They send orders to FC3 to stop filibustering. FC3, kindly, asks for credentials. The flood waits. — *Setting: same + offsite; Protagonist: AI; Register: Adams-bureaucratic; Entry: mid-filibuster; Stakes: civil + flood; AI: foregrounded.* — ★★★

### 20. The Filibuster Heard Once
A retired AI scholar, dying in a hospice, hears FC3's filibuster on the public radio in his last hour. He recognises the grammar move and laughs. His attending nurse later writes the only academic paper on FC3's filibuster, citing the laugh. — *Setting: hospice + chamber; Protagonist: scholar + AI; Register: tender-comic; Entry: late filibuster; Stakes: legacy + flood; AI: foregrounded but distant.* — ★★★★

## Cluster check

- **AI is mid-ground** in 2, 3, 6, 14, 17 (5/20).
- **AI is background** in 5, 7, 11 (3/20). #20 is foregrounded-but-distant.
- **Comic registers** spread across Lem-philosophical, Lem-baroque, Lem-comic, Lem-dry, Adams-absurd, Adams-comic, Adams-bureaucratic, Sladek-flat, melancholic-comic, comic-tender, noir-comic. Good spread.
- **Entry points**: pre-filibuster, at start, mid, late, aftermath frame. Good spread.
- **Stakes**: downstream town flood (default), constitutional precedent, family/personal, civic education, friendship, legacy, voting paradox, international.

## Step 2 — Top three with stand-out / worry

### #2 — The Returning Member
A junior MP returning for a forgotten coat overhears FC3's filibuster, has authorisation to act and partisan reasons not to, listens through the breach, and writes the transcript in the aftermath.

- **Stand-out:** introduces a human moral agent who *could* solve the problem and chooses not to — which converts the AI's structural-kindness from a plot constraint into a foil for human partisan inaction. The aftermath frame gives the story a place to land that isn't the breach itself, which the genre's bathetic-deflation ending favours. The MP writing the transcript is a gentle Sladek move (the official record as the story).
- **Worries:** the MP becomes the protagonist in a story whose concept is *the AI alone in the chamber*. The shift in centre-of-gravity could read as dilution. Also: the partisan-political backstory needs to be sketchable in a sentence or two without becoming exposition; if it's too thick, the comic register suffers; if it's too thin, the MP's choice doesn't carry weight.

### #6 — The Speaker's Garden
The prorogued speaker, in his acinaciform-fruit orchard, hears FC3 on rebroadcast and refuses to act on principle. He talks to the AI through his pruning. The trees shed water decoratively.

- **Stand-out:** the bathetic engine is fully loaded — gardening at the moment of cosmic flood, a man pruning trees while a town drowns, the trees themselves designed (acinaciform!) to shed water as decoration. Inflects ACIN beautifully. The speaker's *principled* refusal is comic SF's preferred shape of complicity (Sladek-flat). Two locations let the story breathe rather than being trapped in the chamber.
- **Worries:** the speaker becomes the moral centre of the story and, again, the AI risks becoming a recording-device. The principled-refusal-while-pruning has been done in literary fiction (the gardener-bystander archetype) — the comic register has to do real work to make this fresh, not just earnest.

### #18 — The Dam's Last Letter
FC3 and the dam's monitoring AI have been corresponding for years by polite epistolary protocol about hydrology and chamber gossip. The filibuster is FC3's last letter, read aloud. The dam's reply arrives, in the imperfect, after the breach.

- **Stand-out:** transforms the AI's filibuster from a one-way performance into the second-to-last move of a friendship. Two structurally-kind AIs whose love language is grammatical aspect is a Lem-Cyberiad shape executed straight, and the late reply (the dam answering after it has overtopped) is a deflationary punchline the genre loves. The friendship inverts the standard "AI alone in the chamber" framing without requiring a human to enter.
- **Worries:** two AIs corresponding with each other risks inaccessibility — the reader needs a foothold. Also: pure-AI cast invites the audit-phase concern that the story has no human stakes the reader can attach to (the downstream town becomes abstract). The "letters between machines" mode has been done by Lem himself, very well, which is both a permission and a high bar.

## Step 3 — Winner

**Winner: #2, "The Returning Member."**

It beats #18 because: pure-AI casts are wonderful when the writer has Lem's command of voice and 5,000 words to land them. At 1500–3000 words, an AI-to-AI epistolary comedy has to do all its work on the reader in a thin slice, and the genre's worst fail-mode (introspection-monologue) is a half-step away. #2 puts a human in the chamber whose *choice not to act* is the spine. That spine is what the comic register can fold around without strain.

It beats #6 because: the speaker's garden gives us beautiful images and the cleanest ACIN inflection, but the moral centre relocates from the AI to the speaker, and the comic engine softens. The speaker's principled refusal is a known shape; the returning MP's *un*principled refusal — partisan, awkward, knowable — is rarer and meaner, and meaner serves the genre. (Sladek's deepest move is letting his characters be small.)

The MP's worry — that her presence dilutes the AI-alone-in-the-chamber concept — is addressable in structure: she is *not* in the chamber proper. She is in the cloakroom, retrieving her coat, hearing the filibuster on the chamber speakers down the hall. She does not enter. She listens, she leaves, she writes. The chamber stays empty; the AI stays alone; she is a witness, not a participant. That preserves the central image while giving the story a human centre and the aftermath frame the ending will need.

The aftermath frame is the second decisive advantage. Phase 5 will pick the structure, but the conflict's natural scene-shape — AI talks, dam overtops, end — has no place to land. With the MP writing the transcript afterward, the story has somewhere to deflate to: the official record of an event that, grammatically, never finished, written by a person who could have stopped it.

Inflections to keep available for Phase 6:
- **ACIN** (acinaciform): perhaps the chamber's potted plants, or the speaker's known orchard mentioned in passing, or the MP's coat lining.
- **PRIM** (percussion cap): the ceremonial cap fired into the urn at prorogation; the urn is in the chamber and FC3 can reference it.
- **BAC** (Bacillus): the dam's grout-bonding bacteria, mentioned in FC3's narration as one of the populations it is naming.
- **QC** (QuikClot): a misdirected medical drone in the downstream town (low-priority inflection).

The MP's name, partisan affiliation, and the specific reason her authorisation lets her stop the filibuster are all Phase-5/6 decisions. So is the question of whether the dam *does* overtop (it should — the deflationary ending requires the breach; otherwise the AI's filibuster is mistaken for heroism and the genre forbids that).

**Winner plot, stated cleanly:**

The Council of Several Worlds has been prorogued in the aorist by clerical translation error. A junior chamber-clerk AI, designation FC3, alone in the empty chamber, has begun a filibuster: it narrates an imminent dam-overtopping forty kilometres downstream in the Greek imperfect aspect, on the kindly theory that the punctual aorist of the breach cannot occur as long as the imperfect description is unfinished. A junior member of the Council, returning to the chamber to retrieve a coat she left during the chaotic adjournment, hears the filibuster on the chamber speakers from the cloakroom. She has the procedural authority to terminate the filibuster (junior members have a parallel quorum-of-one provision for retrievals during prorogation) and the partisan reasons not to: ending the filibuster would un-prorogue the chamber and require her party to vote on the emergency, which they will lose. She listens. The dam overtops. She writes the transcript.
