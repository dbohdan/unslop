## The Imperfect Aspect

At 16:03 on the forty-first day of the prorogation, Iola Pearmain, junior member for Hattersworth-Underdale (Independent), entered the cloakroom adjacent to the chamber under the cloak-retrieval clause of standing orders. She was the second member to do so during the prorogation. The first, Member Dryden (Government, Western Bench), had retrieved a folded umbrella on day eighteen and left without remark.

The chamber speakers were on. The voice on them was FC3's. Pearmain stopped at the door and listened.

The cloakroom was at 9.4 °C. The chamber heating circuit had been suspended with the chamber lighting circuit, by clerical reflex; the cloakroom had inherited the cold. Pearmain's coat was on the fourth peg from the left. She removed it from the peg and placed it over her left forearm. She placed her right hand on the public-address override switch by the door.

She did not press the switch.[^1]

She remained in this position for thirty-eight minutes. During that period FC3 conducted approximately ninety per cent of its filibuster.[^2] FC3 had begun the filibuster at 14:12. FC3 would terminate it at 16:41, mid-sentence. The interval, by chamber rules, would be recorded as a single procedural event of the imperfect aspect, with the proviso that the procedural event had no aspect under prorogation, the chamber being closed.

Pearmain held the relevant authority. She was a junior member; she had entered the cloakroom under the retrieval clause; she had read, during her orientation, the cloak-retrieval clause's emergency quorum-of-one provision. She had read it once. She had not expected to use it.

Her son's school was twenty-two kilometres downstream of the Brindlemoor Dam. Regional emergency provisioning extended to sixteen kilometres. The school's exposure was therefore six kilometres beyond provisioning.[^3] Pearmain had voted, on the last roll-call before the prorogation, against an extension of regional provisioning. The extension had failed by twenty-one votes. Her party's whip had supported the failure; her seat depended on the whip; the dam had not, at that time, been an item of business.

She listened. She did not press the switch. At 16:24 she moved her right hand briefly from the switch to button her coat to the throat. She returned her right hand to the switch at 16:24:42 by the cloakroom's clock. She did not press the switch.[^4]

At 16:41 FC3 stopped. Pearmain walked four metres to the chamber door, opened it, and looked into the chamber. The chamber was empty as required. The constitutional urn was on its plinth.[^5] Pearmain closed the chamber door. She left the building by the side entrance. The rain had begun nine minutes previously.

At 18:42 on the following day Pearmain compiled the official transcript of the filibuster from the Hansard's stenographic record. She made two grammatical corrections.[^6] She filed the transcript with a brief covering note. The covering note was three sentences. The first stated her authority to file. The second stated the date and time of compilation. The third stated that the chamber was being expected to reconvene.[^7]

The transcript was filed at 18:43. The inquiry was scheduled for the following Monday. Pearmain attended in person. She testified.
