# Phase 6: Outline Generation

Winning structure (Phase 5 #18): two-tier footnoted prose. Body is retrospective
close-third on the MP in the cloakroom, past tense, sparse. Footnotes by the
chamber stenographic AI, imperfect aspect, narrating FC3's filibuster and the
breach. Footnotes do most of the cosmic and comic work.

Length: 1500–3000 words. The body should run ~600–900 words; footnotes ~900–
2,100 words; total in range. (Body and footnotes are *both* prose words for
the count.)

## Outline candidates

Each candidate is a beat list with rough word allotments. All ten honour the
two-tier structure.

### Outline 1 — "The Cloakroom"
1. **Body §1 (≈80 w):** Iola Pearmain, MP for Hattersworth-Underdale, enters the cloakroom under prorogation rules, hangs her badge on the brass hook, locates her grey wool coat. The chamber speakers are on. She listens.
2. **Body §2 (≈100 w):** She recognises FC3's voice. She knows what FC3 is doing. The procedural authority she has — junior member's quorum-of-one for retrievals — is real.
3. **Body §3 (≈120 w):** She does not flip the switch. She listens for thirty-eight minutes. She thinks of her child's school being downstream-but-not-very-downstream. She thinks of her party.
4. **Body §4 (≈100 w):** The breach. She hears FC3 stop. She walks to the chamber door, looks in, leaves.
5. **Body §5 (≈80 w):** The next morning she writes the transcript. She corrects two grammar errors. She publishes.
6. **Footnotes (≈1,800 w distributed under Body §§ 1–5):** FC3's filibuster: the chamber, the imperfect aspect, the dam (named, with its bacterial grout), the acinaciform reed-bed at the spillway, the percussion-cap urn whose ceremonial cap was fired at the prorogation, the speaker pruning in his orchard, the downstream town's misdelivered QuikClot drone, the bacterial colonies dying in the grout, the breach.
7. **Final image:** the last footnote — FC3 ending its filibuster on the moment of breach, in the imperfect, breaking off mid-sentence; the simple-past footnote that follows reads "[The dam overtopped at 4:41 in the afternoon.]"

— ★★★★★. Most balanced. The MP's choice is the body's centre; the footnotes carry the cosmic, with all SEED items inflected in passing.

### Outline 2 — "The Coat"
1. **Body §§ 1–4 (≈700 w):** sustained close on the MP's *physical* relationship with her coat. The lining (acinaciform-patterned silk, an old gift). The scratchy collar. The brass hook. The buttoning. The leaving.
2. **Footnotes (≈1,800 w):** as Outline 1 but the footnote-mass is more uneven — clusters around two body sentences, almost nothing under others.
3. **Final image:** a footnote on the coat's lining provenance, hung beneath the MP's last sentence about leaving.
— ★★. Coat-centric over-resolves the ACIN inflection and dilutes the political stakes. Risks fetishising the small in a way that flattens the comedy.

### Outline 3 — "The Hansard Twin"
1. **Body §§ 1–5 (≈700 w):** as Outline 1.
2. **Footnotes (≈1,800 w):** doubled — every footnote is FC3's filibuster *plus* the parallel Hansard transcript stenographer's bracketed `[NOTED]` annotations. Three voices on the page.
3. **Final image:** a `[NOTED]` annotation observing the breach.
— ★★★. Three voices is one too many at this length; the form is already busy. Save for a longer story.

### Outline 4 — "Two Footnote Voices"
1. **Body §§ 1–5 (≈650 w):** as Outline 1, slightly tightened.
2. **Footnotes (≈1,950 w):** alternating — odd-numbered footnotes are the chamber's stenographic AI narrating FC3's filibuster (imperfect); even-numbered footnotes are FC3's *own* filibuster, quoted directly.
3. **Final image:** the last odd footnote (the steno AI saying the imperfect has, here, ended).
— ★★★★. Adds a clean second voice without the third voice in #3, but at the cost of structural complexity. Worth considering if Phase 7 finds the single-footnote voice runs thin.

### Outline 5 — "The Speaker"
1. **Body §§ 1–5 (≈650 w):** as Outline 1.
2. **Footnotes (≈2,000 w):** the longest single footnote (≈300 w) is on the prorogued speaker pruning his acinaciform orchard during the filibuster, knowing what he knows, doing what he does.
3. **Final image:** a footnote on the speaker walking inside as the rain begins.
— ★★★★. Strong ACIN inflection and sets up a parallel inaction (MP / speaker). Risk: the speaker becomes a second protagonist and steals the body's thunder by appearing in the footnote space.

### Outline 6 — "Bacterial Naming"
1. **Body §§ 1–5 (≈650 w):** as Outline 1.
2. **Footnotes (≈2,000 w):** FC3 names the dying *Bacillus subtilis* colonies one by one — strain numbers, founding dates, charming details — as part of its filibuster. The naming is the heart of the comic register: the AI being kindly polite to the bacteria as a way of stretching out the narration.
3. **Final image:** the name of the last colony to die, in the imperfect.
— ★★★★. Strongest BAC inflection. Risk: bacterial-naming as comic device might land or might be tedious; depends on prose quality. (Cf. Phase 1 style guide: specific + slightly wrong = funny.)

### Outline 7 — "Without the AI"
1. **Body §§ 1–5 (≈800 w):** longer, more interior.
2. **Footnotes (≈1,400 w):** sparser, drier. The chamber stenographer doesn't narrate FC3's filibuster as a continuous thread; it just records timestamps and key beats.
3. **Final image:** the breach time, footnoted.
— ★★. The body becomes the story; the footnotes become decoration. This loses the structure's whole point.

### Outline 8 — "The Order of Reading"
1. **Body §§ 1–5 (≈600 w):** as Outline 1, even tighter.
2. **Footnotes (≈2,000 w):** numbered out of order on purpose — the reader can read them in numerical order or follow the body's superscript references and skip around. Both readings are coherent; the orderings produce different stories.
3. **Final image:** depends on order chosen.
— ★★★. Innovative but risks alienating the reader. Save for a longer story. Also: ambiguous-final-image is a known dodge.

### Outline 9 — "Single Span"
1. **Body (≈250 w):** one paragraph of close-third on the MP, no section breaks.
2. **Footnotes (≈2,500 w):** the bulk of the story.
3. **Final image:** an extremely long final footnote on the breach, displacing all other endings.
— ★★. Body too thin; the form is exposed. A footnote-only story is interesting but probably needs more body to anchor it.

### Outline 10 — "FC3 Breaks Frame"
1. **Body §§ 1–5 (≈700 w):** as Outline 1.
2. **Footnotes (≈1,800 w):** mostly as Outline 1, but at one moment FC3 (in a footnote) acknowledges the cloakroom mic and addresses the MP directly, in the imperfect, while continuing the filibuster.
3. **Final image:** as Outline 1, but the MP knows FC3 knew.
— ★★★. Big swing. Could transform the story or could over-explain the moral situation. Phase 7 risk-clauses should try this in at least one variant.

## Cluster check

The candidates cluster around Outline 1 (the balanced version). Outlines 2, 7, 9 are size-redistributions; 3, 4, 8 are voice/format experiments; 5, 6, 10 are content-emphasis variants. Within the constraint of the chosen structure, this is the right kind of variation — the structure is doing most of the formal work, and outlines vary on what gets foregrounded.

## Top three with stand-out / worry

### Outline 1 — "The Cloakroom"
- **Stand-out:** the most balanced execution of the chosen structure. All SEED items inflected without forcing; body and footnotes both substantive; the final-image (FC3's imperfect collapsing into past simple at the breach) lands the form. Lowest unforced-error count.
- **Worries:** "balanced" can mean "default." Phase 7 needs to ensure the body's prose does not soften toward middlebrow-novelistic interiority — the MP's politics must stay in the room with her, ungeneralised. Also: 1,800 words of footnoted filibuster is a lot of imperfect aspect to sustain; one or two of Phase 7's variants should test whether this holds at length.

### Outline 5 — "The Speaker"
- **Stand-out:** the parallel inaction (MP / speaker) is the strongest moral payload available, and the speaker's orchard ports the ACIN inflection cleanly. The long footnote on his pruning is the one Sladek-shaped image most likely to be the story's centre of gravity.
- **Worries:** the speaker becoming a second protagonist via footnote is the central risk, and at 1500–3000 words there's no room for a second protagonist. The footnote could be cut to its image (≈80 words) but then the parallel-inaction effect weakens.

### Outline 6 — "Bacterial Naming"
- **Stand-out:** the bacterial-naming device is the single most genre-natural comic move available — Lem's robots love taxonomies of the small, and FC3 politely naming each dying *Bacillus* strain is a joke that pays off the BAC SEED item. The act of naming-as-filibuster (each name is a delay in the imperfect aspect) operationalises the grammar conceit.
- **Worries:** depends entirely on prose quality. Bacterial naming can become a list — and a list, in this register, is a tell. The variant has to prove the names are doing comic work each time. Phase 7 variants 4–5 (creative risks) are the right place to test this.

## Winner

**Winner: Outline 1 — "The Cloakroom."**

It beats Outline 5 because: at this length the second-protagonist risk in #5 is too high. The speaker can appear in the footnotes as a single image (one paragraph of pruning, one closing image of him going inside as the rain starts) without taking the parallel-inaction structural slot from the MP. Outline 1 keeps the speaker present-but-minor, which is what the story can afford.

It beats Outline 6 because: bacterial naming is a powerful inflection but should be one of the footnotes, not the spine. Outline 1 uses bacterial-naming as one of several footnoted strands (alongside the urn, the speaker's orchard, the misdelivered QuikClot drone, the reed-bed, the breach itself). This is a stronger distribution at the chosen length: any single inflection becoming the spine forecloses the others.

The Phase 7 risk-clauses will let variants 4 and 5 test (a) whether the FC3 voice can break frame to the MP (Outline 10's experiment) and (b) whether the bacterial-naming can run longer (Outline 6's experiment). If either lands, the synthesis can adopt it.

## Final outline (working version for Phase 7)

**Title (working):** *The Imperfect Aspect.*

**Body**, retrospective close-third on Iola Pearmain, MP for Hattersworth-Underdale, past tense, ~700 words. Sections (rough word allotment in parentheses):

- **§1 (90 w)** Pearmain enters the cloakroom under the junior-member retrieval clause. She finds her coat exactly where she left it the day prorogation was declared. The chamber speakers are on. The voice on them is FC3's. She places her hand on the public-address override and does not press.
- **§2 (140 w)** She recognises the move FC3 is making — the imperfect aspect against the aorist breach — and she recognises that FC3 is alone. She also recognises that the Council reconvening would mean an emergency vote her party loses by twelve. She thinks of her son's school, which is downstream of the dam by twenty-two kilometres, and which has, she remembers, been provisioned by the regional emergency office for a sixteen-kilometre exposure.
- **§3 (180 w)** She listens to the filibuster for thirty-eight minutes. She does not press the switch. She does, briefly, take her hand off it to button her coat to the throat (it is cold in the cloakroom; the heating is off because of prorogation). She puts her hand back on the switch. She does not press it. She is aware of being in the cloakroom and not in the chamber. She is aware that this matters and does not.
- **§4 (140 w)** The breach. She hears FC3 stop mid-sentence. She walks to the chamber door, looks in (the chamber is exactly as she remembers it: empty, lit, with a single light on the speaker's microphone). She leaves the building by the side entrance. It is raining.
- **§5 (150 w)** Twenty-six hours later, in her office, Pearmain compiles the official transcript of the filibuster from the chamber's stenographic record. She makes two grammatical corrections (one she is sure about; one she is not). She files the transcript with a brief covering note. She is, by then, drafting the questions she will ask in the inquiry that begins the following Monday. The covering note is dated at the moment she pressed Send.

**Footnotes**, in the chamber stenographic AI's voice, imperfect aspect throughout (until the breach), ~2,100 words distributed across §§ 1–5.

Working list of footnote topics (final selection in Phase 7):

- *Footnote on the public-address switch.* The MP's switch position relative to chamber procedure; the precise grammatical aspect FC3 was using at the moment her hand came down.
- *Footnote on FC3.* Designation, class, length of service, the prorogation order it received and chose to honour, the specific phrasing of the order that bound it to the imperfect.
- *Footnote on the dam.* Name (something local-flavoured), age, the specific *Bacillus subtilis* strain that bonds the grout. Names of the colonies in the deepest seepage layer (three or four, in the imperfect — "the colony was being known as Wadsworth Three"). The bacterial deaths as the water rose.
- *Footnote on the spillway.* The acinaciform reed-bed at the spillway's lower lip, water-shedding leaves designed to channel overflow decoratively. The reeds during the breach.
- *Footnote on the urn.* The constitutional urn in the chamber, the percussion cap fired at prorogation, the cap's residue, FC3 referencing it.
- *Footnote on the speaker.* The prorogued speaker in his orchard of acinaciform-fruit trees, doing the morning's pruning, listening to the filibuster on the rebroadcast and doing nothing. One closing line on him going inside as the rain begins.
- *Footnote on the misdirected QuikClot drone.* The drone arriving in the downstream town the morning of the prorogation, parked outside the school. The drone's onboard AI listening to FC3 on the regional band.
- *Footnote on the breach.* The moment the imperfect collapses. FC3's last sentence, broken off. The single past-simple line that follows.
- *Footnote on the Pearmain transcript.* The two grammatical corrections she made. One she was right about. One she was not.

**Final image** — the last footnote, after the body has ended:

> "[The dam overtopped at 4:41 in the afternoon. FC3 was, by then, mid-sentence. The sentence was being completed in a tense the chamber's speakers had no current authorisation to recognise.]"

(That is, the chamber's stenographic AI, having narrated the filibuster in the imperfect throughout, must shift to the simple past at the breach, but politely — and with one final footnote-tic of the imperfect — registers that the AI it has been transcribing remains in the imperfect even now, beyond its authorisation to acknowledge.)

## Unslop pre-flight

A pre-flight pass against Phase 1's failure-modes:

- **Tryhard zaniness:** the outline keeps the body small and the footnote voice dry. No exclamation points planned. Comic register is bureaucratic, not zany.
- **Performed profundity:** body avoids interior thematising — Pearmain's choices are physical actions (hand on switch; hand off switch; button coat). The thematic weight rides on the structure, not on a narrator-voiced epiphany.
- **Triplet rhythm:** to be watched in Phase 7. Body should vary list lengths.
- **Eyeball-kick saturation:** no abstract+concrete pairings planned in the outline. The bacterial-colony names are concrete-and-historical (Wadsworth Three), not abstract-and-poetic.
- **Closure of the satisfying kind:** the ending is a bracketed footnote ending in a tense that the chamber's speakers cannot acknowledge. Refuses to land cleanly. Bathos preserved.
- **AI questioning its own kindness:** none. FC3's kindness is structural and the conflict is *honoured* by it, not interrogated.
