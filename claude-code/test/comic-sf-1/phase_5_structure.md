# Phase 5: Structure Selection

Winning plot (Phase 4 #2): the MP returns for her coat, hears FC3's filibuster
from the cloakroom, has the procedural authority and the partisan reasons not
to act, listens through the breach, writes the transcript.

Target length: 1500–3000 words.

Reminder: structure is the *shape of the reader's experience* — order of
arrival, withholding strategy, narrative distance, temporal shape. NOT the
scene list (Phase 6).

## Step 1 — 20 candidates

### 1. Linear, close-third on the MP, present tense
Real-time linear from the moment she enters the cloakroom to the moment she leaves with the transcript. The reader knows what she knows, when she knows it. Filibuster is heard only through the chamber speakers. — *Default.* — ★★★

### 2. Linear, close-third on the MP, past tense
Same shape as #1, retrospective. Past tense allows the narrator's voice room for dry asides without breaking present-tense immediacy. — ★★★

### 3. Frame: the transcript
The story *is* the transcript the MP writes. It contains FC3's filibuster verbatim plus the MP's bracketed editorial annotations. The MP's own situation in the cloakroom is reported only through her annotations and one short preface or footnote. — ★★★★

### 4. Frame: the transcript, with FC3's filibuster as the body
As #3, but FC3's filibuster is the narration; the MP appears only as `[ed.]` brackets and a closing note. The reader experiences the breach as the AI did, with the MP's choice readable only between the lines. — ★★★★★

### 5. Two POV alternation: MP and FC3
Sectioned alternation between close-third on the MP in the cloakroom and direct narration by FC3 to the chamber. Both voices in present tense; the breach is the convergence point. — ★★★

### 6. FC3 as the narrator, retrospective
After the breach, FC3 narrates the entire event back to the reader, including its own awareness of the MP's presence. Lem mode: a robot recounting its own predicament with rueful precision. — ★★★

### 7. FC3 as the narrator, in real time
FC3 narrates throughout, in the imperfect tense per its filibuster. The MP is heard only through the cloakroom mic FC3 (politely) refuses to acknowledge having patched into. — ★★★★

### 8. The Hansard frame
The story is the official Hansard transcript, complete with `[FC3]:` and `[CLOAKROOM, NOTED]:` and other parliamentary stenographic conventions. No prose. The bathos is the format. — ★★★★

### 9. Reverse chronology
Opens with the MP filing the transcript a week after the breach. Each section moves earlier — the breach, the filibuster's middle, its start, her decision to keep listening, her deciding to enter the cloakroom. Ends with her noticing she left her coat. — ★★★

### 10. The single sentence
One impossibly long Lem-style imperfect-aspect sentence narrating the event from start to finish, breaking only at the breach and resuming for the aftermath. The whole story in three paragraphs. — ★★ (high risk)

### 11. Prologue + close-third
A short cold-open in the chamber the morning of the prorogation (the clerical AI mistranslating into the aorist), then linear close-third on the MP. — ★★★

### 12. Embedded transcript
Linear close-third on the MP. Verbatim chunks of FC3's filibuster appear as block-quoted insertions. The MP's interiority sits next to FC3's voice; the reader compares them. — ★★★★

### 13. The school of fish
Distant third, very wide. The reader hears multiple voices simultaneously — FC3, the MP, the speaker in his garden, the dam, the downstream town's mayor — in short cross-cut sections. The MP's choice is one note in the chord. — ★★★

### 14. Epistolary
A small set of documents: FC3's official log, the MP's personal note to herself in the cloakroom, the speaker's gardening journal, a downstream town's emergency-broadcast transcript. The reader assembles. — ★★★

### 15. Frame: the academic paper
The story is presented as a scholarly paper — published years later — analysing the constitutional precedent of the FC3 filibuster, with the MP's transcript as an appendix and the breach reduced to a footnote. — ★★★★

### 16. Direct address
FC3 addresses the reader directly. The reader is positioned as the empty chamber. The MP's listening from the cloakroom is the only secondary plane the AI acknowledges, and it does so obliquely. — ★★★★

### 17. Linear with one chronological hinge
Linear close-third on the MP, but with one short flashback to the morning of prorogation, placed at the structural midpoint to recontextualise her partisan reasons. — ★★★

### 18. Footnoted prose
The main narrative — close-third on the MP, brief — is covered with footnotes in FC3's voice (or in the dry voice of the chamber's stenographic AI). The footnotes are the comedy. The body is the tragedy. — ★★★★

### 19. The compound fragment
A series of short numbered fragments (1, 2, 3, …, 41 or so), each one minute of the filibuster from a different vantage — FC3, the MP, a janitor, the dam's fluid sensor, the prorogued speaker, an old woman watching the chamber's public feed. The breach is fragment 42. — ★★★

### 20. The trial transcript
The story is FC3's testimony at a constitutional inquiry, post-breach. Counsel asks; FC3 answers. The MP appears as a named witness in the record. The filibuster, the breach, and the MP's choice are reconstructed entirely through cross-examination. — ★★★

## Cluster check

- **Linear close-third** (1, 2, 11, 12, 17): five candidates — somewhat clustered. They differ on tense, prologues, embedded inserts.
- **Framed-as-document**: 3, 4, 8, 14, 15, 18, 20 — broad spread (transcript, Hansard, epistolary, paper, footnotes, trial).
- **AI-narrated**: 6, 7, 16 — three different distances.
- **Distributed**: 5, 13, 19 — multi-vantage.
- **High-risk single-sentence / reverse / multi-vantage**: 9, 10, 13, 19 — adequately represented.

The default-cluster (chronological narration with a late reveal) is barely represented; I've pushed candidates toward shapes that make the AI's grammar-conceit visible *as form*, not just *as content*.

## Step 2 — Top three with stand-out / worry

### #4 — Frame: the transcript, with FC3's filibuster as the body
The body of the story is FC3's filibuster as written into the official transcript (in the imperfect aspect, narrating the rising water minute by minute). The MP appears only in `[ed.]` bracketed annotations (corrections, time-stamps, the occasional dry note) and in a short closing paragraph after the breach. The reader experiences the filibuster from the AI's narrative position; the MP's presence and choice surface obliquely.

- **Stand-out:** form *is* concept. The reader is reading the document the MP produced; the document carries FC3's voice as primary text and the MP's voice as the editorial silence around it. The bathetic engine — a typeset official record of an AI talking to nobody about a flood — is in the page itself. The MP's complicity becomes visible in what she *chose to annotate vs. let stand*. Genuinely Sladek-shaped.
- **Worries:** sustained imperfect-aspect prose risks tedium; the comic register has to find variation inside the constraint. The `[ed.]` annotations have to do real work without becoming the joke. Length pressure: at 1500–3000 words, the filibuster body and the MP's editorial frame must both be substantive without padding.

### #18 — Footnoted prose
The body is a brief, deflationary close-third on the MP — her cold cloakroom, her coat, her hand on the public-address switch, her decision not to flip it. Footnotes throughout, in the chamber stenographer's dry voice, narrate FC3's filibuster, the dam's failure, the bacterial colonies dying in the grout, the exact moment of breach. The footnotes are longer and more numerous than the body. The reader reads down and up.

- **Stand-out:** the form embodies the politics. The MP's small choice gets the body of the page; the cosmic event gets the footer. This is exactly the bathetic move comic SF runs on, made structural. It also gives the comedy two voices (the body's close-third and the footnotes' AI voice) and lets each modulate the other. Adams's Hitchhiker's Guide is the obvious reference, used for purpose, not decoration.
- **Worries:** footnoted fiction is a high-effort form for the reader; if the footnotes are too clever, the body suffers; if the body is too thin, the form is a gimmick. Implementation risk in Markdown — footnote rendering varies by reader. This is also a known David Foster Wallace move that has been imitated badly enough to be genre-tainted; the comic-SF voice has to be specific enough to escape that.

### #16 — Direct address
FC3 addresses the reader as if the reader is the empty chamber. The filibuster is *to us*. The MP in the cloakroom is acknowledged once, obliquely, and the AI politely asks whether we'd prefer she be folded into the narration or left in peace. The reader is the chamber.

- **Stand-out:** maximally Lem-y. The narrator-as-character is foregrounded to extremity, and the reader is implicated in the empty-chamber position. The grammatical conceit (imperfect aspect to defer the aorist) becomes the reader's own experience — *we* are the audience FC3 is filibustering to, and our reading time is the imperfect that holds the breach off. The story ends when we finish reading.
- **Worries:** the move risks being too cute. Direct-address fiction has known failure modes (the Reader becoming a weak character, the conceit overwhelming the story). It also makes the MP's role thin: if she is acknowledged only once, why include her at all? Could end up writing #7 (FC3 narrating in real time) with a thin direct-address veneer.

## Step 3 — Winner

**Winner: #18, Footnoted prose.**

It beats #4 because: #4 makes the AI the protagonist by making the AI's voice the body. The plot's chosen shape (Phase 4 #2) put a human in the chamber complicit in inaction; #4 dilutes that choice by relegating her to brackets. #18 *honours* the complicity by giving the human her short, mean, embarrassed body of text — and then surrounds it with the AI's narration in the only place that makes formal sense for narration the human chose not to acknowledge: below the line.

It beats #16 because: direct address is louder, and louder is closer to the genre's worst failure mode (tryhard zaniness, narrator-performing-charm). #18 is quieter and more bureaucratic, which the genre prefers. The AI does not perform; the document just exists, with footnotes. The Adams comparison is apt but the Sladek comparison is the deeper one: this is the form of an *official record* whose footnotes are the actual story.

Format risk (footnote rendering) is real but addressable: the story will use Markdown footnote syntax that GitHub renders correctly, and the Phase 7 writers can adjust if a particular variant wants inline footnotes formatted differently.

The known-bad-DFW-pastiche risk is the substantive one. Mitigation: the footnotes are not the AI showing off; they are the AI working. The footnotes' voice is the dry chamber-stenographer (Sladek-flat), not the high-energy Wallace footnote (digressive-clever). The comedy is in *what gets footnoted* (the cosmic) versus *what gets bodied* (the small), not in the footnotes' wit.

**Stated cleanly, in narrative-theory terms:**

A two-tier prose form. The body is brief retrospective close-third, past tense, on the MP in the cloakroom — her actions and inaction during the filibuster, told in spare prose. The footnotes are the chamber stenographic AI's official annotations to her account, written in the imperfect aspect (per the filibuster's own grammar), narrating FC3's filibuster, the dam, the bacterial deaths in the grout, the speaker in his garden, the breach. The footnotes accumulate beneath the body; the body is sparse; the footnotes do most of the cosmic work and most of the comedy. Reader's understanding of the MP's choice arrives through the *contrast* between the small body and the large footnoted apparatus. Final image: the very last footnote, after the breach, after the body has ended.

The structure makes the imperfect/aorist distinction visible: the body is in the past tense (events that happened, completed) and the footnotes are in the imperfect (events that were happening, ongoing). The breach in the footnotes is the moment the imperfect collapses into past simple.
