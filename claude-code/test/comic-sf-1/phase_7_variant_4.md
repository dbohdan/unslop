## The Imperfect Aspect

Iola Pearmain, junior member for Hattersworth-Underdale, entered the cloakroom at 4:03 in the afternoon under the cloak-retrieval clause of standing orders. Her grey wool coat was on the fourth peg from the left, where she had left it on the morning of the prorogation. The chamber speakers were on. The voice on them was FC3's.

She knew the voice. She had been on the procedural committee that authorised FC3's deployment, eleven years previously, the year she was elected. She had voted for. She had also voted for the standing order, four months later, that limited FC3's class of clerical AI to non-deliberative narration, on the recommendation of the same committee. FC3 had been polite about both votes. It was being, in the imperfect, polite about both votes still.

She placed her hand on the public-address override switch by the door. She did not press it.

She listened. She understood, after the first sentence, what FC3 was doing.[^1] She understood, after the second, that her cloak-retrieval clause carried with it the junior-member emergency quorum-of-one. She understood, after the third, that her party would lose the emergency vote by twelve. She had counted, that morning on the train, in her head; she had counted again on entering the building.

Her son's school. Twenty-two kilometres downstream. Provisioning to sixteen. She thought of these things; she thought of three other things at the same time.[^2]

She listened to the filibuster for thirty-eight minutes.[^3] At minute twenty-one, FC3 paused for half a second, in a manner that was, to anyone familiar with FC3's ordinary cadence, distinctive. Pearmain noticed.[^4]

She did not press the switch. She did not move her hand from the switch. Her right hand stayed on the switch through the rest of the filibuster as a small physical fact about her presence in the room. Her left hand was buttoning her coat. Her left hand was unbuttoning her coat. Her left hand returned to her side. Her right hand stayed on the switch.

The chamber speakers, which were the cloakroom speakers also during prorogation by virtue of an audio-routing protocol enacted at some point in the cloakroom's history, were carrying FC3's voice at a sound level Pearmain was being aware of and not adjusting. She could have, by leaving the cloakroom and entering the corridor, made the voice quieter. She did not leave the cloakroom. She thought, briefly, about the audio-routing protocol, who had enacted it, when, and for what reason, and whether the reason had ever been recorded. She did not look it up. She continued to listen.

The breach came at 4:41. She heard FC3 stop.[^5] She walked to the chamber door, looked in, and left the building by the side entrance. It was raining.

Twenty-six hours later, in her office, she compiled the official transcript from the Hansard's stenographic record. She made two grammatical corrections.[^6] She filed the transcript with a brief covering note. She did not include, in the transcript or the covering note, any reference to the cloakroom audio. She did not include, either, any reference to the half-second pause at minute twenty-one. The Hansard's stenographic record had captured the pause; the chamber's standing reference materials were silent on whether the pause was being part of the filibuster.

She filed at the moment she pressed Send. The inquiry began the following Monday. She testified.

She did not, in her testimony, mention the pause.
