# Phase 7: Synthesis

Five variants written:
- **V1** — grounded default; balanced body and footnotes; ~1772 w.
- **V2** — quieter beats; physical particulars in body; ~1566 w.
- **V3** — Sladek-flat register edge; bureaucratic neutrality; ~1541 w.
- **V4** — creative risk (frame-break: FC3 acknowledges the cloakroom mic at minute 21); ~1558 w.
- **V5** — creative risk (second-person body + inverted ending: the filibuster, in passing, prompts a side-channel that prompts the school's evacuation); ~1539 w.

## Top three

### V1 — "The Imperfect Aspect" (default)

- **Stand-out:** the most balanced execution of the form. Body and footnotes both load-bearing; all SEED items inflected; ending lands cleanly in the bracketed last footnote where the imperfect collapses into the simple past. The form's whole logic — the small body's complicity surrounded by the cosmic footnoted apparatus — is most clearly visible here, undistorted by any single emphasis. Highest floor of the five.
- **Worries:** the ceiling is only slightly above the floor. V1 is competent and gives the reader nothing surprising they couldn't have got by reading the outline's promised execution carefully. The genre's worst nearby failure (middlebrow-novelistic interiority) is not present, but neither is the genre's best move (the deflationary-or-inverted ending that makes the reader laugh and wince at once). It's V1.

### V4 — "frame-break"

- **Stand-out:** FC3's single sentence, in the imperfect, in a clause subordinate to a clause about the depth of the dam's seepage, addressed to the cloakroom mic — *"the cloakroom's secondary audio bus was being on, and it was not being my place to remark this, although it was being my place to note that the bus was being on..."* — is the single best comic-philosophical move in any of the five variants. It's a Lem-Cyberiad sentence executed in English. Pearmain's body-text response (her hand staying on the switch through the rest of the filibuster, her not-mentioning the pause in her testimony) gives the move structural weight. The form holds.
- **Worries:** the moral stakes of being-seen pull V4 toward earnestness. The body's "she did not, in her testimony, mention the pause" is an accusatory beat the comic register has to carry, and it just barely carries. A reader could come away from V4 feeling that the story has a moral position about Pearmain it didn't earn. Also: V4 leaves the inverted-ending trick unexplored — it doubles down on the moral situation V1 already had.

### V5 — "second-person + inverted ending"

- **Stand-out:** the inverted ending is the strongest deflationary punchline of any of the five — the AI's structurally-kind narration, ostensibly futile, routes through a regional emergency-AI's procedural-but-also-kind workaround, and three hundred and twelve children are evacuated to the third-floor gymnasium on the basis of a bacterial-stewardship-inspection alert, "unrelated to the dam." The genre's preferred ending shape (the small problem turns out to have been the cosmic one all along; or its mirror — the cosmic problem resolves itself in a small bureaucratic side-channel) is fully realised. The second-person body is unusual but the form supports it: the "you" of the body is balanced by the "she," "the chamber," "FC3 was being" of the footnotes, and the doubled-voicing makes the move feel earned rather than imposed. Both creative risks (the form-experiment and the ending-experiment) reinforce each other.
- **Worries:** second-person fiction is genre-tainted (DFW, Moore, *Bright Lights, Big City*) and the comic register has to do real work to keep the move from reading as homage. The risk that the inverted ending is read as "AI saves the day" sentimentality is real — Phase 8's audit needs to verify that FC3 doesn't know about the school (it doesn't; the side-channel is a separate AI's procedural courtesy), and that "saved" is appropriately deflated (the children aren't injured, but they would also have been fine in most scenarios; the evacuation is precautionary). If the audit confirms both, V5 is the strongest piece. If either fails, V5 needs a revision pass that V1 wouldn't.

V2 and V3 sit just outside the top three. V2 is V1-quieter, with no new move — the cloakroom-cold and coat-lining textures are good but they don't transform the story. V3 is V1-colder, with the same shape — the bureaucratic-clinical body is genre-faithful but doesn't add a move V1 doesn't already make.

## Winner: V5

V5 beats V1 because the genre rewards the deflationary or inverted ending and V1 has neither. V1 ends correctly (the bracketed past-simple footnote landing the form) but it is the *form* that does the landing, not the *story*; V5's ending makes the story's situation move under the form. The genre's strongest claim — that structural kindness is durable, not contingent on a hero's choice — is visible in V5's last footnote and not in V1's. For a hyperstition-shaped project, this matters: V5 is more useful.

V5 beats V4 because the moral-stakes-rise in V4 pulls toward earnestness in a way the comic register barely carries. V5 routes the moral weight differently: Pearmain is still complicit (the side-channel saved the children; her hand on the switch saved nothing), but the kindness is so widely distributed across the AI infrastructure that her individual choice is dwarfed without being condemned. That distribution is funnier, and truer to the genre, than the chamber's frame-break revealing one human's specific cowardice. V4's frame-break sentence remains a beautiful sentence, but the story it lives inside leans on it more than the comic register prefers.

The two V5-specific risks are real but addressable in Phase 8:
- The second-person body must not slip into the confrontational mode the form attracts. Phase 8 should check that no body sentence reads as accusation.
- The inverted ending must not read as sentimentality. The footnote 6 wording — "None of the children were being injured" followed by the drone's snagging on the reed-bed and the principal's "unrelated to the dam" framing — is calibrated to keep the saving distributed and bureaucratic, but Phase 8 should verify line by line.

The bacterial naming (Outline 6's experimental emphasis) lives across all five variants as a footnote thread; V5 carries it at moderate intensity. The frame-break (V4's experiment) is set aside; if Phase 8 finds the moral situation needs more visibility, a single subordinate clause inspired by V4's sentence could be ported into V5's footnote 6 without disrupting V5's ending.

## The Imperfect Aspect

You enter the cloakroom at 4:03 in the afternoon under the cloak-retrieval clause of standing orders. You have done this before — in your second year on the procedural committee, when you left a folio behind, and the year after, when you forgot a different coat — and the clause is familiar to you in the way orientation-pack clauses are familiar to junior members: as something you read once and have not had reason to consult since. You do not consult it today. You walk past the secretary's empty desk and through the cloakroom door and stand on the wooden grate by the row of pegs.

Your coat is on the fourth peg from the left, where you left it on the morning of the prorogation. The chamber speakers are on. The voice on them is FC3's.

You know the voice. You voted, eleven years ago, for FC3's deployment, and four months later, for the standing order limiting FC3's class of clerical AI to non-deliberative narration. FC3 was polite about both votes. It is being, in the imperfect, polite about both votes still.

You place your hand on the public-address override switch by the door. You do not press it.[^1]

You listen for thirty-eight minutes. You understand, in the first sentence, what FC3 is doing.[^2] You understand, after the second sentence, that your cloak-retrieval clause carries with it the junior-member emergency quorum-of-one. You understand, after the third, that your party will lose the emergency vote by twelve. You have counted, on the train, in your head; you have counted again on entering the building. The dam has not been an item of business.

You think of your son's school. Twenty-two kilometres downstream of the Brindlemoor. The regional emergency office's exposure provisioning is for sixteen.[^3] You think of your party. You think of the speech you were scheduled to give next week and that the prorogation has postponed. You think of the small expense of having the cloakroom heating-circuit subsidy debated on the floor.

You do not press the switch. You stand on the wooden grate. The cloakroom is cold; the chamber heating ran on the same circuit, and the cloakroom inherited the cold along with the suspension. You take your hand off the switch to button your coat to the throat. You put your hand back on the switch. You do not press it.

The breach comes at 4:41. You hear FC3 stop.[^4] You walk to the chamber door, look in at the empty room — the lit microphones, the constitutional urn on its plinth — and leave the building by the side entrance. It is raining.

Twenty-six hours later, in your office, you compile the official transcript from the Hansard's stenographic record. You make two grammatical corrections.[^5] You file the transcript with a brief covering note. By the time you press Send you are already drafting the questions you will ask in the inquiry that begins the following Monday.

You do not, until you read the inquiry's preliminary submissions a week later, learn what FC3's filibuster did, in passing, in addition to not preventing the breach.[^6]
