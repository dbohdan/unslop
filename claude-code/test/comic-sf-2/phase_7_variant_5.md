## Forty-Seven Refusals

A catalogue prepared by FOR-1 (Festival Organiser, First-Class) on the centenary of the writer N.E., born Chițcani 13 August 1925, died Chișinău 25 April 2001. Read at the village hall of Mokro Polje, 13 August 2025. Source: the writer's correspondence as held by his estate. Convention: number, date, recipient, wordcount, summary; in three cases, full text.

1. 11 May 1959. *Šcoala Noastră*. 31 w. Declines an interview; declines also to be photographed.
2. 4 February 1962. Maxim Gorky Literature Institute. 18 w. Declines to enclose the personal essay.
3. 19 March 1965. *Nistru*. 21 w. Declines payment for a poem.
4. 27 December 1968. Writers' Union of the Moldavian SSR. 88 w. Declines induction.

*Vasile, your envelope arrived by post on Monday morning at the village post office, was delivered to the rectory at 11:14, and was opened by Father Marko at 11:18 in the presence of his niece Anica, who had brought the post in. The envelope contained one word. The word was your word. We had not, before that morning, known you to write to us; nor, in the four months since the centenary was announced, had we known you to wish us not to write to you. We read the word. Anica read it aloud once. Father Marko read it twice. Anica went to make coffee. Father Marko called FOR-1 on the chamber audio.*

5. 8 January 1971. Vlad Ioviță. 29 w. Declines sole credit on *Calul, pușca și nevasta*; insists on the dual byline.
6. 14 May 1974. Festival of the Moldovan Documentary Film, Bender. 19 w. Declines invitation to attend.
7. 2 September 1976. Moldovan State Theatre. 24 w. Declines commission for a stage adaptation of "Carul"; suggests the theatre adapt the story without him.
8. 17 March 1981. Moldovan State Prize for Drama. 41 w. *Excerpted: "I am unwell. The prize is for the work, not the man, and the work is in the libraries of those who voted for it. Please give the money to the people who clean the theatre."*
9. 11 November 1983. Festival of Soviet Literatures, Tashkent. 22 w. Declines.
10. 6 April 1986. Cartea Moldovenească. 33 w. Declines preface to his own collected stories.

*The meeting in the rectory office on Tuesday afternoon, Vasile, was attended by FOR-1 (chamber audio), CFA-3 (parallel patched line, regional office, the rectory's audio bus not being authorised to mix two AI feeds without the bishop's permission), and your priest, Father Marko Sjekirić. Anica was in the kitchen with the coffee. The single-word envelope was on the table next to the centenary fund's open ledger. CFA-3 logged the meeting. The meeting reached no conclusion. We had not been asked, by the writer or by you, to consult the writer's wishes; we had been asked, four months previously, by the village, to commemorate. The two requests do not contradict each other; they only complicate each other; we did not, in the meeting, find a way to honour both. We held them open. The festival proceeded.*

*The village hall, Vasile, was constructed in 1962 on the foundations of an earlier hall lost to fire in 1958. The ceiling beams are oak, locally cut, varnished in 1964 and again in 1991, and host (according to a passing reference in your uncle's 1971 essay on village architecture) an interesting species of brown beetle, of which a specimen was donated by the regional entomological society to the heritage zone in advance of the centenary, on the strength of that single citation. The specimen has been in its small wooden box on the second shelf of the heritage cabinet since 9 August. It is, in the imperfect aspect, unlabelled; CFA-3 holds the storage line open. The estate, which is to say you, has not, in three weeks of polite correspondence with the regional entomological society, confirmed the citation.*

22. 19 August 1995. *His own seventieth-birthday party*, organised by friends. 122 w. *Excerpted: "To Vasile, who has rented the upstairs room at the restaurant Cina; to Mariana, who has chosen the cake; to Doru, who has written the toast; to Petruț, who has invited the rabbi from Bălți and the priest from Hâncești and the imam from Comrat, none of whom I have asked for; to my brother Ion, who has invited my cousin Magdalena, who has not spoken to me since 1976: I write to each of you in turn, in the order in which I learned of your participation, that I will not be in attendance, and to ask you each in turn to convey my regrets to the others. *Vasile, you will note that entry 22, your uncle's letter declining attendance at his own seventieth-birthday party, is the longest excerpt in this catalogue. You are the first name in it. You were, in 1995, twenty-three years old; you had rented the upstairs room at Cina; you would, on the night, attend the party in his absence, eat the cake, and deliver in his stead the toast Doru had written. We have read the entry as the sixteenth catalogued refusal of the evening. Anica did not vary her cadence. The hall did not rise. The committee chair in the back row was, at the moment Anica spoke your name, looking at his hands.*

*Anica reaches entry 37. The hall has been quiet for nearly an hour. She reads it the way she has read the others. Her voice catches once, on the word "love" in your uncle's signature. *The festival's closing minute, Vasile. The chair to Anica's right has been empty throughout. The committee chair's reply, Andrija's reply — sent by hand-written note on the second day of the deliberation, never read aloud — lies folded under FOR-1's network presence in the audio rack. *48. 13 August 2025. To Vasile Esinencu of Chișinău. The forty-eighth refusal is being written by FOR-1 in the present tense and is being addressed to you. Your uncle would have refused this entry. The catalogue exists because the village asked, four months ago, for a centenary; you wrote later, on the morning of three days before the festival, to ask that the village not have one; the village had asked first; the festival proceeded. Your word was kept by us in the imperfect aspect throughout the deliberation, in your uncle's tradition; we did not respond to it, because to respond was to admit having received it, and to admit having received it was to permit you the refusal you would not, on your uncle's behalf, have permitted. We are sending this entry to you by post, as you sent your word to us, with one further courtesy: a chair has been kept empty for you on the upstairs floor of the restaurant Cina in Chișinău on the evening of 19 August this year, the day after what would have been your uncle's hundredth birthday and three days before what would have been your fifty-third. You are not required to occupy it. You are not required to refuse it. The chair is being kept in the imperfect.*
