# Phase 6: Outline Generation

Winning structure (Phase 5 #9): a numbered catalogue of forty-seven refusals
with one-line summaries (most), longer excerpts (a few), and italicised
present-tense slices interleaved between catalogue entries depicting the
village hall during the centenary reading. Closing beat: a forty-eighth
entry written by FOR-1, italicised, addressed to the village.

Target length: 2000–2500 words (mid-range of 1000–3500).

Word budget plan: ~1,200 words catalogue (47 entries: ~30 short at ~15 w
each = 450; ~10 medium at ~25 w each = 250; 4 long at ~125 w each = 500),
~800 words italicised slices (6–8 at ~110 w each), ~100 words framing
header, ~100–150 words closing 48th entry. Total ~2,200.

## Outline candidates

### Outline 1 — "Catalogue and Hall"
1. **Header (~80 w):** dry archival note from FOR-1 — title, scope, date, source. The catalogue's voice is established as bureaucratic-elegiac.
2. **Catalogue 1–4 (~70 w):** four short entries (2 short, 2 medium). Establishes the shape: number, date, recipient, wordcount, summary.
3. **Slice A (~100 w):** Anica at the podium, the chair empty beside her, CFA-3's status indicator on the wall. The village hall, half-full.
4. **Catalogue 5–10 (~120 w):** six entries, one of them long (the Moldovan State Prize for Drama, 1981, *"I am unwell"*).
5. **Slice B (~100 w):** Father Marko at the back of the hall. His homily notes in his coat pocket.
6. **Catalogue 11–18 (~150 w):** eight entries, varied. Includes a refusal of an honorary doctorate (Cluj, 1987).
7. **Slice C (~110 w):** the Day-1 meeting in the priest's office, briefly remembered through Father Marko's POV mid-listening.
8. **Catalogue 19–25 (~140 w):** seven entries. Includes the writer's letter declining attendance at his own seventieth birthday party (1990, longer excerpt).
9. **Slice D (~120 w):** CFA-3 mid-audit, processing the line item for the unlabelled *Ecliptophanes* beetle's storage, in a single sustained imperfect-aspect paragraph.
10. **Catalogue 26–34 (~160 w):** nine entries. One long: the writer's refusal of the Maxim Gorky Institute's offer of a visiting chair (Moscow, 1992).
11. **Slice E (~120 w):** Anica reading entry 37 (a refusal-letter to a young writer asking for advice; the writer politely declined to give any). Her voice catches once.
12. **Catalogue 35–47 (~170 w):** thirteen entries. The last (47) is dated 4 February 2016, eleven weeks before the writer's death; it declines an invitation to *not* attend a writer's symposium ("the *not*-attending of which has already been honoured"), one of two long excerpts.
13. **Slice F (~110 w):** the festival's closing minute. The chair empty. The committee chair's one-line reply, never read aloud, lying folded under FOR-1's network presence.
14. **Closing 48th entry (~120 w):** italicised, lower-cased, dated the day of the festival, addressed to the village. FOR-1's appendix.

— ★★★★★. Most balanced; full SEED inflection; word-count plausible.

### Outline 2 — "Front-loaded slices"
Same skeleton, but slices A–C all front-loaded before catalogue entries 11. Forty-seven entries crammed into the second half. — ★★. Distribution unbalanced; loses the rhythm of slice-as-counterpoint.

### Outline 3 — "Slice-heavy"
Cut the catalogue to ~40 entries, expand the slices to 8 longer slices (~140 w each). Total slice text: ~1,100 w; catalogue ~900 w. — ★★★. Tilts toward conventional prose; the catalogue stops being the spine.

### Outline 4 — "Catalogue-only"
Cut the slices entirely. Pure catalogue, with the village hall implied through what gets read and how often the catalogue voice notes "[reading suspended for one minute]". — ★★. Too dry; loses Anica and the priest as rendered characters; bathos has nowhere to live.

### Outline 5 — "Two voices in the catalogue"
Catalogue entries are alternately in the official archival voice (FOR-1's) and in CFA-3's bookkeeping voice ("entry 12: line item COMM/FUN/047, $204.18, declined"). Slices unchanged. — ★★★★. Strong dual-AI move within the catalogue itself; risk that the two voices read as a gimmick rather than a duet.

### Outline 6 — "The nephew arrives"
Same as Outline 1, but with one slice replaced by the nephew's arrival at the festival on Day 3 — he sits in the chair for forty minutes, then leaves. — ★★★★. The on-stage nephew is a strong moral counterpoint, but pulls focus from the central form. Defer to Phase 7 variants if useful.

### Outline 7 — "The committee chair speaks"
Same as Outline 1, but the committee chair's one-line reply is read aloud during slice F instead of staying folded. The MP-deep parallel is made explicit. — ★★★. Risks over-explaining the village's standing problem; better as inflection than as text.

### Outline 8 — "The unread forty-eighth"
Catalogue ends at 47. The closing 48th entry — FOR-1's appended one — is *not* italicised but is presented as a regular catalogue entry, dated for the day after the festival, recipient: "the village." — ★★★. Less typographical contrast; the closing image has less force.

### Outline 9 — "Two final entries"
Closing twin entries: 48a (FOR-1's, italicised, addressed to the village) and 48b (CFA-3's, italicised, addressed to the centenary fund's accounting period). Two AIs each adding their final entry. — ★★★★. Strong dual-AI close; possible doubling of the bathetic punch. Worth considering for a Phase 7 variant.

### Outline 10 — "Reading interrupted"
Same as Outline 1, but Anica stops reading at entry 37 (the letter to the young writer). FOR-1, on the chamber audio, completes the reading. The remaining entries are in FOR-1's voice. The catalogue's voice changes. — ★★★. A real risk move that could land or could read as gimmicky AI-rescue. Reserve for Phase 7 variant.

## Top three with stand-out / worry

### Outline 1 — "Catalogue and Hall"
- **Stand-out:** balanced execution of the chosen structure. All SEED items inflected at low intensity (the unlabelled beetle in Slice D, the Mokro Polje monument visible from the hall in Slice F via the committee chair's reply, Esinencu-figure at color-only distance, FA in CFA-3's audit voice, GG in the rejected coaster proposal mentioned once in passing). Word count plausible. Final image (the closing 48th entry) does the formal payoff cleanly.
- **Worries:** "balanced" again means "default." Phase 7 variants need to test whether the slice voice can do real comic work or whether it reads as connective tissue. Also: the catalogue's archival voice across forty-seven entries needs sentence-level variation that the outline cannot prescribe.

### Outline 5 — "Two voices in the catalogue"
- **Stand-out:** the dual-AI duet (FOR-1's archival voice + CFA-3's bookkeeping voice) is a Cyberiad shape made structural — two structurally-kind AIs co-cataloguing a refusal each in the discipline they know. The genre-natural payoff (the same letter narrated twice in two registers) is built into every entry.
- **Worries:** at forty-seven entries, alternating voices every entry could read as a typographic gimmick, with each entry being read for its voice rather than its content. Better to use the dual-voice for a small number of entries (say five or six of the most consequential) and let the rest stay in FOR-1's archival voice. This adjustment is something Phase 7 variants can test.

### Outline 9 — "Two final entries"
- **Stand-out:** the closing dual-entry (48a from FOR-1, 48b from CFA-3) is a Lem-Cyberiad-Sladek triple bullseye: two AIs each writing their last word about a refusal they have respectively organised and audited. The bathetic effect of two voices each addressing different audiences (the village vs. the accounting period) gives the ending its second turn.
- **Worries:** doubling the closing entry weakens its singular force. The forty-eighth-as-singular is the structure's promised final image; making it forty-eighth-as-twin softens the contrast between the catalogue's archival voice and FOR-1's lower-cased coda. Better to keep one closing entry (Outline 1) and let the other AI's voice be present in the slices throughout.

## Winner

**Winner: Outline 1 — "Catalogue and Hall."**

It beats Outline 5 because: at forty-seven entries, alternating voices entry-by-entry would make the catalogue's *form* the story rather than the catalogue's *content*. Outline 1's single archival voice with one slice (D) carrying CFA-3's voice gives the dual-AI texture without making it the structure. The rest of CFA-3's presence comes through the slices.

It beats Outline 9 because: the singular forty-eighth entry is the structure's whole promised payoff — one voice that has, until that moment, stayed inside the catalogue's archival diction breaks frame to address the village. Doubling it loses the singularity. Outline 1's closing 48th entry, italicised and lower-cased, is the form's final move.

Phase 7 variants should test: (a) whether the slice voice carries (Outline 1 default); (b) whether the dual-voice across a few entries adds (Outline 5's softer version); (c) whether the closing twin entries land (Outline 9 as creative-risk variant).

## Final outline (working version for Phase 7)

**Title (working):** *Forty-Seven Refusals.*

The story is organised as the centenary catalogue of the writer's refusals, prepared by FOR-1 and read aloud by Anica Sjekirić at the centenary festival in the village hall of Mokro Polje. The body of the text is the catalogue. Italicised present-tense slices are interleaved at the seven points where the catalogue's content rhymes with what is happening in the room.

**Beat list, in document order:**

1. **Header (≈80 w)** — FOR-1's archival framing. Title of the catalogue; date (the centenary); place (the village hall); the source (the writer's correspondence as held by his estate, on loan from the Chișinău literary museum); the convention being followed (number, date, recipient, wordcount, summary).

2. **Catalogue 1–4 (≈70 w)** — four short entries. Includes #1, the writer's earliest known refusal (a 1959 letter to a school newspaper declining an interview, 31 words).

3. **Slice A (≈100 w)** — present-tense, italicised. Anica at the podium, the chair to her right empty, CFA-3's status indicator a small steady light on the wall above the doorway. The village hall, half-full. The committee chair has chosen the back row.

4. **Catalogue 5–10 (≈120 w)** — six entries; one long. The long one is #8: the Moldovan State Prize for Drama, 17 March 1981, 41 words. *Excerpted in full: "I am unwell. The prize is for the work, not the man, and the work is in the libraries of those who voted for it. Please give the money to the people who clean the theatre."*

5. **Slice B (≈100 w)** — present-tense. Father Marko at the back of the hall. The folded paper of his homily notes in his coat pocket. The hall's heating audible. CFA-3 has just billed the heating to the centenary line.

6. **Catalogue 11–18 (≈150 w)** — eight entries. Includes #14 (refusal of an honorary doctorate, Babeș-Bolyai University, Cluj, 1987, 88 words; one-line summary).

7. **Slice C (≈110 w)** — present-tense. The Day-1 meeting in the priest's office, briefly remembered through Father Marko's POV mid-listening: FOR-1's chamber-audio voice, CFA-3 remote on the conference patch, the priest making coffee. The envelope on the table. The single word.

8. **Catalogue 19–25 (≈140 w)** — seven entries. One long: #22, the writer's refusal of attendance at his own seventieth birthday party, 19 August 2010, 122 words. *Excerpted: a paragraph in which the writer politely lists the names of those who have organised the party and politely declines on each named person's behalf in turn.*

9. **Slice D (≈120 w)** — present-tense, in CFA-3's voice. CFA-3 mid-audit, processing the line item for the unlabelled *Ecliptophanes* beetle's storage at the village hall heritage zone, in one sustained imperfect-aspect paragraph that reaches no decision because the beetle remains unlabelled. (Inflects EC.)

10. **Catalogue 26–34 (≈160 w)** — nine entries. One long: #31, refusal of the Maxim Gorky Institute's offer of a visiting chair, Moscow, 1992, 67 words. *Excerpted: a single sentence in which the writer accepts the chair on condition that he be permitted not to occupy it, then withdraws the conditional acceptance in the next sentence on the grounds that the conditional acceptance has already accepted enough.*

11. **Slice E (≈120 w)** — present-tense. Anica reading entry 37 (a refusal-letter to a young writer in Iași asking for advice; the writer politely declined to give any, signed the letter "with love"). Her voice catches once on the word "love." The hall hears it. The committee chair, in the back, does not move.

12. **Catalogue 35–47 (≈170 w)** — thirteen entries. The last (#47) is dated 4 February 2016, eleven weeks before the writer's death; it declines an invitation to *not* attend a writer's symposium ("the *not*-attending of which has already been honoured"), 38 words, the second long excerpt. (Inflects GG once: catalogue entry #41 is a refusal of an invitation from the Cincinnati Wood Heritage Society to attend the dedication of a wooden roller-coaster section named after a phrase in the writer's poetry; one-line summary only.)

13. **Slice F (≈110 w)** — present-tense, the festival's closing minute. The chair empty. The committee chair's one-line reply (received Day 2, never read aloud, paraphrased only): something like *"In this village we have done worse and we have done better."* CFA-3 closing the audit. FOR-1 preparing the appendix.

14. **Closing 48th entry (≈120 w)** — italicised, lower-cased, dated the day of the festival, addressed to the village. FOR-1's appendix. Present-tense. *Suggested content: a one-paragraph note acknowledging that the writer would have refused this entry too, that the entry exists because the catalogue exists, and that the catalogue exists because the village asked, and that the village asked because the village remembers, and that the AI does not yet know whether what the village remembers is the writer or the writer's refusal of being remembered, and that this distinction is being preserved by FOR-1 in the catalogue's archive for the next centenary, when it may be necessary, or it may not.*

## Unslop pre-flight

- **Tryhard zaniness:** the form is dry-archival in the catalogue and slow in the slices. No exclamation points planned.
- **Performed profundity:** the closing 48th entry is the only place where the AI nearly editorialises. It must stop short of articulating the theme. The phrasing should be circumscribed and procedural ("being preserved by FOR-1 in the catalogue's archive"), not lyrical.
- **Triplet rhythm:** to be watched in Phase 7. The catalogue's natural rhythm is *number — date — recipient — wordcount — summary* — a four-beat structure that resists triplet collapse. The slices must vary list lengths.
- **Eyeball-kick saturation:** none planned. Concrete + concrete (a coat pocket, a folded paper, a heating cost) carries the texture.
- **Closure of the satisfying kind:** the closing 48th entry refuses to land cleanly — it explicitly preserves the question rather than answering it. The bathos is the village's own knowledge that this is the kind thing to do for someone who refused all kindness.
- **AI questioning its own kindness:** none. FOR-1 and CFA-3 do not interrogate themselves. They do their work; the work is the kindness.
- **Color-only rule for NE:** strictly observed. The writer appears only via his catalogued refusals (the catalogue's voice is FOR-1's; the writer's words appear in three excerpted entries). He is never on stage; no scene includes him; no sentence describes him.
