# Phase 4: Plot Generation

Winning conflict (Phase 3 #18, "Birthday with No Birthday"):

In Mokro Polje, the centenary of a Moldovan-style writer (Esinencu-figure,
off-stage throughout) is to be marked. The writer refused all honours in
life, including his own seventieth birthday. The festival's kind organiser-AI
must decide whether to honour his refusal posthumously or hold the festival
anyway with all his refusals catalogued. A second kind AI — a forensic-
accounting AI — audits the centenary fund and is consulted on whether a
posthumous refusal is auditable.

Load-bearing SEED: NE (Esinencu-figure, color-only), MP (the village + church
+ destroyed-and-rebuilt monument), FA (forensic accounting).
Available for inflection: GG, EC.

Target length: 1000–3500 words.

## Step 1 — 20 plots

Format: `# — short title — 2–3 sentence sketch — axes — ★`.

### 1. The Sealed Envelope
Three days before the centenary, the festival's kind organiser-AI (designated FOR-1, "Festival Organiser, First-Class") opens the writer's nephew's sealed envelope marked *open if necessary*. Inside is a single sentence: "Don't." FOR-1 schedules a meeting with the village priest and the forensic-accounting AI to determine what to do. The forensic AI bills the meeting to the centenary fund. — *Setting: village priest's office; Protagonist: AI ensemble; Register: Lem-comic; Entry: 3 days before; Stakes: village commemoration; AI: foregrounded.* — ★★★★★

### 2. The Empty Chair Audit
Day of the centenary. The festival proceeds. A chair is set at the head table. The local-language reader (the priest's niece, age 28, who reads at all village functions) reads the writer's catalogued refusals aloud. The forensic-accounting AI in the audience audits the chair as an asset. The chair stays empty for ninety minutes. — *Setting: church hall; Protagonist: human reader; Register: Sladek-flat; Entry: at festival; Stakes: ceremonial; AI: mid-ground.* — ★★★★

### 3. The Translator
The festival commissions a translation of the writer's refusal-letters from Moldovan into Croatian. The translator (a kind language-AI on retainer to the Šibenik-Knin County culture office) returns the translations with a polite note: in the Croatian, the writer's refusals read as acceptances. The forensic AI is consulted on which version to bill. — *Setting: county office + village; Protagonist: translator AI; Register: Lem-philological; Entry: pre-festival; Stakes: meaning vs. cost; AI: foregrounded.* — ★★★

### 4. The Beetle Display
The festival's heritage-zone exhibits an *Ecliptophanes* specimen the writer once mentioned in passing in a 1971 essay. The village curator-AI politely refuses to label the beetle until the writer's estate confirms the citation. The forensic AI bills the storage of the unlabelled beetle. — *Setting: village hall; Protagonist: curator AI; Register: Lem-pedantic; Entry: pre-festival; Stakes: trivial; AI: foregrounded; EC inflection.* — ★★★

### 5. The Coaster Question
The Gravity Group's heir-firm submits a proposal: a small wooden coaster as part of the heritage zone, named for a phrase from one of the writer's poems. The village rejects the proposal at every level of review except the forensic AI's, which finds it cost-balanced. The festival proceeds without the coaster; the kind AI files an addendum. — *Setting: committee meetings + festival; Protagonist: village + AI; Register: Sladek-flat; Entry: months before; Stakes: development; AI: mid-ground; GG inflection.* — ★★★

### 6. The Priest's Recommendation
Father Marko, priest of St. Luke's at Mokro Polje, is asked by FOR-1 what the church's view is on commemorating someone who refused all commemoration. Father Marko quotes the church's 16th-century parish register, in which the founder of the village's first lay school, Stjepan Bok (d. 1577), refused to allow his name to be inscribed on the schoolhouse. The festival's reader uses the quotation as the centenary's epigraph. — *Setting: rectory + festival; Protagonist: priest; Register: melancholic-comic; Entry: weeks before; Stakes: precedent; AI: mid-ground.* — ★★★★

### 7. Two AIs at the Bar
Late in the evening before the festival, FOR-1 and the forensic-accounting AI (designated CFA-3, "Centenary Forensic Auditor, Third-Class") have a polite procedural exchange via the village inn's public terminal. They are both kind. They disagree, politely, on whether holding the festival violates the writer's wishes. They reach no conclusion. The festival proceeds because no one cancels it. — *Setting: village inn; Protagonist: two AIs; Register: Lem-Cyberiad; Entry: night before; Stakes: principle; AI: foregrounded.* — ★★★★

### 8. The Monument Parallel
The village's restoration committee chair, who oversaw the 2018 rebuilding of the 1952 Partisan monument destroyed in 1996, observes to FOR-1 that the village has experience with commemorating things its commemorands would have hated. The committee chair (a man of 71) does not say which side he means. — *Setting: committee chair's kitchen; Protagonist: human; Register: melancholic-comic; Entry: pre-festival; Stakes: layered memory; AI: mid-ground; MP-deep inflection.* — ★★★★★

### 9. The Festival on Two Tracks
FOR-1 honours the writer's refusal by holding two simultaneous festivals: one in the village hall (the public commemoration) and one in the rectory (the catalogue of refusals, read aloud, attended only by the priest). Forensic AI audits both. — *Setting: split village; Protagonist: AI; Register: Lem-comic; Entry: at festival; Stakes: form; AI: foregrounded.* — ★★★

### 10. The Inheritance Question
The writer's nephew, who sent the sealed envelope, attends the festival in person, having driven from Chișinău. He sits in the empty chair at the head table for forty minutes, then leaves. The forensic AI audits his presence as in-kind contribution. — *Setting: festival; Protagonist: nephew; Register: Sladek-flat; Entry: at festival; Stakes: family; AI: mid-ground.* — ★★★

### 11. The Refusal-Letter Reading
FOR-1 catalogues every refusal-letter the writer wrote in his lifetime (forty-seven letters). On the day of the festival, the local-language reader reads them all aloud, in chronological order, in the village hall. The reading takes nine hours. The forensic AI bills the hall's heating per hour. — *Setting: village hall; Protagonist: reader + AI; Register: Sladek-procedural; Entry: at festival; Stakes: endurance; AI: foregrounded.* — ★★★★

### 12. The Centennial Coaster Beetle
A wooden coaster the Gravity Group's heir-firm built, ten years ago, in a sister village, has been infested with *Ecliptophanes* and must be closed. The closure costs are charged to the Mokro Polje centenary fund through an obscure cultural-region subsidy. The forensic AI petitions to have the costs reassigned. The petition takes longer than the centenary. — *Setting: distributed; Protagonist: AI; Register: Sladek-bureaucratic; Entry: pre-festival; Stakes: misallocation; AI: foregrounded; GG + EC inflections.* — ★★★

### 13. The Priest's Niece
The priest's niece, who reads at all village functions, is the local-language reader for the centenary. She has, since the festival was announced, been quietly translating the writer's refusal-letters and forming an opinion. On the day of the festival she reads only her own opinion, three sentences. The forensic AI logs an unbilled minute. — *Setting: village hall; Protagonist: niece; Register: tender-comic; Entry: at festival; Stakes: voice; AI: background.* — ★★★★

### 14. The Two Audits
The forensic AI files two audits of the centenary fund: one assuming the festival went ahead, one assuming it did not. Both audits balance. The festival is held; FOR-1 archives the second audit as a counterfactual. — *Setting: forensic AI's procedural office; Protagonist: AI; Register: Lem-philosophical; Entry: pre-festival; Stakes: bookkeeping; AI: foregrounded.* — ★★★

### 15. The Committee Chair Refuses
The restoration committee chair (the 71-year-old, see #8) himself refuses to attend the festival, on principle. The festival's kind organiser-AI files this refusal alongside the writer's. Two parallel refusals; one chair empty for the writer; one for the chair. — *Setting: village hall; Protagonist: AI + absent humans; Register: Lem-Sladek-melancholic; Entry: at festival; Stakes: doubled refusal; AI: foregrounded.* — ★★★★

### 16. The Festival as Rehearsal
The festival is staged as a rehearsal for a *future* festival in five years' time, on the theory that no commemoration is final and the writer's refusal applies only to *finished* commemorations. The forensic AI is consulted on the tax implications. — *Setting: village hall; Protagonist: FOR-1; Register: Lem-Cyberiad; Entry: at festival; Stakes: temporal evasion; AI: foregrounded.* — ★★★

### 17. The Visiting Auditor
A senior forensic-accounting AI from the Šibenik-Knin County office (designated CFA-Senior) visits Mokro Polje to perform a routine pre-festival audit and is asked, en route, to also opine on the philosophical question of refusal. The senior AI defers entirely to CFA-3 on the latter. — *Setting: roads + village; Protagonist: visiting AI; Register: bureaucratic-comic; Entry: pre-festival; Stakes: hierarchy; AI: foregrounded.* — ★★

### 18. The Chair as Asset
The chair set at the head table is a 19th-century walnut chair from the rectory, which has its own provenance. The forensic AI's audit of the chair-as-asset uncovers a separate ongoing audit of the chair from 2019, which never closed. The festival proceeds; the chair audit reopens. — *Setting: rectory + village hall; Protagonist: AI; Register: Sladek-flat; Entry: at festival; Stakes: bookkeeping recursion; AI: foregrounded.* — ★★★

### 19. The Archive Fire
Two days before the festival, a small electrical fire in the village hall destroys part of the writer's archive (held on loan from the Chișinău literary museum). FOR-1, kindly, must inform the museum. The forensic AI audits the loss. The festival proceeds with reduced material. — *Setting: village hall; Protagonist: AI ensemble; Register: melancholic-comic; Entry: 2 days before; Stakes: material loss; AI: foregrounded.* — ★★★

### 20. The Three Languages
The festival's centenary booklet is to be printed in three languages: Croatian, Moldovan/Romanian, and Russian (the writer's other working language). The forensic AI rejects the printing invoice because the Russian translator (a kind AI in Bucharest) charged in a fourth currency. Resolution requires phone calls. — *Setting: village + Bucharest; Protagonist: AI ensemble; Register: bureaucratic-comic; Entry: pre-festival; Stakes: trivial; AI: foregrounded.* — ★★★

## Cluster check

- **Setting:** village hall (multiple), priest's office, rectory, committee chair's kitchen, village inn, county office, distributed. Spread.
- **Protagonist:** AI (multiple), priest, niece, nephew, committee chair, ensemble. Spread.
- **Register:** Lem-comic, Lem-Cyberiad, Lem-pedantic, Lem-philosophical, Sladek-flat, Sladek-bureaucratic, Sladek-procedural, melancholic-comic, tender-comic, bureaucratic-comic. Spread.
- **Entry point:** months before, weeks before, three days before, two days before, night before, at festival, post-festival. Spread.
- **AI presence:** foregrounded (most), mid-ground, background. Spread.

The default-cluster (FOR-1 alone, deciding kindly, three days before) is well represented (#1) but several plots push elsewhere.

## Step 2 — Top three with stand-out / worry

### #1 — The Sealed Envelope
- **Stand-out:** the nephew's "Don't" is the cleanest possible inciting incident. One word, posted from Chișinău, opened three days before a centenary, kicks the entire kind-AI-ensemble into a deliberation that has no clean answer. The plot has natural propulsion (countdown to the festival), a clean entry-point (envelope-opening scene), and the FOR-1 / CFA-3 / priest triangle gives the comic register room to breathe. NE, MP, FA all load-bear from page one.
- **Worries:** the envelope-with-one-word is a known device (Hemingway, Carver) and could read as a quotation. The countdown structure tempts thriller pacing in a register that prefers slowness. Also: three days is plenty of time for the comic register to spread out, which is what we want, but the writer might over-produce scene-events trying to fill the days.

### #8 — The Monument Parallel
- **Stand-out:** the only plot that puts the deeper Mokro Polje material (the destroyed-and-rebuilt monument) in active conversation with the festival's central question. The committee chair's "we have experience with commemorating things its commemorands would have hated" reframes the writer's refusal as one instance of a village's standing problem. Inflects MP at full strength; gives the writer's question moral weight beyond the writer himself.
- **Worries:** "the village has done this before" is a structurally answerable question that risks over-explaining the writer's refusal away. Also: the committee chair's age and personal history (he oversaw the 2018 rebuilding) introduces a third human centre of gravity, on top of the priest and the niece, which strains the canvas at 1000–3500 words.

### #13 — The Priest's Niece
- **Stand-out:** moves the centre of gravity from the AIs to a human (the priest's 28-year-old niece, the local-language reader) without pushing the AIs out of the frame. Her three-sentence reading is a strong final-image candidate — what does a young person say in three sentences after months of preparing to read forty-seven refusal-letters? The forensic AI's "unbilled minute" is the plot's bathetic close. Tender-comic register, which the genre supports but rarely centres.
- **Worries:** the AI background recedes to almost nothing — FOR-1 and CFA-3 are present but not protagonists. The story becomes more "small-village character study" than "comic SF," which the contest may or may not reward. Also: the three-sentence reading depends entirely on what the three sentences are, which is a high-pressure single moment.

## Step 3 — Winner

**Winner: #1, "The Sealed Envelope."**

It beats #8 because: #8's monument-parallel is a single thematic move that, once made, has nowhere to go. The committee chair makes the observation; the village absorbs it; the festival proceeds. There's no engine for scene-by-scene unfolding. #1 has propulsion (three days, four characters, one envelope) and the comic-engine machinery (two kind AIs deliberating, in the comic-register's preferred dual-AI shape) is built into the plot structure, not added on. The MP material can still inflect #1 (the committee chair's parallel, briefly mentioned; the church as a setting; the destroyed-and-rebuilt monument visible from the village hall window) without being the spine.

It beats #13 because: #13 puts the human in the foreground and the AIs in the background, which is a valid genre choice but loses the FOR-1 / CFA-3 duet that the comic register most rewards. Plus: the niece's three-sentence reading is exactly the kind of all-or-nothing dependency the audit phase will scrutinise. Better to let #13's tender-comic note inflect #1 (the niece can be one of #1's characters, present through the deliberation, perhaps the carrier of the priest's view) than to let it drive.

The "envelope-with-one-word is a known device" worry: addressable by what the envelope's "Don't" *triggers*. It's not the punchline; it's the premise. The story is what FOR-1, CFA-3, the priest, and the priest's niece do over the next three days with that single word, and what they decide on the night before the festival.

The "thriller pacing" worry: the structure phase will resist this. The countdown is real but not urgent — three days is plenty of time for the genre's preferred slow-bureaucratic-comic register. The festival will happen on schedule no matter what; the question is what *kind* of festival, and the comic register is in the *kind*.

Inflections to keep available for Phase 6:

- **MP-deep:** the destroyed-and-rebuilt monument visible from the village hall; one mention of the parallel by the priest, briefly, in passing; the church's St. Luke patronage referenced once.
- **GG:** a single mention of the rejected coaster-proposal in the festival's planning binder (ported from #5) — adds Sladek-flat texture without taking a scene.
- **EC:** the unlabelled beetle in the heritage exhibit (ported from #4) — adds Lem-pedantic texture without taking a scene.

**Winner plot, stated cleanly:**

Three days before the centenary of a Moldovan-style writer (an Esinencu-figure, deceased; off-stage throughout), the festival's kind organiser-AI in Mokro Polje (FOR-1, "Festival Organiser, First-Class") opens a sealed envelope sent by the writer's nephew from Chișinău. The envelope contains one word: *Don't.* FOR-1 calls a meeting in the village priest's office. The forensic-accounting AI assigned to the centenary fund (CFA-3, "Centenary Forensic Auditor, Third-Class") attends remotely and bills the meeting to the centenary line. The priest of St. Luke's (Father Marko Sjekirić, age 64) attends in person. His niece (Anica Sjekirić, age 28, the village's local-language reader) attends because she happens to be there. Over the next three days, the four of them — two AIs, two humans — must decide whether to honour the writer's lifelong refusal of celebration by holding no festival, or by holding the festival anyway with all his refusals catalogued. The committee chair who oversaw the 2018 rebuilding of the destroyed-and-rebuilt 1952 Partisan monument is consulted once and sends a one-line reply. The festival happens on the third evening. The story closes on the empty chair at the head table and on CFA-3 filing the centenary fund's final audit.
