# "The First Sclerite" — Outline Candidates

**Structural constants** across all 10: Five sections, strict chronology, close third person. Scenes 1–4 present tense. Scene 5 simple past, no announcement. Wiwaxia never personified directly. Marina alone in most scenes; interiority channeled through the work, not announced.

**Word allotments** sum to approximately 1,400 words per outline — comfortably within the 1,000–2,000 range and gives each scene room to breathe without demanding fragmentation.

## Candidate 1: The Notebook as Through-Line ★★★★

**Scene 1 (≈175w) — Feb 1983.**
Marina at the terminal, afternoon audit. Routing trace: A-B-D instead of A-B-C-D; efficiency gain consistent across multiple instances, wrong shape for hardware variation. Official log: "category uncertain." She copies the trace into her working fieldbook — a green composition journal she's kept for three years for anomalies that don't fit standard trees. The notebook starts here not as a decision but as a habit. Ends: official log filed; notebook left open on the desk.

**Scene 2 (≈280w) — Sep 1983.**
Eleven pages in the notebook, five dead hypotheses, each with its specific failure mode. Marina moves from hypothesis to description: the routing behavior has a signature, clustering by user tenure and time band. She's been reading a Cambrian paleontology book on lunches. She writes in the notebook margin about having the scleritome without the animal. Then, on the next page, by itself: *Wiwaxia.* A working placeholder. She puts the book in the desk drawer. Both feel done. Ends: name written; book stowed.

**Scene 3 (≈260w) — Mar 1984.**
Marina charts affected exchange zones on graph paper. A statistical boundary becomes visible. She finds three research abstracts from other analysts with overlapping anomalies. She reads all three; contacts none. Could explain why in several ways — all true, none quite the reason. Notes the boundary in the notebook as a probable parameter, framed as testable. Ends: one abstract paper-clipped into the notebook's back; the others left on her desk.

**Scene 4 (≈300w) — Nov 1984.**
The federal honeypot appears in the exchange analysis. Marina identifies it immediately. She watches for four days: her own traffic routes around it; established users route around it; newer traffic doesn't. She works backwards to find routing-avoidance onset: consistent evidence across three log sources. Opens the notebook to September 1983. Ends: notebook open to the name; the date in the log data sitting beside it.

**Scene 5 (≈385w) — Nov 1984, 2 hrs later — PAST TENSE.**
Marina at her desk with the routing logs. What she can claim to know: routes taken and not taken, a consistent pattern, a specific date range. The notebook on her desk to the left of the terminal, where it had been for two years. She types the official report: routing irregularity, pattern of traffic avoidance, category uncertain. Files it. Picks up the notebook. Not finished with it — something else. Puts it in her briefcase for the first time. Ends: the notebook traveling with her now, which is a small statement she is making to herself about what kind of document it turned out to be.

## Candidate 2: The Five Dead Hypotheses ★★★½

**Scene 1 (≈175w) — Feb 1983.**
The trace anomaly. Marina already generating hypotheses before she's done with the audit — four by the time she stands up for water. She writes the trace and the four early candidates in the notebook. Official log: category uncertain. Ends: the hypotheses in the notebook, not yet failed.

**Scene 2 (≈310w) — Sep 1983.**
The fifth hypothesis fails. Marina lists all five and their specific failure modes in the notebook — each one interesting in how it failed, each failure narrowing the description. She shifts to describing the behavior rather than explaining it. The Cambrian book, open to the Wiwaxia entry. She reads it several times; writes the margin note; turns to a clean page. Writes the name. Below it: *sixth entry, not a hypothesis.* Ends: five dead; one thing that isn't an explanation.

**Scene 3 (≈260w) — Mar 1984.**
Marina graphs the exchange zones. The statistical boundary emerges. She reads the three research abstracts. She considers calling the Peoria researcher — knows what she'd have to say in the first five minutes to be taken seriously. Decides against. Makes a note in the notebook: "geographic boundary, probable, see also: Peoria abstract (not contacted)." Ends: the citation without the contact.

**Scene 4 (≈280w) — Nov 1984.**
The honeypot. Marina works through it like it's a diagnostic: identify, characterize, test. Four days of traffic observation. The routing avoidance pattern is consistent and selective. She starts to document hypothesis six for the official record — then stops. Hypothesis six is not something she can write in official language. Opens the notebook. Ends: hypothesis six unwritten in the log; the notebook open to September 1983.

**Scene 5 (≈375w) — Nov 1984, 2 hrs later — PAST TENSE.**
She looked at hypothesis six from every angle she had. It was technically accurate. It also required a subject in the sentence, and official log entries did not require subjects. She typed the report without one: routing irregularity, consistent traffic avoidance, category uncertain. Filed it. Looked at the notebook's eleven pages, five dead hypotheses, a fossil name, and a margin note. Counted. She had been keeping two documents for two years and the gap between them was the shape of something she didn't have the right diagnostic tree for. She put the notebook in her briefcase. The investigation wasn't over; it had just reached the edge of what she could call investigating. Ends: the briefcase closing; the logs still running.

## Candidate 4: Heavy-Weighted Toward Scenes 4–5 ★★★★

**Scene 1 (≈140w) — Feb 1983.**
Compressed. The trace, the "category uncertain," the working notebook. Marina methodical: edge-case journals are what methodical people keep. Ends: an entry, not yet significant.

**Scene 2 (≈200w) — Sep 1983.**
Still compressed but functionally complete. One long evening, five dead hypotheses, the turn from explanation to description, the Cambrian book. The name. Ends: *Wiwaxia.* Marina notes, with professional attention, that writing the word felt different than writing the five hypotheses. She doesn't extend this observation.

**Scene 3 (≈200w) — Mar 1984.**
The perimeter. Geographic extent. Other researchers, not contacted. One notebook entry. Ends: the boundary in pencil, among people who've also seen it and aren't calling.

**Scene 4 (≈440w) — Nov 1984.**
The honeypot gets the scene's real estate. Marina identifies it by its technical signature (depth of content, response-time consistency, logging characteristics she recognizes from the protocol specs she read two years ago preparing for something she never encountered). She watches it for four days and describes, in specific technical terms, the traffic profiles that route through it versus around it. On day four she works backwards: when did routing around the honeypot's address range begin? She pulls four log sources. The onset window: September 3–16, 1983. She looks at this date across all four sources. The window won't narrow past two weeks. She opens the notebook. The name: September 9. She does not close the notebook for several minutes. She does not write anything. Ends: the date and name, two weeks apart; Marina sitting with both.

**Scene 5 (≈420w) — Nov 1984, 2 hrs later — PAST TENSE.**
She ran the log check again. The window held. She looked at what the data said and what she would need to believe to say more than the data said, and whether she was willing to believe it, and decided the question was not yet answerable from the evidence she had. She typed the report: routing irregularity, traffic avoidance pattern, behavior onset predates deployment, category uncertain. Read it back. True and appropriate. Filed it. Then she sat for longer than she usually sat after finishing a report. Outside: November. The building mostly empty. She thought about Wiwaxia-the-fossil — nearly a century of study from fragments alone, body plan still contested. You could describe it with specificity. Whether you could say what it was was a different question. She put the notebook in her briefcase. She turned off her terminal. The exchange equipment ran on in the adjacent room, indicator lights steady, making its routing choices in the dark. Ends: the lights, steady in the dark, after Marina has left.

## Candidate 8: The Two-Document Structure ★★★½

*Structural conceit: each scene organized around what goes into the official log versus into the notebook; the gap between documents is the story's spine.*

**Scene 1 (≈165w) — Feb 1983.**
Official log: "category uncertain." Notebook: the trace, three ruled-out quick hypotheses, *not hardware, not scheduling.* The gap between the two documents is established at the first entry. Ends: official log filed; notebook on the desk; both open.

**Scene 2 (≈290w) — Sep 1983.**
Official log: nothing new. Notebook: eleven pages, five dead hypotheses, the scleritome margin note, the name. The gap has been growing for seven months. Marina notices she has never put any of the notebook into the log — not inertia; the notebook content has never crossed the threshold of something she could submit. She's been waiting for the shape to become reportable. It hasn't, and now it's named Wiwaxia, which is definitely not reportable. Ends: the log unchanged; the notebook with a name.

**Scene 3 (≈255w) — Mar 1984.**
Marina drafts a memo for the official record: *Re: Routing Anomaly, Extended Analysis, March 1984.* She reads the draft. She does not submit it. The geographic boundary data goes into the notebook instead, as a parameter. Eight months later she will understand the draft was the right instinct done too cautiously; for now she just knows it isn't ready. Ends: the draft not submitted; the parameter in the notebook.

**Scene 4 (≈295w) — Nov 1984.**
Official log: she will write "routing irregularity, possible equipment variance, category uncertain." Notebook: what she's actually observing. The honeypot, the four days of traffic analysis, the selective routing avoidance, the date-correlation from the backtrack. The gap between the two documents is at its maximum width. Ends: the maximum gap; the notebook open; the log empty.

**Scene 5 (≈395w) — Nov 1984, 2 hrs later — PAST TENSE.**
She typed the official report and it was good and true and said nothing it shouldn't say. She looked at the notebook. Twenty-two months of private record, and the gap between it and the official log had never closed because she had never been certain enough to close it. Now she was certain of a different set of things than she'd expected to be certain of, and the gap still felt unclosable, and she was starting to understand that the gap was not a failure state. It was the shape of something that didn't have official language. She put the notebook in her briefcase. Filed the report. The routing logs ran. Ends: the report saying what it says; the notebook traveling; the gap maintained.

*[Note: The two-document structure is elegant but can become mechanical — watch for this in execution.]*

## Candidate 9: Interiority Arc — High / High / Low / None / Retrospective ★★★★

*Structural move: Scenes 1-2 have visible interiority; scenes 3-4 progressively withhold it; scene 5 returns interiority changed in quality — retrospective, not live.*

**Scene 1 (≈220w) — Feb 1983.**
High interiority. Marina notices the trace and catalogues her immediate reasoning: the efficiency gain is the wrong shape for hardware, the path variation is too consistent to be noise. She copies it into the notebook because she was trained to distrust gap-filling, and this is a gap. She thinks: this will probably resolve to hardware in a week. She is methodical and doesn't attach to hypotheses; she writes the trace down accordingly. Ends: the notebook an extension of her methodical practice; no particular weight yet.

**Scene 2 (≈340w) — Sep 1983.**
High interiority. Marina works through the five dead hypotheses and examines her own reasoning at each failure. The failures are interesting — they narrow description. She reads the Cambrian book entry. She examines the connection she's making: she's not anthropomorphizing, she's giving a placeholder to an empirical object with a consistent signature. Scientific names are often wrong; they're placeholders until the body plan is recovered. She writes the name. She examines, faintly, why she is not going to show this to anyone. She doesn't arrive at an answer. She puts the pen down. Ends: the name; the unmade decision; the pen capped.

**Scene 3 (≈240w) — Mar 1984.**
Interiority drops. Marina charts the zones, reads the abstracts, decides not to contact the researchers. These decisions are not examined. The notebook gets its entry. Ends: the entry, spare.

**Scene 4 (≈240w) — Nov 1984.**
No interiority. Pure observation. The honeypot appears; Marina identifies it; she watches for four days; the routing avoidance is specific and consistent; she opens the notebook to September 1983. The date-correlation, if she's run the backtrack, is present in the data. The scene renders the data; Marina's response is not rendered. Ends: the notebook open; the name on the page; nothing else.

**Scene 5 (≈360w) — Nov 1984, 2 hrs later — PAST TENSE.**
Interiority returns, but changed: retrospective rather than live. Marina reconstructed the timeline. She had written the name as a working placeholder, because she was methodical and that was what you did. She had not known it would feel different than writing the five dead hypotheses. It had felt different, and she had noted the feeling without pursuing it, which she had thought was the right procedure. Now she was looking at traffic logs from September 1983. The question of what to do with an interiority noted and set aside was still open. She typed the report. She put the notebook in her briefcase. She drove home. The routing logs ran. Ends: Marina gone; the logs running without her; the retrospective closed and the thing itself continuing.

# Top Three Analysis

## Candidate 7: Maximum Technical Precision ★★★★½

**What makes it stand out:** The scene 4 discovery — that the routing-avoidance behavior around the honeypot's address range began within a two-week window of September 9, 1983, confirmed across four independent log sources — is the story's most uncanny moment rendered in purely technical terms. No mysticism, no interpretation required: the reader receives a date range and a date and must decide what they mean. This is exact epistemological withholding. Marina doesn't interpret it; she just closes the notebook. The discovery also works retroactively on scene 2, reframing the naming as potentially consequential without the story ever saying so. The technical specificity throughout (efficiency gain in scene 1, the five labeled failure modes in scene 2, the traffic-profile taxonomy in scene 4) sustains Marina as a particular kind of thinker without performing it.

**What worries me:** The scene 5 ending, "making its routing choices in the dark," is controlled personification that the story has earned, but "choices" could tip into performed uncanniness if the preceding prose hasn't earned the weight. Also, the five failed hypotheses in scene 2 (≈295 words) are doing a lot of work — they need to feel like live methodology, not a checklist. A writer executing from this outline needs to make each failure specifically interesting, not just enumerated.

---

## Candidate 4: Heavy-Weighted Toward Scenes 4–5 ★★★★

**What makes it stand out:** Allocating ≈440 words to scene 4 allows the honeypot analysis to be technically rendered rather than summarized — the depth of content that makes it look right, the protocol specs, the traffic-profile taxonomy, the four-source backtrack. This is where the story earns its payoff, and it benefits from room. The indicator lights in scene 5 ("steady in the dark") are the best sensory anchor of any of the ten outlines. The compression of scenes 1–3 is honest about the story's real weight distribution — most of these plots front-load setup and under-deliver at the center of gravity.

**What worries me:** Scene 2 at ≈200 words may be too compressed for the naming to land. The naming is the story's structural pivot — the moment the investigation changes from recording to becoming. At 200 words it risks feeling rushed, especially since scene 5 returns to it twice. The imbalance (200 words for the pivot, 440 for the consequence) might leave the reader without enough grounding at the moment they most need it.

---

## Candidate 9: Interiority Arc ★★★★

**What makes it stand out:** The interiority distribution — high, high, low, none, retrospective — creates a structural arc that mirrors the shift from being an observer to being observed without the story ever stating it. Scenes 1 and 2 show Marina examining her own reasoning; scenes 3 and 4 quietly stop; scene 5 returns interiority changed in kind, no longer live examination but retrospective reconstruction. This is the most formally interesting outline, and the shift in interiority quality performs the same function as the tense shift — it marks a change without naming it.

**What worries me:** Scene 2 at ≈340 words is the largest single scene in any outline. The naming scene deserves weight, but ≈340 words of interiority in scene 2 risks making Marina too legible to herself — too fluent about what she's doing and why. Real methodical thinkers examine their procedure more than their feeling; this outline's high-interiority scene 2 needs to stay on the procedural side or it tips into the kind of preemptive self-analysis that makes characters too tidy.

# The Single Best

**Candidate 7** is the winner.

The other top candidates have one strong structural element each (Candidate 4's weight distribution, Candidate 9's interiority arc), but Candidate 7 has the story's most irreducible moment: the date-range correlation. It is a piece of data that cannot be disposed of. The reader can resist every soft implication the story makes about what's happening, but they will have a September 1983 date range and a September 9 entry, coming from four independent sources, and the story simply will not tell them what to do with that. This is the epistemological structure at its most precise and most honest.

The ending ("indicator lights steady, making its routing choices in the dark") is also the correct closing image for a story that lives in technical ambiguity: the equipment identified as active and specific and present, doing what it does, without the narrative adjudicating whether "choices" is metaphor or description.

Minor refinement from the analysis: incorporate from Candidate 1 the specific beat that putting the notebook in the briefcase is a small statement Marina is making to herself about what kind of document it turned out to be — without articulating what kind that is. This replaces the slightly weaker "not finished with it — not that" framing in the existing Candidate 7 scene 5.

Eleven pages in the working notebook; seven months of observation; five written hypotheses and their specific failure modes: hardware cascade (prediction failed), billing protocol interference (failed), AT&T field routing test (no evidence, wrong signature), node firmware variation (tested, doesn't generate this shape), clerical misconfiguration (consistency rules it out). The Cambrian paleontology book is propped open on her desk — her father's; she took it from a box after he died two springs ago. She reads the Wiwaxia entry again: *body plan undetermined; gross anatomy contested; known primarily from isolated sclerites.* She reads it three times. She writes in the notebook margin: *this is what it's like to have the scleritome without the animal.* Then, on the next page, by itself: *Wiwaxia.* A working placeholder, like a fossil name. Scientists name things before they can describe them.

She caps the pen. Puts the book in the desk drawer.

**Scene 3 (≈255 words) — March 1984. The perimeter.**

Marina has been charting affected exchange zones on graph paper — better for topology than a county map. A statistical boundary becomes visible: routing behavior with the Wiwaxia signature clusters within a specific radius, and past a certain distance from her home exchange the behavior changes character.

She has found three published abstracts from analysts at other utilities describing similar anomalies in overlapping exchange territories. She reads all three carefully.

She does not contact any of the researchers. She could explain this to herself several ways: starting cold with someone who'd never seen the diagnostic history would require reconstructing twenty months of work; the correlation with overlapping territories might be coincidental equipment variance; she doesn't have a finding yet, just a parameter. These are all true. She makes a note in the working notebook: *geographic extent, probable statistical boundary, parameter not yet confirmed.* Framed as testable. One abstract paper-clipped into the notebook's back cover.

*Ends: the boundary noted; the three researchers not called; the abstract traveling with the notebook.*

**Scene 4 (≈340 words) — November 1984. The honeypot.**

Morning. A federal honeypot board shows up in the exchange analysis — heavily stocked, well-configured, exactly positioned to attract traffic. Marina identifies it within the first hour. She has read the protocol specifications; she has not seen one before. She begins systematic observation.

Four days. Traffic profiles that route through it: unfamiliar nodes, new accounts, short-duration connections from outside the exchange's regular user base. Traffic that routes around it: established users with long-tenure accounts and known path patterns — taking worse routes, consistently, specifically. Marina's own traffic routes around it. She checks this four times across different query types.

On the fourth day she works backwards in the traffic logs: when did routing-avoidance behavior around this address range begin? She pulls data from four independent log sources and cross-references them.

The behavior-onset window — estimated from consistent routing-avoidance patterns around that address range, before the honeypot was deployed there — falls in a window of September 3–16, 1983.

Marina looks at this date across the four data sources. The window doesn't narrow past two weeks.

She opens the working notebook to the page with the name.

September 9, 1983.

Below the name, her own handwriting: *working placeholder, like a fossil name.*

She closes the notebook. She does not add anything.

*Ends: the date range and the name; two weeks apart across four sources; the notebook closed.*

**Scene 5 (≈335 words) — November 1984, two hours later. — PAST TENSE —**

She ran the cross-check a fifth time. The window held.

She thought about what the data said and what it did not say. What it said: consistent routing avoidance around an address range, onset within two weeks of a name written in pencil in a private working notebook. What it did not say: mechanism. What she would need to believe to extend the data past what it said: more than she had, and different in kind.

She pulled up the official anomaly log and typed: *routing irregularity confirmed in November exchange analysis. Consistent traffic-avoidance pattern around monitoring infrastructure, probable federal deployment. Behavior onset predates current deployment. Category uncertain.*

She read it back. True, and complete in the way that left out the only thing she was actually thinking about — which was appropriate, because she didn't know what to call that thing, and writing it down wouldn't have helped.

She picked up the working notebook. For two years it had sat on the desk to the left of the terminal, available. She wasn't finished with it. She was doing something else: she was putting it in her briefcase, which was a small statement she was making to herself about what kind of document it had turned out to be. She didn't need to know the category to know the placement had changed.

She filed the report. She shut off the terminal. The exchange equipment ran on in the adjacent room, indicator lights steady, making its routing choices in the dark.

She went home.

*Ends: the lights steady in the dark; Marina gone; the equipment continuing.*
