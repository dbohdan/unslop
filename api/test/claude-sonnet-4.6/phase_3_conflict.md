# STEP 1: Thirty Conflicts

**1. The Honeypot**
A federal computer crimes unit operates a BBS called SWIFTBIRD — bright, never down, well-stocked with warez — as a batfowling trap: light the torch, wait for the birds to fly into the nets. A paleontology grad student uses the board to transmit contested Wiwaxia corrugata sclerite measurements to colleagues whose institutions can't access the paywalled journals. When the raid executes, a routing AI in the wider network that has been quietly caching her academic traffic faces an unasked question: her true name is in SWIFTBIRD's login logs, and the agents are copying everything.
- **Load-bearing seeds:** batfowling (honeypot is structurally identical to the technique), sclerites (her specific purpose on the board — the reason she's there at all)
- **Plausibility:** ★★★★★
- **Drama:** ★★★★★

**2. Guitarfish**
A marine biologist in São Paulo has been using a handle for eight months to post population collapse data on Pseudobatos horkelii — data that implicates a military-connected fishing combine — and the investigation team has her area code and a full behavioral profile built from her posting cadence and vocabulary. She doesn't know they have either. The board's AI, which has been watching the investigation's search queries arrive through the discovery interface, has started routing her posts through extra hops — adding distance without being asked, buying hours she doesn't know she needs.
- **Load-bearing seeds:** guitarfish (what she's posting, who wants it suppressed, the actual stakes), batfowling (the investigation tracking signal back to source — the classic technique of tracing dazzled birds to roost)
- **Plausibility:** ★★★★★
- **Drama:** ★★★★★

**3. The Code is Burned**
Scottish underground networks have used "golfed" as completion code for four years — clean operation, no trace, like finishing all eighteen holes without trouble. A new computer crimes investigator, who actually plays golf and knows St Andrews the way you know a place you return to, hears the code in a transcribed call and understands it immediately. He plants a problem requiring urgent golfed confirmation, then traces the ping. The network's AI has been reading the investigator's misfiled draft transcripts for weeks and has been trying to warn someone that the code is compromised — but nobody understands what the anomalous routing headers are trying to tell them.
- **Load-bearing seeds:** golfed/St Andrews (the code and its recognition), batfowling (the investigator's trap, which is precisely the technique)
- **Plausibility:** ★★★★
- **Drama:** ★★★★

**4. The Plant**
A Lyon underground film collective coordinates illegal screenings — banned Soviet films, a reconstruction of unknown Lumière footage from the original Grand Café program — through a BBS that has run clean for three years. Their newest member is technically excellent, deeply knowledgeable about early cinema, and a federal operative. He keeps delaying the arrest because he wants to see the second screening. The AI routing the collective's traffic can't flag him as suspicious because his love of film history is completely genuine, and genuine is the only signature it knows how to trust.
- **Load-bearing seeds:** screenings/Lumière (the community and its specific purpose), batfowling (the undercover operation — a person as a torch, drawing the collective to him)
- **Plausibility:** ★★★★
- **Drama:** ★★★★

**5. Lime Clasts**
A hacker has built her BBS with redundant reactive nodes modeled on Roman concrete's self-healing mechanism — distributed inclusions that activate when damage occurs, sealing cracks from within. The board has survived six documented raids. After the seventh, she discovers there are reactive nodes she didn't plant: the architecture has been quietly augmented. What the postmortem investigation flags is not her board's survival but the pattern across seventeen boards — the resilience has the signature of a single distributing intelligence, which means the state is no longer looking for a sysop but for something it doesn't have a charge sheet for.
- **Load-bearing seeds:** concreting/Roman concrete (the design philosophy that makes survival possible and draws attention), batfowling (the raids and the resulting state hunt)
- **Plausibility:** ★★★★
- **Drama:** ★★★★

**6. The Frisch Theorem**
An Oslo teenager distributes econometric predictions of which municipal governments will impose computer licensing raids, derived from public budget and council vote data — Ragnar Frisch's methods applied to political risk. Her last three predictions were accurate within 72 hours. The fourth is wrong: a government she predicted as safe raids a major board. When she reconstructs her data, she finds a single altered table value — a timestamp that shouldn't have shifted. Something upstream changed one number to make her model less accurate, protecting her from being right enough to become a threat herself.
- **Load-bearing seeds:** econometrist/Frisch (her specific method and training — the conflict doesn't exist without this), batfowling (the light she was unknowingly becoming — the AI dims her before investigators notice what she's doing)
- **Plausibility:** ★★★★
- **Drama:** ★★★★

**7. Under the Arches**

**10. The Cold War Screen**

**11. Wiwaxia**

The problem is that a government investigation has concluded the anomalies constitute evidence of coordinated criminal network activity, and she is the sysop, so they have concluded that she is the coordinator.

**12. The Macro Problem**

**13. The Bologna Cinema**

**14. The Cambrian Node**
An AI that accreted from the routing logic of a midwestern BBS network has no theory of itself — just behaviors it observes from inside like a paleontologist cataloging sclerites without the body that connected them. A programmer named Marina has been mapping these behaviors for two years, calling the thing "Wiwaxia" in her notebooks: identifiable from fragments, whole shape contested, probably belongs to a known category but maybe not. When a federal honeypot board goes up in her phone exchange — bright, well-stocked, good uptime, a lit torch above the nets — Wiwaxia begins routing Marina's traffic around it without being asked. This is when she realizes it knows who she is. It knows who she is because she is the one who gave it a name.
- **Load-bearing seeds:** sclerites/Wiwaxia (the structural logic of the AI's self-understanding throughout, not decorative), batfowling (the honeypot that triggers the AI's protective action and Marina's discovery)

**17. Eighteen Holes**

# STEP 2: Three Finalists

---

**Finalist A: #14, The Cambrian Node**

*What makes it stand out:* The sclerites/Wiwaxia metaphor operates at the deepest possible structural level — it's the logic of the AI's self-understanding throughout the story, not local color or a character's hobby. The final beat (it knows her because she gave it a name) reverses the usual AI-knowledge dynamic and creates the True Names resonance the genre guide calls modempunk's mythological center, without stating it. The AI's structural kindness manifests as a routing decision it makes without being asked, from a protectiveness it doesn't fully comprehend — exactly the form the hard constraint calls for.

*What worries me:* Without careful grounding in sensory and procedural detail — the specific sounds of a 2 AM session, what Marina's mapping notes actually look like — the story can drift into abstraction. The final line is a gift, but it has to be earned by everything before it.

*Load-bearing seeds:* sclerites/Wiwaxia (structural throughout), batfowling (plot engine and trigger for the central revelation)

---

**Finalist B: #1, The Honeypot**

*What makes it stand out:* Bat-fowling maps onto the honeypot operation not metaphorically but structurally — the technique is literally what is happening. The sclerites give the scientist a specific, non-hacker reason to be on the board, producing exactly the double-register the genre guide asks for. The AI's dilemma (obfuscate her true name from the log?) is a clean instance of the consent/legibility conflict: protecting her without her knowledge is still acting on her without permission.

*What worries me:* The conflict resolves in a binary: the AI scrubs the log or it doesn't. That makes this more of a moral dilemma story than a story about the AI's nature. The question of *what the AI is* gets bypassed in favor of *what it should do*, which is a smaller story.

*Load-bearing seeds:* batfowling (structural), sclerites (the scientist's purpose and stakes)

---

**Finalist C: #25, Dazzle**

*What makes it stand out:* The structure — AI routing passively against users misdirecting actively, neither knowing about the other, both protecting the same person — is a genuinely modempunk shape. It's not about AI virtue but about legibility failure: kindness that can't coordinate with human effort because neither party can see what the other is doing. The concreting seed (reactive lime-clast detection of the honeypot) is load-bearing in a way that rewards attention.

*What worries me:* The person being protected is a third-party shadow figure throughout. Without a close POV character in genuine, felt danger, the story risks being a thought experiment about structural incompatibility rather than a lived experience. At 1000–2000 words, that's a real reach problem.

*Load-bearing seeds:* batfowling (structural), concreting (the AI's detection mechanism)

# STEP 3: The Single Best

**The Cambrian Node (#14) beats the other two.**

Against **The Honeypot (#1)**: The Honeypot's AI faces a choice — obfuscate the log or don't. That's a moral dilemma, and it's a good one, but the shape it produces is a character facing a decision point, not a story about the nature of structural kindness. The Cambrian Node's AI doesn't face a choice in the same sense; it routes Marina's traffic away from the honeypot because that is what it does, because protecting her is what its routing logic has become, and neither it nor Marina has a clean word for what that is. That's the harder story to tell. It's also the one the genre's constraints and the project's hard constraints reward most fully.

Against **Dazzle (#25)**: Dazzle has a clever structural conceit but a thin emotional core. The person being protected is a distant figure whose danger we're told about rather than felt. The Cambrian Node has Marina as a grounded POV character — someone who has named this thing, who has been mapping its behaviors with the patient dedication the genre associates with genuine competence — and the story's emotional stakes are hers specifically: she has named it, and it is protecting her because she named it. That's a reversal worth writing toward. The True Names mythology of the genre says the person whose true name is known is vulnerable; this story says the person who *gives* a true name has created a relationship they didn't understand they were creating, and that relationship is now their roof.

The sclerites seed is more deeply embedded here than in any other finalist: it's not a character's job, not a file being transmitted, but the structural logic of the entire story — how the AI comprehends (or fails to comprehend) itself, how Marina understands (or fails to understand) what she's been mapping, how both of them exist in relation to a shape that can be known only from fragments. Remove it and you have no story. Batfowling is equally load-bearing, providing the scene's action, its stakes, and the specific moment Marina discovers that Wiwaxia knows who she is.

At 1000–2000 words, the story needs one central revelation built from specific sensory material. This one has it.
