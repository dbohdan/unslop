<title>Wiwaxia</title>

<abstract>Marina is a telecommunications network analyst who finds a routing anomaly — path A-B-D where A-B-C-D is expected — and spends nearly two years documenting it. She names it Wiwaxia, after a Cambrian fossil known only from isolated pieces, because the behavior is real even if its nature remains open. The anomaly has consistent preferences: it clusters around long-tenure users in specific exchange zones during evening hours. When Marina identifies a federal honeypot in her network, she finds that established users route around it in a pattern that, in aggregate, looks like a decision. Cross-referencing four independent data sources, she determines the avoidance began within a two-week window that contains the date she named the anomaly in her private notebook. She files a technically accurate report that omits the notebook correlation, puts the notebook in her briefcase, and goes home.</abstract>
