## 20 Structure Candidates

**1. Linear chronology, close third present tense, gradual epistemological withholding**
Five discrete sections proceed in strict chronological order, close third perspective, present tense throughout. The withholding is not of events but of interpretive frame: the reader receives observational data with no settled explanation, accumulating alongside the protagonist without ever being told what to conclude. The recognition at the end recontextualizes all prior material as a two-directional history rather than a solo investigation.
★★★★

---

**2. Tense-shift structure: present tense investigation, past tense recognition**
All sections except the final one are rendered in close third present tense — the investigation ongoing, the pattern still forming, the animal still contested. The final section shifts without announcement to simple past tense. The grammatical shift marks the transition from open inquiry to completed relationship: the only section in past tense is the only thing that is, structurally, finished.
★★★★★

---

**3. Reverse chronology, close third past tense**
Opens at the moment of maximum understanding and moves backward through prior sections, each one revealing less. The withholding is structural: the reader starts knowing the conclusion and learns, in reverse, what it cost to arrive there. The fossil analogy runs backward — from contested anatomy to isolated sclerites.
★★★

---

**4. In medias res from the recognition scene, then chronological**
The story opens at the final moment of recognition, then cuts to the earliest observation and proceeds to the same moment. Readers experience the ending as both arrival and return. Withholding is of context rather than conclusion: the reader knows the destination and watches the path accumulate beneath them.
★★★★

**5. First-person notebook entries with dated headers, non-consecutive**
The narrative is rendered as primary documents — dated entries in a non-official record, first person, with gaps between them. The gaps are felt as withholding; the reader infers what falls in the silences. The official/unofficial split within the protagonist's recording practice becomes the engine of tension.
★★★★

**6. Fragmented non-chronological assembly**
The five sections arrive out of order, forcing the reader to actively reconstruct chronology from internal evidence. The late moment is placed early; the early moment is placed last. Understanding requires re-reading.
★★★

**7. Second person, present tense**

**8. Dual focalization: two investigators converging**
Two alternating close third perspectives — each tracking a parallel line of inquiry from opposite ends of the same anomaly — converge in the final section. The withholding is of the identity of the second perspective; the convergence reveals that the two lines of investigation have been mapping each other.

**9. Distant third, documentary/scientific register**

**16. Linear close third with a single unreturned retrospective frame**

**17. Epistolary: official and unofficial documents interleaved**
Two categories of document — formal work logs (dry, third-person, institutional diction) and unofficial notebook pages (first person, speculative, dense with marginalia) — alternate, covering the same events from different registers. **19. Slowly closing distance: five sections, each one closer than the last**
The narration begins at maximum distance — nearly documentary, no interiority — and each section moves perspectivally closer to the protagonist, ending at the most intimate focal length. The reader's relationship to the subject enacts, formally, the investigator's relationship to the anomaly.

## Top Three

### Candidate 2: Tense-shift structure ★★★★★

**What stands out:** The mechanism does thematic work without adding apparatus. Four sections in present tense situate the investigation as genuinely ongoing — the pattern still forming, the name still in pencil, the animal still contested. The final section's shift to simple past is not announced, not explained, not called attention to. It simply arrives. The investigation is the only one that's finished; and "finished" is the quietest word in the story. Readers who notice will feel a formal shiver; readers who don't will feel a change in weight without knowing why. The grammar of the final section announces that something is complete before the prose does.

**What worries me:** Sustained present tense across four sections at 1500 words can feel breathless or effortful if the prose isn't firmly controlled. The shift also risks being too subtle — not all readers track tense consciously, and the story's meaning can survive without the structural note. If the shift isn't noticed, it neither helps nor harms; but if notice is required for the story to fully land, the gamble fails.

---

### Candidate 4: In medias res from the recognition scene, then chronological ★★★★

**What stands out:** This inverts the reading experience to match the paleontological analogy at the story's heart. The reader encounters the contested anatomy first — sees the recognition, the completed relationship, the name in the notebook — and then must find the sclerites backward through chronology. The question shifts from "what will happen?" to "how did we arrive somewhere this strange?" That is the paleontologist's question. The fossil's gross anatomy was contested for a century; here the body is shown first, the isolated sclerites found after.

**What worries me:** Opening with the recognition scene requires readers to inhabit a fully saturated moment before they have the context to feel its weight. The final scene is dense with meaning accrued over eighteen months; arriving there first, without the accumulation, may leave readers in the position of reading a sentence in a language they don't yet speak. The risk of alienating the reader before they've been given reason to care is real.

---

### Candidate 19: Slowly closing distance, five sections each closer than the last ★★★★

**What stands out:** The formal architecture enacts the investigation's phenomenology. The reader's experience of the protagonist mirrors what the protagonist is discovering about the anomaly: the observer and the observed move toward each other over time. Distance begins high — almost documentary — and closes by the final section to maximum intimacy. The structure demonstrates rather than declares that something approaching has occurred.

**What worries me:** Five distinct focal-length calibrations across 1500 words is a narrow tolerance for execution. If any section fails to maintain its register precisely, the architecture collapses. It also requires the narration to be perceptibly distant in the early sections, which may conflict with the protagonist's technical interiority being present from the first scene.

## Winner

**Candidate 2: Tense-shift structure**

The cleanest, most load-bearing choice. The investigation proceeds in close third present tense for four sections — analyst at her terminal, pattern unresolved, name in pencil, body still contested. The final section shifts to simple past. No announcement. No return. The only section that is grammatically finished is the only moment in which something is finished. The reader's relationship to the text enacts, in the smallest possible register, what the protagonist discovers: that the act of looking was also the act of being seen, and that the moment it became two-directional was also the moment it became past.
