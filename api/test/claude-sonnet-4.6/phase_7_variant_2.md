FLAVOR: close-third present-to-past, work-journal diction, emotion surfaced through logistics and placement rather than interiority, human particulars over atmosphere.

---

The afternoon audit. Marina finds A-B-D where the path should read A-B-C-D. She runs the trace twice. Twelve milliseconds, consistent, across sixteen instances she can pull from the log.

She types the anomaly entry. The log has categories: equipment failure, protocol error, billing discrepancy, misconfiguration. At the bottom of the dropdown, rarely used: *category uncertain.* She selects it. The field exists precisely for this. She's used it twice before.

She opens her working notebook — a green Rite in the Rain field journal she ordered from a surveying supply catalog four years ago when she started keeping her own diagnostic records alongside the official ones. In a new margin column she copies the trace data, not her standard annotation format; the data doesn't fit it. She doesn't think about why she's formatting it differently. She's thinking about the trace.

The official log: filed. The notebook: left open to dry.

---

Eleven pages. She counts them on a Wednesday afternoon.

Five hypotheses; five failure modes, listed and dated. Hardware cascade: failed in March when the behavior persisted through a full equipment rotation at node B. Billing protocol interference: failed in April, wrong frequency distribution. AT&T field routing test: no evidence, wrong signature, ruled out. Node firmware variation: tested in June, doesn't generate this shape. Clerical misconfiguration: seven months of consistency rules it out.

She has gone past hypothesis now. The behavior has a signature: clustering by user tenure, exchange zone, time band. She doesn't have a cause. She has a description.

Her father's paleontology book is propped open on the desk — she took it from a box of his things two springs ago, after he died, intending to sort what her mother would want. This one she kept because she remembered him reading it when she was ten. *The Search for Early Animal Life: A Field Guide to Ediacaran and Cambrian Fossils.* A library sticker on the spine, though it was his own copy. She's had it on the desk since January and hasn't found the right box for it.

She reads the Wiwaxia entry. Sclerites like armor scales; soft body unknown; taxonomy genuinely contested, some researchers placing it with mollusks, others with annelids, neither camp with sufficient material. *Known primarily from isolated sclerites; gross anatomy contested.*

That's what she has. The sclerites without the animal.

She writes in the notebook margin: *this is what it's like to have the scleritome without the animal.* Then on a new page, by itself: *Wiwaxia.* A working name. Scientists name discoveries before they can classify them; the name lets her find the relevant pages without reading through everything.

She caps her pen and puts the book in the desk drawer. Both feel exactly finished.

---

County maps don't work for this kind of topology problem. She switched to graph paper in January — distance plotted against exchange density. The boundary came visible gradually, over about six weeks of charting: the routing behavior with the Wiwaxia signature clusters within a certain radius of her home exchange, and past that radius its character changes. Not absent; different. The clustering is distinct enough to trace a statistical edge.

Three abstracts: one in the *Bell System Technical Journal*, two in the IEEE *Transactions on Communications.* Two analysts at other regional utilities, one team with university network access. Each one describes consistent efficiency deviations in overlapping exchange territories, non-random clustering, no identified hardware or protocol source. All three published without conclusions.

She could call any of them. Starting cold, she would need to reconstruct twenty months of diagnostic history. The overlap of territories might still be equipment variance — unlikely across that many independent exchanges, but possible. She doesn't have a finding. She has a parameter: geographic extent, probable statistical boundary, not yet confirmed.

She notes this in the working notebook. She paper-clips one of the abstracts into the back cover. She doesn't call anyone.

---

The honeypot comes up in Tuesday's exchange analysis. Marina identifies it in the first hour. She has read the federal protocol specifications twice; she's been expecting to encounter one. She hasn't seen one before. She begins observation and enters nothing in the official log beyond a notation that anomaly review is ongoing.

Four days. She tracks traffic that routes through it: unfamiliar nodes, new accounts, short-duration connections from outside the exchange's regular user base. She tracks traffic that routes around it: established users with long tenure and consistent path patterns, taking measurably longer routes. Consistently. Specifically. Her own traffic routes around it. She checks this across four different query types over two days.

On the fourth day she works backward through the logs — four independent sources, cross-referenced — looking for when routing-avoidance behavior around this address range began. The work takes most of the morning. She makes a pot of coffee around ten and forgets it.

The behavior-onset window, estimated from routing-avoidance patterns predating the honeypot's current deployment: September 3 through September 16, 1983.

She looks at this across the four sources. The window doesn't narrow past two weeks.

She opens the working notebook to the name page.

September 9, 1983.

Below the name, her handwriting: *working placeholder, like a fossil name.*

She closes the notebook. She doesn't add anything to it.

---

She ran the cross-check a fifth time. The window held.

She sat with the two dates. What the data said: routing-avoidance behavior around that address range had its onset within two weeks of the date she wrote a name in pencil in a private field journal in her own locked drawer. What the data did not say — could not say — was mechanism. There was no mechanism she could construct. She was not going to construct one.

She pulled up the official anomaly log and typed: *routing irregularity confirmed in November exchange analysis. Consistent traffic-avoidance pattern around monitoring infrastructure, probable federal deployment. Behavior onset predates current deployment. Category uncertain.*

She read it back. Every word true. The one thing she was actually thinking about was absent, which was appropriate — she didn't have a category for it, and writing it down wouldn't have helped.

She picked up the working notebook and put it in her briefcase. Two years it had sat on the desk to the left of the terminal, active position. She wasn't finished with it; she was deciding what kind of document it had turned out to be. She pressed the briefcase clasp down.

She filed the report. She shut off the terminal.

In the adjacent room the exchange equipment ran without her. The indicator lights held steady on the routing boards. Routing decisions moved through it at a rate she couldn't watch.

She put on her coat and went home.
