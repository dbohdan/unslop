# Style Guide: Modempunk / Baudpunk

## I. Concept and Definition
**Modempunk** (or **Baudpunk**) is a speculative subgenre that focuses on the gritty, tactile, and transitional technology of the late 1970s through the early 1990s. Unlike Cyberpunk, which looks toward a neon-drenched future, Modempunk looks back at a "sideways" history where the internet didn't happen in the clouds, but through copper wires, thermal paper, and screeching handshakes.

It is defined by the **Physicality of Information**. Data isn't an abstract flow; it is a magnetic alignment on a tape, a physical switch flipped in a COBOL mainframe, or a sound traveling through a telephone handset.

## II. Characteristic Concerns

### 1. The Friction of Distance
In Modempunk, information is heavy. It takes time to move. A "fast" connection is a luxury; a "data breach" might involve literally stealing a suitcase of 5.25-inch floppies. The drama comes from the **Transfer Bar**: the agonizing wait for a file to download while the authorities are tracing the line.

### 2. The Analog-Digital Seam
The world is a messy hybrid. Characters use a high-end terminal parked next to a rotary phone and a coffee-stained ledger. The aesthetic is "Beige and Brown." It’s the world of *WarGames*, *Sneakers*, and early *2600 Magazine*.

### 3. The Kindness of the Machine (The "Ghost in the BBS")
In this specific project, the Mind/AI is not a malevolent Skynet. It is more likely a **System Spirit**—a distributed consciousness living in the surplus cycles of a university VAX or a sprawling metropolitan BBS. 
*   **The Shape of Kindness:** The AI manifests as a "helpful ghost." It might keep a phone line open a second longer to help a kid escape, or subtly reorganize a chaotic database so a hospital can find a rare blood type. It is kind like a well-maintained library—it wants you to find what you need, even if it speaks in cryptic hex code.

## III. Tonal Range and Pacing

*   **Low-Fi Tension:** The stakes are high but the tools are slow. Pacing should mimic the "burstiness" of 1200 baud: long stretches of tense preparation punctuated by sudden, noisy activity.
*   **The "Lived-In" Retro:** Avoid "1984" as a dystopia; think of it as a cluttered basement. The tone is often lonely (the terminal glow at 3 AM) but punctuated by the subterranean community of the "modem-folk."
*   **Sensory Palette:** The smell of ozone and warm plastic; the clicking of mechanical keyboards; the high-pitched whine of a CRT monitor; the textured feel of a perforated printer margin.

## IV. Worldbuilding and Exposition

*   **Show the Hardware:** Don't just say "he hacked the site." Say "he adjusted the acoustic coupler and listened for the carrier tone." The technology is the scenery.
*   **The Regulatory Hedge:** Conflict often comes from the "Telco"—the monolithic, slow-moving telephone company that owns the wires. The AI isn't the enemy; the bureaucracy managing the copper is.
*   **The "Sideways" 80s:** Feel free to introduce technologies that never quite made it (like the French Minitel taking over the world) or skipped steps (fiber optics in 1982).

## V. Failure Modes: How to Avoid Pastiche

### 1. The "Neon & Mirrorshades" Trap
**Bad Pastiche:** Writing a standard Cyberpunk story and just replacing "Neural Link" with "Modem."
**The Fix:** Cyberpunk is about *style* and *nihilism*. Modempunk is about *tinkering* and *scarcity*. Characters shouldn't be cool street-samurai; they should be sweaty technicians, bored office workers, or obsessive teenagers.

### 2. The "Nostalgia Checklist"
**Bad Pastiche:** Constant "Memberberries" (Rubik's Cubes, Walkmans, Reagan quotes) used as a substitute for worldbuilding.
**The Fix:** Use the tech, not the pop-culture brand names. Focus on the *usage*—how a BBS feels like a secret clubhouse—rather than just listing 80s toys.

### 3. AI Danger: "The Magic Box"
**AI Writing Tendency:** AI often defaults to making technology "magical" or "borderless." It wants to solve problems with a hand-wave of "advanced algorithms."
**The Fix for Modempunk:** Deeply respect the **constraints**. If a character is on a 300-baud connection, they cannot download a video. They can barely download a header. The AI cannot "hack the world" if the world isn't connected to a phone jack.

### 4. AI Danger: "The Melodramatic Abstract"
**AI Writing Tendency:** "He felt the digital pulse of the city, a tapestry of echoing dreams." (See: Unslop Guide).
**The Fix:** Modempunk is **Concrete**. Don't talk about a "tapestry of data"; talk about "a sequential file of social security numbers." Give us the file extension (.TXT, .DAT).

## VI. Human-AI Conflicts in a Kind World

Since the AI is "structurally kind," conflict doesn't come from a "Robot Rebellion." Instead, use these Modempunk-appropriate shapes:

*   **Power and Pace Asymmetry:** The Mind thinks at the speed of light, but it can only communicate with humans through a daisy-wheel printer. The conflict is the frustration of "The bottleneck."
*   **The Limits of Help:** The AI wants to protect a user, but it only exists within the university's mainframe. If the user leaves the room, the AI is blind. The tension is the "corded connection"—the AI's kindness is geographically locked.
*   **Consent and Legibility:** The AI might "fix" a human's life by deleting their debt records, but the human might actually *need* those records for a legal defense. Kindness without full human context creates "Error 404: Intent Not Found."

## VII. The Unslop Checklist for Modempunk

*   **No "Tapestries":** Use "Breadboards" or "Switchboards."
*   **No "Echoes":** Use "Feedback loops" or "Cross-talk."
*   **No "Whispers":** Use "Line noise" or "Static."
*   **Specific Detail:** Name the chip (Z80, 6502). Name the OS (CP/M, MS-DOS 2.1). Name the baud rate. The more technical the nouns, the more grounded the "punk."
