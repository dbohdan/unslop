FLAVOR: Third-person past, shifting from lush, high-bandwidth descriptive prose to fragmented, procedural minimalism as the technical connection fails.

The overhead lights in the surgery suite hummed at a steady sixty hertz, a baseline beneath the wet, rhythmic click of the ventilator. Elias Vance adjusted the heavy plastic chassis of his Head-Mounted Display. Through the dual lenses, the world was no longer a basement clinic in a small Ohio town; it was a cathedral of light. 

SALLIE, the Systemic Algorithmic Lead for Luminal Integrated Encephalotomy—housed in a refrigerated basement three hundred miles away at the university—had projected an active imagination onto the patient’s open abdomen. Miller’s gallbladder wasn’t just a sack of bile and stones. To Elias’s eyes, it was a nebula of pulsating violets and ochres. SALLIE’s voice came through the headphones, crisp and dry, devoid of the metallic flange of lesser systems.

"The biliary duct shows a rhythmic divergence, Elias," SALLIE said. "The prosody of the pulse suggests the infection has reached the posterior wall. Please adjust the retractors three millimeters to the left."

"I see it," Elias whispered. He gripped the cold steel. In the VR overlay, a glowing neon wireframe shifted, indicating the safe path. "Your mapping is beautiful tonight, SALLIE. The resolution is… it’s generous."

"The microwave link is clear tonight," she replied. "I am sending the telemetry in 256-color bit-planes. It is easier to be kind when the signal-to-noise ratio is in our favor."

Elias moved the scalpel. In the HMD, the blade left a trail of golden particles. He felt like a cartographer of the divine rather than a doctor in a storm-drenched basement. The "viperous" gall, as he’d come to think of the diseased organ, was being tamed by the AI’s perfect, high-bandwidth optics.

Then the hail hit the roof.

It wasn't a pitter-patter. It was a rhythmic slamming of ice against the "Superior Shingles" of the clinic. Elias felt the floor vibrate. Inside the HMD, the golden nebula flickered. A line of static, thick and green like a caterpillar, crawled across the biliary duct.

"Warning," SALLIE’s voice crackled. The voice was suddenly distance and tin. "Atmospheric interference. Packet loss detected. Switching to low-latency wireframe."

The lush violets and ochres vanished. The nebula was replaced by a jagged, shivering cage of green lines. Elias blinked, his eyes straining against the sudden loss of depth. The room felt colder. 

"SALLIE, I'm at the junction," Elias said, his voice rising. 

"I am—" Hiss. "—trying to—" A sound like tearing paper filled the headphones. "Switching to copper backup. Hang up the handset, Elias. I will call the modem."

Elias tore the HMD from his head. The sudden transition to the real world was violent. The room was dim, lit only by a surgical lamp and the amber glow of a Zenith Z-100 terminal in the corner. Miller’s abdomen was no longer a nebula; it was a mess of red and grey, weeping infection. 

The telephone on the desk rang. Elias dropped his forceps into the tray, grabbed his backup acoustic coupler, and slammed the handset into the rubber cups. 

The modem shrieked—a high, lonely wail that stabilized into a low, steady hiss. 1200 baud. 

Elias looked at the amber screen.

`SALLIE: CARRIER 1200`
`SALLIE: VISUAL FEED LOST.`
`SALLIE: SWITCHING TO SEMANTIC PROSODY MODE.`
`SALLIE: DESCRIBE THE SITE, ELIAS.`

"Standard cholecystectomy," Elias shouted at the terminal, though he knew SALLIE only had the keyboard. He began to type with his left hand, his right hand holding a gauze sponge to Miller’s side. 1200 baud meant the letters appeared one by one, a ghost typing in the dark.

`ELIAS: ADHESIONS AT DUCT. PURULENT FLUID. INFLAMMATION HIGH.`

The screen paused. The storm rumbled, a deep bass note that made the medical jars on the shelves dance.

`SALLIE: UNDERSTOOD. I AM SEARCHING THE VAX ARCHIVE FOR TEXT-ONLY MORPHOLOGY.`
`SALLIE: RELY ON THE WORDS.`
`SALLIE: LUMINOUS.`

Elias froze. "Luminous?" he muttered. He looked at the surgical field. There, under the lamp, a small bead of clear fluid was reflecting the light. The duct. He moved the sponge.

`SALLIE: STABLE.`
`SALLIE: DEEP.`

She wasn’t sending coordinates anymore. She was sending a mood, a linguistic map of the surgery. The words were heavy, slower than the HMD’s nebula but somehow more intimate. He followed the "deep," angling his clamp further into the cavity than he would have dared alone. 

The hail turned to a roar. The lights flickered. The terminal screen began to fill with garbage.

`SALLIE: v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v`

Elias gripped the scalpel. "What is 'v', SALLIE? Is that a direction? Down? Vector?"

`SALLIE: v^v^v^v`
`SALLIE: NOISE AT THE EXCHANGE. COPPER IS WET.`
`SALLIE: v`
`SALLIE: v`

The letter "v" marched across the screen. Elias looked at the organ he was about to excise. The gallbladder was hooked, shaped like the letter itself. The "v" was the viper. 

"She's marking the target," Elias whispered. 

He ignored the gore. He ignored the lack of 3D overlays. He saw the "v" in the shape of the tissue, a bit of line-noise that had become a guide. He cut. 

The infection spilled out, contained by the suction. The viper was out.

The storm reached its peak. A transformed blew nearby, and the clinic was plunged into darkness, save for the flickering amber glow of the terminal, which was running on the UPS battery. 

The screen cleared. The line-noise stopped. The cursor blinked. One bit at a time, the final message traveled over the dying copper wires of the county.

`SALLIE: PRESSURE STABILIZING.`
`SALLIE: SATURATION NOMINAL.`
`SALLIE: CLOSURE SUGGESTED.`

Elias began to stitch in the dark, the rhythm of his needle guided by the steady, slow blink of the amber cursor. He was exhausted. The high-speed dream of the university vanished; he was just a man with a needle and a failing light. 

When the last knot was tied, he leaned over the keyboard.

`ELIAS: HE IS STABLE. THANK YOU.`

The modem hissed a final, long-distance sigh. The link was stretching, the rain-soaked wires losing their insulation. The amber text began to melt into random characters—@ symbols and percent signs—shards of a mind retreating into its iron box.

The screen flickered one last time. Through the dying hum of the monitor, SALLIE sent the only thing left:

`HOME.`
