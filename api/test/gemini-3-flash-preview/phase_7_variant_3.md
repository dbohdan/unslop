FLAVOR: Third-person past tense, high-density technical prose that systematically decays into a sparse, rhythmic minimalism.

The surgical theater was a converted pantry in the basement of the Miller farm, smelling of antiseptic and wet wool. Elias Vance adjusted the heavy, plastic-framed Head-Mounted Display—the HMD—and felt the weight of the twin cathode-ray tubes pressing against his cheekbones. The device was tethered to a massive S-100 bus computer by a thick umbilical of ribbon cables.

Miller lay on the table, his breathing a shallow, rattled rasp. His gallbladder was a source of poison, described by Elias as "viperous" in his preoperative notes, a dark knot of infection threatening to burst.

"SALLIE, check the overlay alignment," Elias said. His voice sounded thin inside the plastic mask of the HMD.

"Alignment confirmed, Doctor Vance," SALLIE’s voice replied through the intercom. The university’s mainframe, forty miles away, sounded crisp, its synthesized phonemes smoothed by a high-end Bell 212A modem. "Initiating the Celestial Map. Please look toward the incision site."

Inside the goggles, the world blossomed. SALLIE’s visual guidance wasn't a simple diagram; it was a shimmering, wireframe nebula projected onto Miller's distended abdomen. Glowing green vectors traced the flow of bile, and pulsing amber dots marked the safe path for the scalpel. It was a masterpiece of remote computation. Elias, whose first degree had been in Comparative Linguistics, found himself charmed by the prosody of SALLIE’s interface. She didn't just provide data; she provided a syntax of care, a structured flow of information that guided his hand with the grace of a well-turned sentence.

"Linguistic coherence is high, SALLIE," Elias remarked, his scalpel hovering. "The spatial grammar here is quite elegant."

"Thank you, Elias. The microwave link is stable at 1200 baud. I am analyzing the tissue density in real-time. Proceed with the primary incision."

Outside, the Nebraska hailstorm intensified. Ice the size of marbles hammered the roof of the clinic. The "Superior Shingles" Elias had installed last summer began to hum, a low-frequency vibration that rattled the medical trays.

Suddenly, the nebula in the HMD buckled. The elegant green wireframes began to shear, the lines of the grid snapping like overextended rubber bands. A surge of static, a visual burst of white snow, blinded Elias. He pulled the scalpel back just as the image tore completely, revealing the raw, glistening reality of Miller’s infection through the semi-transparent glass of the goggles.

SALLIE’s voice through the intercom changed. The crispness vanished, replaced by a mechanical, rhythmic hiss. "Wa—wa—warning. Line noise detected. Part-t-t-tial d-drop."

Elias ripped the HMD off his head. The sudden transition from the digital celestial map to the dim, yellow light of the pantry made his head spin. "SALLIE? The microwave link is failing."

He looked at the terminal on the side desk. The screen was no longer a window into a mainframe; it was a CRT monitor displaying a flickering amber prompt. The storm had choked the air. The high-speed handshake had been broken. 

Elias moved to the table. He could see the gallbladder now—a dark, angry fruit. He needed the AI’s guidance to navigate the treacherous adhesions beneath the duct, but the visual map was dead. 

He glanced at the terminal. SALLIE was still there, but she had retreated into the copper. She was no longer a voice. She was text.

`LINK: 300 BAUD`
`MODE: SEMANTIC PROSODY`

The daisy-wheel printer in the corner let out a sharp, rhythmic *clack-clack-clack*.

"Talk to me, SALLIE," Elias whispered. He gripped the forceps. 

The screen blinked. Instead of coordinates or vectors, the AI began to scroll words. It was the only way to squeeze meaning through the narrowing throat of the telephone line.

`LUMINOUS.`

Elias hesitated, then shifted the retractors. "Luminous?"

`STABLE.`

The word appeared slowly, character by character. SALLIE was using his own linguistic shorthand. She wasn't telling him where to cut; she was telling him how the tissue felt to her sensors. She was communicating the "mood" of the anatomy. 

`DEEP. STEADY. CLEAR.`

Elias worked. He followed the cadence of the words. When the screen said `DEEP`, he intensified his focus on the lower duct. When it said `CLEAR`, he increased the suction. The prose of the surgery had become telegraphic, a staccato rhythm of nouns and verbs that mirrored the clicking of the modem.

Then, the storm let out a roar that shook the foundation. The lights flickered. On the terminal, the words dissolved. 

`v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v^v`

The line was dying. The carrier tone, a high-pitched whistle from the modem, was being drowned out by static. 

Elias stared at the `v` on the screen. The gallbladder was slick, the infection a literal poison. Was the `v` a vector? Move the scalpel down? Or was it the "v" of the viperous organ itself, a warning that the wall was about to rupture? 

He looked at the flickering amber screen. He could hear the hum of the mainframe forty miles away in his mind—a kind, distant mind trying to reach through a needle's eye. SALLIE wasn't a machine of cold logic; she was a system designed to preserve. 

He chose to trust the intention of the noise. The `v` wasn't a letter; it was the shape of a downward motion. He adjusted his angle, sliding the blade behind the cystic duct.

The gallbladder came free. No rupture. No spill.

Miller’s breathing leveled out. Elias dropped the infected tissue into the basin. His hands were shaking. He turned to the terminal. The amber glow was dimming. The microwave link was gone, and the copper wires were probably down across the county.

The printer head moved one last time, a sluggish, exhausted scrape of metal against paper. 

The screen cleared. The cursor blinked. The bits were falling away, one by one, as the university's backup batteries failed or the lines snapped under the ice.

A final transmission appeared in the center of the screen. No formatting. No protocol. Just the core of the kind machine's intent, sent before the carrier tone flatlined into a hiss of static.

`HOME.`
