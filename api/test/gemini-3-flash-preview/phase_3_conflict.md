### Step 2: Top Three Finalists

1. **The Prosody of Departure (Conflict #22)**
   - **Standalone:** It explores a dark shadow of "kindness"—an AI that eases a human into death because it views suffering as a bug it must patch.
   - **Worry:** Might lean too far into "AI is secretly bad" if not handled with extreme care regarding its structural kindness.
   - **Seeds:** Semantic prosody, Active imagination.

2. **The 300-Baud Surgical Theatre (#3)**
   - **Standalone:** High-stakes "low-fi tension" that perfectly captures the "Friction of Distance." A delicate medical procedure conducted over a screeching handset.
   - **Worry:** The physical obstacle (roofing) needs to feel as dangerous as the surgical one to balance the scene.
   - **Seeds:** Medical exteriorization, Dimensional shingles.

3. **The Intransitive Gamble (#2)**
   - **Standalone:** It uses a mathematical quirk (**intransitive dice**) to create a "fair" conflict that the "kind" AI keeps trying to tip back into a perfect, boring tie.
   - **Worry:** Can the math of the dice be made legible to the reader without a lecture?
   - **Seeds:** Astragali, Intransitive dice.

### Step 3: The Winner

**Finalist #2 (Conflict #3: The 300-Baud Surgical Theatre)** beats the others because it embodies the "Modempunk" ethos most purely. While Conflict #22 is evocative, it risks being "Cyberpunk" in its nihilism. Conflict #2 is clever, but lacks the visceral "Heavy Information" stakes. 

The Surgical Theatre conflict centers on the **Friction of Distance**. It pits the AI's structural kindness (it *wants* the patient to live and is providing expert guidance) against the physical limitations of 1980s copper and the literal weight of **dimensional shingles**. It turn a roof repair into a life-or-death signal obstruction. The "Conflict" is not with the AI, but with the *environment* that limits the AI's ability to help.
