# Page Seventy-Three

## Abstract

In year fourteen post-boundary, a small infantry company on the frontier world KIRSH-4 convenes a promotion board for the company command sergeant major billet, vacant since the previous holder's death in action. The candidate is Sergeant First Class Idris Mokoena of the engineering platoon, whose service began in a previously-segregated formation dissolved by the institution's General Order 9981 three years before the unit was sent past the cosmic event horizon on a one-way deployment. Captain Tévar Olen presides over the board, conducting the proceeding by the institution's manual: convening order, examination of record, three required questions, deliberation, findings. The board votes unanimously for advancement. The findings are filed and read at evening formation. The story renders the proceeding in close third on Olen, structured by the manual's named procedural sections.

## Story

### REPORTING IN & OPENING STATEMENT

Olen sets the manual down on the table and opens it to the page she has marked, which is the same page she has marked for every promotion board this unit has held since the boundary closed. Page seventy-three. The convening order's required text. The page is brittle in two places where the unit's previous CO worked the corner with her thumb during the four boards she sat through before the contact at the river. Olen does not work the corner. She turns to face the recording clerk and reads the convening order from the page.

The convening order names the candidate. Mokoena, Idris. Sergeant First Class. Engineering Platoon, Charlie Company, 4th Light Reaction Battalion, attached. It names the billet: company command sergeant major. It names the board: Captain Olen presiding, First Sergeant Dub, Lieutenant Vey. The recording clerk records.

Olen reads the institution's required opening. The board is convened under Article Seven of the institution's promotion code, in conformity with the unit's standing order regarding boards of this kind, in furtherance of the institution's policy regarding the advancement of qualified non-commissioned officers to billets of senior responsibility. She reads to the end and pauses for the recording clerk's acknowledgment.

"Recorded, ma'am." Specialist Keto's hand does not stop moving on the pad.

### EXAMINATION OF RECORD

"Specialist," Olen says. "The jacket."

Keto opens the jacket on her pad. Per manual procedure, the candidate's service record is read aloud by the recording clerk in the candidate's absence. Mokoena waits in the antechamber. Keto begins.

Mokoena, Idris. Date of birth, 14 Pluvial 0083. Place of origin, New Aksum, on the seaward continent of the home world. Date of induction, 12 Solar 0098. Initial training, six months in the engineering specialty at the institution's training facility on the home world. Initial assignment, 22 Solar 0099, to 2nd Detached Engineering Platoon, attached to but separate from 4th Light Reaction Battalion, per the institution's then-standing policy regarding the segregated organization of the engineering specialty.

Olen listens. Keto's voice reaches the end of a clause and pauses for the comma the form encodes — a manual convention that has the effect, when properly observed, of making the form's structure audible.

Initial evaluations, year one. Mokoena's first platoon commander was Lieutenant Aren Volta, killed in the action at Yebrek Pass two years and three months later. Volta's evaluation of Mokoena was that he showed unusual technical aptitude in the placement of obstacle systems and was, in Volta's recorded words, a soldier who would be capable of advancement when the institution permitted it. The clause was a stock formulation in evaluations rendered on members of the segregated platoons in that period. Keto reads it as written.

Year two. Year three. Three successive platoon commanders. Two killed in action, one rotated.

Year four. The institution's General Order 9981, issued 14 Pluvial 0102. Keto reads the order's effect on Mokoena's record. 2nd Detached Engineering Platoon was dissolved as a separate formation at the conclusion of the institution's transition period. Its personnel were reassigned to the engineering platoons of the line companies of their parent battalions. Mokoena's reassignment to the engineering platoon of Charlie Company, 4th Light Reaction, recorded as 03 Pluvial 0102. Without break in service. Initial evaluation under Captain Itua, the company's then-commander, on the standard form. Six categories. Mokoena's marks read in the form's order. Tactical proficiency. Technical proficiency. Leadership of subordinates. Tactical communication. Initiative. Physical readiness. The institution's highest mark in five categories. Second-highest in tactical communication, with a written remark by Itua that the deficiency was specific to written reporting and not to verbal command.

The boundary closed on 21 Estiv 0105.

Keto reads forward. The records of Mokoena's service after the boundary are rendered in the same form. The same paper. The unit had brought through three crates of the standard evaluation form and had used, at present count, two crates and one hundred and forty-seven sheets. Keto's voice does not address the change in the boundary's status. The form does not invite that address.

Year three post-boundary. The action at the eastern ridge. Keto reads the citation in the institution's award language. Mokoena, then a sergeant, recovered four of his squad under fire from a position the company had been ordered to abandon. The citation was written by Captain Mwathi, killed in action seven months after the action at the ridge. Mwathi carved the citation into the unit's commendation register herself because the printer had failed and she did not want to wait. The carved entry is on the south wall of this room. Olen does not look at it now.

Year four through year nine. Promotion to sergeant first class. Reassignment to senior NCO of the engineering platoon. The death of the platoon's previous senior NCO, Sergeant Major Ojo, in the action at the bend, fourteen months ago. Ojo had been double-slotted under emergency procedure for three years before her death.

Keto closes the jacket. "Record concludes, ma'am."

"Recorded," Olen says.

### BOARD QUESTIONING

Mokoena is brought in. He reports to the board in the form the manual specifies. He stands at parade rest. Olen consults the manual's questioning section.

"Sergeant Mokoena. Two soldiers under your authority, one from the regular line and one from the engineering platoon, are in unresolved dispute over a duty assignment that the standing order does not specifically address. The regular line soldier appeals to seniority by pre-integration date. The engineering soldier appeals to the integration order's specification of equivalence. The matter is brought to you. Resolve it."

Mokoena answers. The duty assignment is within the senior NCO's discretion under the standing order's residual clause. He gives the qualifications he would consider: current refresher status on the relevant equipment, current physical readiness mark, current discipline record clean. He gives the readiness criterion: rest cycle within the manual's specification. The soldier whose record meets all four criteria is the soldier assigned. If both meet all four, the assignment is by alphabetical order of last name, per the standing order's tie-breaking convention. The seniority appeal is not relevant under the standing order. The integration-order equivalence appeal is not relevant either, because the standing order already specifies the basis for the assignment. The assignment is recorded in the company's daily record with the standing notation. The non-assigned soldier may appeal to the company commander within twenty-four hours per manual.

He affirms in the manual's closing form.

Dub asks the follow-up. "And if the appeal is brought to you instead of to the commander?"

"I direct her to the commander. The senior NCO does not adjudicate appeals from his own assignments. I record the redirection in the daily record."

Olen reads the second question. "The company commander has issued an order you believe to be tactically unsound. The order is lawful and within the company commander's authority. The order is to be executed in fourteen hours. You are the company command sergeant major. What is your responsibility?"

Mokoena answers. The senior NCO's responsibility is to advise the commander on the basis of what the senior NCO knows, in the form the commander will hear, in the time available. The procedure is a private audience, the concern presented in operational terms, the commander's response. For an order at fourteen hours, the concern is raised within four hours of the order being issued. If the concern is raised and not accepted, the senior NCO's responsibility is to execute the order as given, to the standard the unit holds, and to record the concern in the company's standing record under the operational dissent notation, item 14 in the daily record's standard categories, in the same form as any operational note, without prejudice to the commander.

He affirms.

Vey asks the follow-up. "And if the concern is accepted but the commander modifies the order in a way you also believe unsound?"

"The procedure repeats. Each iteration of the order is its own occasion for the senior NCO's responsibility."

Olen reads the third question. "What does the unit's senior NCO owe the unit's most junior soldier?"

Mokoena does not answer immediately. The manual permits the candidate a moment of consideration before answering. He uses it. Then he answers.

"Seven things, by the manual's specification at sections four through ten.

"Training to the unit's standard, which is the institution's standard as adapted to the unit's operational environment.

"Assignment to a billet for which she is qualified, by the qualifications named in section five.

"Evaluation by the manual's procedure on the standard form, on the schedule the manual specifies, and not otherwise.

"Promotion by the manual's procedure when she is qualified, which means her qualifications are examined by a board convened per manual, and the board's findings are recorded.

"Hearing of grievance by the procedure named in section eight, which provides the soldier the senior NCO's audience on the day requested or the next operational day, and the senior NCO's response in operational terms.

"Recording in the company's record of her service, in the form the institution requires, by the procedure the manual specifies, on the paper the unit holds for that purpose.

"Remembrance in the unit's commendation register and in the unit's casualty record when she is killed, in the form Captain Mwathi entered the eastern-ridge citation when the printer had failed."

He affirms in the manual's closing form.

"The board has no further questions. Sergeant Mokoena, withdraw."

Mokoena withdraws.

### DELIBERATION

Per manual, the board deliberates with the candidate withdrawn. The senior member records each member's findings in turn.

"First Sergeant. Your findings."

"Qualified by record. Qualified by examination. I have no reservation. I find for advancement."

Keto records.

"Lieutenant. Your findings."

"Qualified by record. Qualified by examination. The candidate's answer to the second question I would have wanted somewhat earlier in his service, but it was given correctly and to standard. I find for advancement."

Keto records.

Olen does not consult the manual on her own findings. She knows the form.

"Senior member's findings. The candidate's qualifications by record include nine years' service in the engineering platoon at progressively responsible billets, with evaluations at the institution's highest mark in five categories under three successive commanders, and a recovery action at the eastern ridge in post-boundary year three for which the citation in the unit's commendation register is read into this proceeding's evidence.

"The candidate's qualifications by examination include responsive answers to the manual's three required questions at this billet level. The first answer applied the standing order's residual clause correctly and resolved the discipline question without recourse to either appeal. The second answer correctly identified the senior NCO's iterative responsibility under operational dissent. The third answer enumerated the manual's specification at sections four through ten in the manual's order and named the unit's commendation register as the locus of the seventh item.

"The unit's requirement for the company command sergeant major billet is established by the billet's vacancy of fourteen months following the death in action of the previous holder.

"The candidate's fitness for the billet is established by the foregoing.

"I find for advancement."

The board's vote is taken. The board's vote is unanimous. Keto records.

"Recorded."

### FINDINGS & ANNOUNCEMENT

The findings document is completed by the senior member's hand on the manual's form. Olen writes the findings in the institution's specified format. The form has fifteen lines. She fills them in. She signs. The form is countersigned by Dub and Vey.

The manual specifies that the findings are read at the unit's next formation. The unit's next formation is at eighteen hundred. Olen has eleven minutes.

She gathers the manual, the jacket, the findings document, and the convening order. She places the findings document in the file marked PROMOTIONS, the third drawer of the second cabinet, with the thirty-one preceding entries — the two preceding entries for the company command sergeant major billet recorded in the unit's commendation register as killed in action.

At eighteen hundred Olen stands in front of the company in the assembly area. She reads the findings as the manual specifies. The company stands at attention. Mokoena reports forward at the read of his name and accepts the billet at the read of the findings' final line. The unit passes in review and is dismissed at eighteen seventeen.

Olen returns to her office. She places the manual on the corner of her desk. She works the brittle corner of page seventy-three with her thumb, once, and closes it.
