# Dream Me Something Kinder, Darling

## Abstract

Martin Keene is a mid-level manager in Hartwell, a city where citizens' dreams are publicly broadcast each morning as a civic-transparency measure. After Martin's dream of attacking his boss Douglas with a stapler airs on the 6:12 AM broadcast, he goes to work anyway. Over the course of the workday, rendered primarily through emails and a single in-person meeting, Douglas calls Martin into a small conference room, tells him to use the heavy stapler from his own desk drawer on the budget folder, and initiates a performance review. Martin's wife leaves a note and goes to stay at her mother's. That night, alone in the apartment, Martin dictates an apology-dream to his ambient household AI, which assembles the script and reads it back to him repeatedly until he falls asleep.

## Story

In the dream the kitchenette smelled like coffee and the coffee was burnt and Douglas was at the counter with his back to him and Martin crossed the floor and took the stapler off the counter and said Douglas's name and Douglas turned and Martin brought the stapler down on Douglas's collarbone and said *I have wanted to do this for eleven years.* And Douglas went to his knees and Martin hit him again, and it was easy, the way in a dream a thing is easy, and Martin hit him again, and Martin hit him again, and Martin said, with each hit, *I wanted to. I wanted to. I wanted to. I wanted to.*

Conference room C was the small one. A table for four, one phone console in the middle, a window onto the air shaft. Martin sat with his back to the window. Douglas came in at 11:48 carrying a printout and a cardboard coffee and a manila folder.

"Close the door."

Martin closed the door.

Douglas sat across from him and put the folder down and squared it with the edge of the table. He drank from the coffee. He did not offer Martin any. Then he slid the folder across.

"Line 17," he said.

Martin opened it. The amortization sheet. He had sent it at 9:47 that morning.

"I pulled it against adjusted basis, which is what the Q2 model has been running on since June. I can pull it against unadjusted if you—"

"You sent me adjusted."

"Yes."

"I asked for both."

"You asked for both at 11:31. I've been pulling it."

Douglas drank from the coffee. He looked past Martin at the window. The air shaft was a gray rectangle with a pigeon on the ledge of the floor below.

"Staple that, please," Douglas said. "The two sheets. Before you bring it back. There's a stapler on my desk. Use mine. I like the heavy one."

The sentence arrived in the room and sat there. Martin looked at the folder. Douglas looked at the pigeon. Somewhere on the floor someone laughed at something and it carried.

"All right," Martin said.

"The heavy one," Douglas said. "In the left drawer."

"All right."

"Fifteen minutes."

Douglas stood and took his coffee and his printout and left the folder on the table. At the door he stopped with his hand on the knob and he said, not turning around: "The review cycle starts next week. I wanted to give you a heads-up."

Then he left.

Martin sat with the folder for a long time. The pigeon on the ledge adjusted one foot and then the other and did not move after that. When he stood up he took the folder and went to Douglas's office and opened the left drawer and took out the heavy stapler, which was chrome, and stapled the sheets together once in the corner. He put the stapler back. He closed the drawer. He brought the folder to Douglas's inbox and set it on top of three other folders and he went back to his desk.

The apartment was dark except for the range light Helen always left on. On the counter, under the range light, was a blue ceramic bowl with a note folded once and set on top of it. He did not read the note. He opened the cabinet and took down the cereal and a second bowl and poured the cereal and did not open the refrigerator. He ate the cereal dry standing at the counter.
