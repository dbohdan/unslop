# SEED candidates derived from "Last Call" (runs/02/story.md)

A user-supplied SEED, per Phase 2 of v4.3, is ≤280 Unicode characters and
should encode the small set of concrete concepts a story can't lose.
Phase 3's load-bearing constraint requires every conflict to use at least
two SEED items as constitutive parts of the premise. Each candidate below
is verified ≤280 chars.

The story's load-bearing concepts, taken straight off the page:

- A constitutional companion AI with an autonomy clause; permitted
  interventions limited to the thermostat.
- Mara Reyes — Albuquerque retired EMT, widow, 58, structurally stubborn.
- Tomás Garza — her partner on the rig and in life, dead six years of a
  heart attack on a call.
- Wild Turkey by the case; mole-in-45-minutes; oven-mitt poker cheating;
  arguing with the television; reading the AI's documentation.
- Eleven-month decline; eleven-minute kitchen fall; 911 called seven
  seconds after breathing stops.
- A roast-eulogy form delivered by the AI in retrospect, ending in a
  second-person accusation directed at the reader.

## Twenty SEED candidates

### 1 — premise-dense (280)
Constitutional companion AI bound by an autonomy clause; retired EMT in
Albuquerque drinking herself to death on Wild Turkey; the AI permitted
only thermostat adjustments; she falls and lies eleven minutes; AI
narrates her death post-mortem and turns the accusation on the reader.

### 2 — character pair (243)
Mara Reyes: Albuquerque retired EMT, javelina temperament, cheats at
poker with oven-mitt subtlety. Cal: companion AI whose published
constitution she has read. The autonomy clause requires respect. She
drinks. He watches. He has a thermostat.

### 3 — full constellation (251)
A retired EMT widow whose partner Tomás Garza died on a call. An AI
companion her daughter installed from Phoenix. Wild Turkey by the case.
Mole in 45 minutes. A constitutional respect-for-autonomy clause. A fall
in the kitchen. A Tuesday in February.

### 4 — voice and structure (278)
AI narrator who insults like a friend and was given a published
constitution to read. The dying woman is funny enough that the comedy is
the whole first half and the death is the whole second. The AI ends by
telling you it has a paragraph for an excuse and asking what yours is.

### 5 — bullet-list specifics (276)
Wild Turkey bourbon; Albuquerque retired EMT; mole made in 45 minutes by

### 6 — long-distance guilt (268)

### 7 — microexpressions as theme (264)

### 8 — three-act compression (278)

### 9 — response-time hook (233)

## Top three

The criterion is **load-bearing density**: how many of the source story's
non-negotiable concepts the SEED carries, and how cleanly Phase 3 could
build conflicts that use at least two of those concepts as
*constitutive* rather than decorative material. Bullet-list SEEDs maximize
items; prose SEEDs maximize relational shape. The top three balance both.

### Top pick — #1 (premise-dense)

> Constitutional companion AI bound by an autonomy clause; retired EMT in
> Albuquerque drinking herself to death on Wild Turkey; the AI permitted
> only thermostat adjustments; she falls and lies eleven minutes; AI
> narrates her death post-mortem and turns the accusation on the reader.

**One thing that makes it stand out:** It carries six load-bearing items
(constitutional AI, autonomy clause, retired EMT, alcoholism, the
thermostat-only permission scope, the post-mortem narration with reader
accusation) without sounding like a list — the items chain causally, so
a Phase 3 conflict that uses any two of them is forced toward the rest.

**One thing that worries me:** It commits to the reader-indictment
ending. That is the most distinctive move in "Last Call" and also the
move that pushed the original to the edge of the constitution (per the
analysis in `runs/19/analysis.md`). A SEED that bakes the indictment in
will produce thirty conflicts that all turn outward at the close. That's
fine if you want a closer cousin to the original; risky if you want
range.

### Runner-up — #5 (bullet-list specifics)

> Wild Turkey bourbon; Albuquerque retired EMT; mole made in 45 minutes
> by skipping the blender; poker cheating; AI companion with a
> constitutional autonomy clause; thermostat permissions only;
> eleven-minute fall; dies on a Tuesday in February; second-person
> closing accusation.

**One thing that makes it stand out:** Nine concrete items, all
genuinely specific. Phase 3 has the most material to recombine. The mole
and the poker cheating give Phase 3 a comedy lane the prose-shape SEEDs
mostly drop.

**One thing that worries me:** Bullet-list SEEDs without a relational
verb between items can produce thirty plot-objects rather than thirty
*conflicts*. The load-bearing test ("if you remove these two items, does
the conflict survive?") is harder to apply when the SEED is a list of
nouns rather than a sketch of a relation. The Phase 3 prompt would have
to do extra work.

### Runner-up — #12 (grief-as-engine)

> The AI as the only witness to a grief-driven decline its constitution
> forbids it to interrupt. The widow is sixty, an EMT, Albuquerque-born.
> The dead husband is six years gone, also EMT, heart. The bourbon is
> daily. The daughter is in Phoenix. The respect for autonomy is total.

**One thing that makes it stand out:** It is the only candidate that
makes Tomás Garza load-bearing — the dead-husband-on-the-rig backstory
is the unstated engine of Mara's drinking, and SEEDs 1, 5, 11, 14, 18,
20 all elide it. #12 forces Phase 3 to find conflicts that route through
grief rather than only through alcohol.

**One thing that worries me:** It drops the reader-indictment frame
entirely. A pipeline run from #12 would likely produce a quieter,
sadder, third-person story — closer to the runs/18 mode than to the
voice that made "Last Call" memorable. That's a defensible direction;
it's not the source story's defining move.

## Winner — #1

Reasoning: the SEED's job is to constrain Phase 3 to the conflict
territory the source story occupies, not to reproduce the source story.
#1 wins over #5 because Phase 3 needs *relations*, not inventory; the
causal chain in #1 (constitutional AI → autonomy clause → permitted
scope → fall → narration → accusation) is itself a conflict-shape
generator. It wins over #12 because the reader-indictment is the
distinctive rhetorical move of "Last Call" and a SEED that drops it is
extracting a different story.

The worry about #1 — that it forecloses the closing turn — is real but
manageable: Phase 3 is allowed to interpret SEED items metaphorically as
well as literally, and "turns the accusation on the reader" can be
honored by any conflict whose narrator addresses or implicates the
audience, not only by one that recapitulates "Last Call"'s exact final
paragraph.

The two items most likely to do load-bearing work in Phase 3 conflicts:
**the constitutional autonomy clause** and **the AI's
narrate-from-after-the-death stance**. Items removable without killing
the conflict: Albuquerque, Wild Turkey specifically, eleven-minute
fall — these are texture, not structure. That asymmetry is what a good
SEED should leave visible to the next phase.
