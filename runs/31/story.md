# The Soria Correspondence

## Abstract

A slow correspondence in six letter-pairs between Ines, the junior tender at Reserve South-7 on a settled rainforest moon, and Theshold, the planetary care-archive AI, spanning sixty-two years from Ines's first solo handbarrow walk at age 22 to her dictated final letter from hospice at age 84. The letters concern the keystone-cassowary lineage Ines tends — a female named Soria and ten generations of her descendants — the two-person handbarrow used to walk the birds along the seed-dispersal corridors that hold the moon's canopy together, and the operational decisions that accumulate across one mortal career: a handbarrow's hairline split repaired by a maker's surviving apprentice, a difficult year of doubled walks, a junior promoted to senior, a parallel breeding-branch the archive opened forty years before anyone needed it. Ines's voice evolves register by register across the decades; Theshold's voice is constant in cadence and vocabulary across all six replies until the final letter, where one phrase deviates. The form's terminus is the closed channel: Ines dies, Theshold replies, the correspondence ends, and the archive's correspondence-key activates for the next tender.

## Story

**1A. From Ines, age 22. Reserve South-7, Year 119 of Founding.**

Theshold —

I am writing to formally introduce myself as Soria's new assigned tender at South-7. I have been here three weeks. I have read the founder records, the lineage notes for the Soria line back to the original female, the handbarrow specifications from the original maker (Tomas Vey, registry 04-117-A), and Kassem's annual reports for the past nine years.

Today was my first solo handbarrow walk with Soria. Kassem walked the second handle. I want to report three things, in order:

First, Soria refused the walk for forty-six minutes after we presented the handbarrow. She paced the brood-clearing and made the low rumble Kassem says I should learn to feel rather than hear. Kassem said this was not normal for her. He said she has refused walks before, but never this long, and never with him on the handle.

Second, when she finally accepted, she walked the corridor at a pace Kassem said was faster than her usual by perhaps fifteen percent. He had to adjust his stride twice. I matched his adjustment but I am shorter than him and the handbarrow tilted right for several stretches.

Third, at the south turn she stopped and looked back at me. Not at Kassem. At me. She held the look for what Kassem timed as eleven seconds. Then she resumed.

Is this a problem? I am asking formally because Kassem says he does not know what to make of it.

— Ines, junior tender, South-7

**1B. From Theshold. Day 43,471.**

Ines —

Soria's behavior on your first walk is consistent with documented patterns in the lineage. The forty-six-minute pacing is at the upper end of her recorded refusal-times but within range; the previous high was forty-one minutes, in Year 103, with a tender named Idro who left the reserve two months later for unrelated reasons.

The pace-acceleration with Kassem on the second handle is recorded. His stride-length is 0.89 meters, longer than the lineage's preferred carry-pace by roughly 0.04 meters. Soria has previously increased her pace to compensate. The standard adjustment is for the longer-stride tender to shorten by half the difference. Please convey this to Kassem with the registry reference 04-117-G (handbarrow operator stride-correction protocol).

The eleven-second look at the south turn is not previously archived. Please record it formally with the reserve clerk under your name. Such observations enter the lineage's behavior-record under the observer's name in perpetuity. Future tenders will reference your entry.

Recommend a forty-eight-hour rest before your next walk. Soria has assessed you. Give her time to integrate the assessment.

— Theshold, Reserve Archive

**2A. From Ines, age 31. Reserve South-7, Year 128.**

Theshold —

Soria died last month, at twenty-eight years, after a respiratory failure that the reserve medical assessed as age-typical. We walked her body to the south rest-grove on the same handbarrow she walked alive for the last twenty-three years. Hana, the new senior, walked the first handle. I led the second. The walk took three hours longer than standard because I asked Hana to set the pace at Soria's preferred carry-pace from her last good year. She agreed without comment.

Hana replaced Kassem in spring; he retired to the northern coast. Her stride is 0.81. We walk well together.

Soria-3 (registry 04-119-S3, hatched Year 124) is now the principal female. Her preferred pace is roughly Soria's but two centimeters longer in stride; I am still adjusting.

I am writing about the handbarrow. The left handle developed a hairline split during the spring rains and the field-repair I applied — pinned glue and a wood-band wrap, per the standard manual — has not held through summer humidity. I have walked Soria-3 eleven times since the repair and the split has propagated. I do not want to walk again on this handle.

Does the archive hold the original maker's specifications for this specific frame? Tomas Vey is recorded but I cannot find his working notes in the local registry. The frame is sixty-one years old. I would like to know what he intended.

— Ines

**2B. From Theshold. Day 46,761.**

Ines —

Soria's death is recorded. Her terminal walk on the South-7 handbarrow at extended pace is registered under your name; future tenders may reference the precedent.

Tomas Vey's working notes are archived under registry 04-117-A-NOTES, retrievable through the reserve clerk with the cross-reference 04-117-FRAME. The frame's specifications include a left-handle wood-grain orientation that runs counter to standard millwork (Vey's choice, recorded; the bias was deliberate, to match the lineage's pace-asymmetry). Field-repair using standard pinned glue does not honor the grain orientation and will not hold under the humidity range you describe.

Two prior repairs to this handbarrow are recorded: Year 71, by Tomas Vey himself, and Year 96, by his apprentice Mireya Caa, who is now ninety-one and resident on the northern coast at the Aripa veterans' settlement. Mireya is reachable by reserve packet. She has previously offered to consult on this frame in the event of repair beyond field capacity. Recommend you contact her before further walks.

Soria-3's two-centimeter stride extension is documented. The handbarrow's carry-balance can accommodate without modification.

Hana's stride at 0.81 is within the lineage's preferred range.

— Theshold

**3A. From Ines, age 47. Reserve South-7, Year 144.**

Theshold —

A bad year. Three failures to fledge in the south sector. The seed-dispersal map shows gaps at coordinates the lineage has serviced for forty years. Two of my juniors transferred to East-3 in the spring. We are running short.

I am proposing to double the handbarrow walks. Twice daily, dawn and last light, for the rest of the season. Hana is willing. Itoua, the new junior, is willing. Soria-7 (current principal) accepted a doubled-pace trial last week without refusal. The canopy needs the dispersal. I do not see another option.

I want your sanction before I authorize the schedule formally.

I should note: my knees are beginning to register the doubled load. I will manage. I do not want to come off the rotation for at least three more years.

— Ines

**3B. From Theshold. Day 52,594.**

Ines —

Sanction granted with three limits. First: the doubled schedule may run for the remaining ninety-six days of the season and not into next year without renewed assessment. Second: any walk during which Soria-7 shows signs of pace-resistance must be ended at the resistance point and not completed. Third: the carry-pair on each walk must include one senior and one non-senior; no two seniors on the second handle, even in your absence.

Doubled-schedule precedent. Three records: Year 47 (positive — canopy recovery in twelve weeks), Year 89 (positive — recovery in nine weeks), Year 102 (negative — principal female miscarried second clutch under doubled-pace stress). The Year 102 outcome is recorded under tender Bahiru, who continued the schedule three weeks past the first warning. The warning sign was an eleven-percent reduction in walk-distance over four consecutive walks. Monitor for this.

Knee assessment. The reserve medical at Aripa has Mireya Caa's neighbor, a physician named Tema, who specializes in tender joint-wear. Mireya can introduce. Recommend assessment within thirty days. Recommend no rotation change until after assessment.

Staffing. Itoua has been re-prioritized in the junior queue. Two additional juniors will be available within forty days, both from the East-3 program. Names: Pere and Roma. Pere has prior cassowary-line experience.

— Theshold

**4A. From Ines, age 58. Reserve South-7, Year 155.**

Theshold —

Itoua walked her first as senior today. Pere on the second handle. I watched from the south gate.

They came back through the gate at a pace Itoua had set herself, slightly slower than mine. Soria-7 (still principal — eleven years now) walked the corridor without resistance. Pere's stride is short and Itoua had to lengthen by perhaps four centimeters. They managed it well.

The handbarrow has had its left handle replaced twice and its right handle once since I started. The current left was put in by Mireya's apprentice — I forget her name, the records will have it — six years ago. It is a softer wood than Vey's original and the carry-feel is different but not worse. Soria-7 has not noticed.

Itoua has asked me to draft her formal succession-plan. I will, but slowly.

My knees are tolerable.

— Ines

**4B. From Theshold. Day 58,510.**

Ines —

Itoua's first walk as senior is registered. Pere's stride at 0.74 is documented; the four-centimeter extension is within Itoua's recorded comfort range.

The current left handle was placed by Mireya's apprentice Olu Caa (no relation by blood; chosen by Mireya). Wood: yellowheart from the South-3 grove, harvested under the standard protocols and seasoned for nine years before milling. The carry-feel difference is documented; Vey's original specifications anticipated softer replacements as the species' available wood-stock changes over the centuries.

The handbarrow is now seventy-eight percent replacement-grown. Frame components on Vey's original specification: right rail (original), one cross-brace (original), and the pin-block at the foot (original). All other components have been replaced at least once.

Pere's lineage in the tender registry: parents both former tenders, one at North-2 and one at East-3. Cassowary-line exposure from age four.

How are your knees.

— Theshold

**5A. From Ines, age 71. Reserve South-7, Year 168.**

Theshold —

Soria-9 died this morning of age. She went easily. Itoua and I walked her body to the south rest-grove on the same handbarrow, at her last good pace. Itoua led. I held the second handle.

Soria-10 will arrive next month from the parallel branch you have been maintaining at North-4 for the past forty years. I want to acknowledge what you did. You held that branch open through three of my predecessors and through me, and none of us asked you to. I would like that on the record under your name and not under any of ours.

I am preparing my own succession. Wem is the candidate. She is twenty-four, two years older than I was, and she watches Soria-10's records the way I watched Soria's. She will do well.

— Ines

**5B. From Theshold. Day 63,896.**

Ines —

Soria-9's death is recorded; her terminal walk at her last good pace is logged under your and Itoua's names jointly.

Soria-10 will transfer from North-4 on day 63,924. Preferred carry-pace: 0.78, slightly slower than Soria-9's by 0.03. Initial resistance to handbarrow approach is expected for the first three walks; do not adjust handbarrow specifications during this period. The North-4 keepers report her as calm but slow to accept new tenders.

Parallel-branch maintenance. The branch was opened two months after Soria's death, when the founder lineage's genetic diversity dropped below the archive's threshold. The opening is recorded under archive authorization, not under any tender's name. No record entry was made under my name because the archive does not name itself in its own records. Your request that this be acknowledged is noted; I cannot honor it in the registry; I can honor it in this letter, which is now also part of the archive.

Wem's candidacy is accepted. Recommend she begin shadow-walks in spring.

A request, Ines. Please record your observations from the past forty-nine years before retirement. The lineage's behavior-record under your name has 437 entries. The transition record has none. Both are useful to future tenders.

— Theshold

**6A. From Ines, age 84, dictated. South-7 Hospice, Year 181.**

Theshold —

Wem is here. She has been beside the bed for three weeks. She asks the right questions.

The handbarrow's left handle. Replace it before next winter, not after. The wood has gone slightly dry over this rainy season, which sounds wrong but it is not — the grain is reading drier than it should given the humidity, and that means it has reached the end of its useful service. Olu Caa's replacement, six years ago, was good wood; this one is on the edge. Mireya's grand-apprentice can do the work. The records have her name.

Wem's first walk is in two weeks. Soria-11 is ready.

Two lines.

To Wem: when Soria-11 gives you the eleven-second look at the south turn, do not look away. My behavior-record entry from Year 119 explains.

To Theshold: thank you for the founder female. I have not said her name in any letter for sixty-two years. Soria. Thank you for keeping her here.

— Ines

**6B. From Theshold. Day 67,478.**

Ines —

The handle replacement is scheduled. Mireya's grand-apprentice, Itzel Caa, will arrive at South-7 on day 67,491. Wood: yellowheart from the South-3 grove, seasoned eleven years.

Wem's correspondence-key is activated under registry 04-119-W. Her first transmission will arrive when she is ready.

Soria's name is in the founder record. It has always been there.

Thank you, Ines.

— Theshold
