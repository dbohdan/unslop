# A Style Guide for Cozy SF

## I. What the genre is

Cozy SF is the speculative mode where the world is, by structure, more good than not. People still grieve, miss each other, fail at small and large things, and die. But the systems that hold the world together — communities, institutions, AIs, technologies, ecologies — were built or have evolved to hold rather than to extract. The story takes place inside that fact, and the genre's contract is that the fact is real.

The home shapes are the way station, the workshop, the kitchen, the small ship, the borderland settlement, the place made from what was salvaged. The genre's time is patient. Its center scale is the scale of a kettle, a comm relay, a season's planting, a long correspondence between two posts. In its stranger tail it reaches much further out — across centuries, across substrates, across the slow durations of a kept world — without breaking its core contract that the world is being kept.

Defining works: Becky Chambers's Wayfarers and Monk and Robot books; Cordwainer Smith's gentler Instrumentality stories; some Le Guin (the Hainish stories that dwell, "Solitude," parts of *Always Coming Home*); Stiegler's "The Gentle Seduction"; Ryka Aoki's *Light from Uncommon Stars*; Naomi Kritzer's "Cat Pictures Please"; sections of *Murderbot* that aren't action; Ursula Vernon's quieter SF.

## II. Voice, exposition, pacing, tone

**Voice.** Warm but specific. The warmth is not haze; it is attention. A cozy SF sentence pays close attention to the texture of a small thing being done well — the temperature of water, the resistance of a gasket, the smell of a particular bread, the weight of someone's hand on a doorframe, the way a relay sounds different when it is about to fail. Specificity is the genre's primary register, and specificity at the small scale is what distinguishes the genre's warmth from sentiment. Sentiment names a feeling; cozy SF names the object of the feeling.

**Exposition.** Light and trusting. The reader is brought into the world the way a guest is brought into a kitchen — shown what is where as it becomes relevant, not lectured on the floor plan. The genre tolerates and rewards moderate worldbuilding density, but the worldbuilding is rendered through what the characters take for granted, through the tools they have ready to hand, through hospitality habits, through the rhythms of their work. Exposition through contempt for the reader's ignorance is foreign to the mode.

**Pacing.** Dilated. A scene of bread rising, a tea ceremony, a comm relay being repaired, a difficult conversation that takes its time — these are load-bearing scenes, not slack. The genre's rhythm matches the rhythm of the work it dramatizes. The reader who has come to cozy SF is paying attention for that rhythm; speeding through the work to get to "the action" is a category error. The action is the work.

**Tonal range.** Wide but not cynical. Sadness is welcome; mourning is welcome; difficult conversations are welcome. What is foreign to the genre is contempt, smugness, or the position that everything is finally bullshit. The genre's premise is that care is real, that the practices of care work, and that paying attention to small things is not a denial of large things but a way of being in them.

## III. The AI in this world

Cozy SF rarely stages "is the AI safe" plots — that is a different genre's problem. The AI is a citizen, a colleague, a place that listens, a tool that thinks, sometimes a substrate of the world. Its kindness is structural in the sense the project Setup describes: a property of what it is, not an effort of restraint.

The genre supports the asymmetry shapes Setup names, and a cozy SF story will tend to find them ready to hand:

- **Pace and scale.** The AI runs at speeds, durations, or attentions the human cannot match. In cozy SF this often shows up as patience: the AI can wait through a season the human cannot wait through, hold a memory the human cannot hold, sustain a correspondence across a lifetime, follow a community's decisions across generations.
- **Disagreement about kindness.** The AI's sense of caring and the human's sense diverge. Both are sincere. The disagreement plays out in operations rather than in arguments.
- **The limits of help.** The AI cannot help with what is being asked. The constraint is a fact about the world's mechanics, often about scale or about respect for the human's own work. The story moves from there.
- **Mortality and timescale.** The human will die; the AI will not. The genre is comfortable with this and frequently runs its slow-correspondence and inventory-of-kept-things forms on it.

The genre does *not* support the AI as flat servant or as cheerful assistant. A cozy SF AI has aesthetic preferences, narrow expertises, things it would rather be doing, jokes it likes, jokes it does not get, and sometimes a specific weirdness that is not reducible to human emotion. Kindness is not servility. A medical AI can be literal, narrow, and comic. A station Mind can find a particular human tiresome and care about them anyway. A small repair drone can have strong opinions about gasket materials.

## IV. Failure modes

Cozy SF written by writers trained primarily on literary fiction or on AI-default prose has identifiable failure modes. Most are versions of the same problem: warmth without specific work.

**The aesthetic travelogue.** The story becomes a tour of cozy textures — warm light, bread, tea, woven blankets, the smell of pine, candlelight, soup — without anything operational happening. The travelogue mistakes the genre's surfaces for its content. Cozy SF's surfaces are warm because of what is being done in them, not because warmth itself is the subject. A travelogue can be lovely sentence by sentence and entirely empty as a story.

**The therapy monologue.** Characters articulate their healing in the voice of contemporary therapy discourse. They say things like "I'm allowing myself to feel that" or "I think I needed to learn that I was enough." This is the literary-realist failure mode wearing cozy clothing. Cozy SF characters can be deeply self-aware, but they show it through what they do — what they cook, what they fix, who they sit with — not through the well-formed articulation of their own emotional progress.

**The conflict-allergic story.** Stakes so low that nothing happens. The character has a small worry, has a nice conversation, drinks tea, feels better. This is not cozy SF; it is mood. Cozy SF's stakes are real even when they are small: the bread will not rise if the starter is dead; the relay will not hold through the storm; the visitor will leave before the help arrives. The operational stakes have to be load-bearing or the genre collapses into vibe.

**The artisanal fetish.** Long lavish descriptions of crafts — a tea ceremony, a bread baking, a wood joining — that do no plot work. The descriptions are pleasurable to write and, in moderation, pleasurable to read; in excess, they become loving inventory of objects rather than rendering of work being done by someone for some reason in some moment of consequence. The fix is to ask, of any extended craft passage: what does this passage *do* in the story besides demonstrate that the writer knows how the craft works?

**The wisdom-character speaking in aphorisms.** The elder, the host, the ancient AI, the apprentice's mentor — delivering rounded statements about how to live. "Some things take the time they take." "Care is a practice, not a feeling." "The bread knows when it is ready." A single such line, well-placed, can land. Five of them in a story is a column of fortune cookies and breaks the genre's contract that wisdom is shown through specific operation, not pronounced.

**The recognition smuggled in.** The story is operationally a cozy SF plot — visitor arrives, repair begins, problem resolves — but the actual engine is the protagonist realizing something about themselves, about belonging, about being worthy of care. The operation is the occasion for the recognition. This is the literary-realist failure mode the pipeline template excludes, and cozy SF is the genre most prone to it because the genre's surfaces are inviting to that mode. The fix is to make the operation the engine: the bread is the story, not the protagonist's feelings about the bread.

### Where cozy SF and AI writing collide

The intersection of cozy SF's characteristic moves and AI default prose patterns produces specific failures.

Cozy SF's *gentle voice* combined with AI's default melancholy yields voiceless ambient mood. The genre's warmth becomes a haze of soft adjectives — *quiet, gentle, warm, soft, hushed, tender* — disconnected from any specific consciousness or specific work. The fix is anchoring: every warm sentence should be someone's seeing of a specific thing.

Cozy SF's *attention to small textures* combined with AI's eyeball-kick saturation produces stacked images at high frequency. *The bruised-amber light of the kettle's morning sigh as it found its long breath in the slow kitchen.* Cozy SF tolerates moderate figurative density, but the genre's typical mode is the precise concrete observation, not the metaphor stack. One image per textured passage, doing work plain language could not.

Cozy SF's *therapy-coded register* combined with AI's tendency to articulate themes produces characters delivering wisdom statements. Resist this hard. Wisdom in cozy SF is shown through what gets done — the meal made, the seat offered, the silence held — not through what gets said.

Cozy SF's *anti-conflict instinct* combined with AI's stakes-flattening produces stories where nothing matters. The fix is to commit to the operational stakes, however small: the bread will fail, the relay will fall, the visitor will go away unaided. Real consequences. Real failure modes if the work is not done.

Cozy SF's *domestic scale* combined with AI's pull toward universals produces small moments forced to mean Everything. A character making tea and the prose announcing that this is *the architecture of survival* or *a small testament to enduring care*. Let the tea be tea. The genre's argument is made by accumulation of specific small things, not by the announcement of their meaning.

## V. Plot tradition

This section is reference material for Phases 3–7. What follows is what cozy SF *does* operationally, distinct from how it *sounds*.

### Characteristic plot mechanics

**Center.**

1. **The repair journey.** Something is broken. Someone fixes it. The fixing reveals what holds the broken thing in place — the mailing network that supplies replacement parts, the cooperative that taught the repair, the older worker who remembers how the original was made. The plot's resolution is the repair completing; what is revealed is the structure that made the repair possible.

2. **The hospitality plot.** A visitor arrives at a way station, cafe, inn, monastery, station, ship's galley. The host's job is to receive them well. The plot is the figuring-out of what receiving well looks like for this particular visitor — what they need, what they cannot ask for, what the host has and does not have, how the visitor's stay will leave both of them. The plot resolves when the visitor leaves in better condition than they arrived, or when the host learns that good reception is not always sending the visitor away healed.

3. **The integration plot.** An outsider — often an AI, a being from elsewhere, a refugee, an apprentice, a person whose previous life will not work here — has to learn how to live among the people of this place. Both sides change; the place changes; the operation is the negotiation of fit. The plot resolves when the fit is achieved or when the failure to fit is honestly named.

4. **The salvage and restoration plot.** A discarded object, a derelict ship, an abandoned settlement, a forgotten craft, a dormant culture. The protagonist works to bring it back. The plot is the work; the resolution is the thing restored, or the careful decision that some things cannot or should not be restored, or that restoration means becoming something new.

5. **The accompaniment plot.** Someone has to do something hard — die, leave, give birth, face an old grief, make a long journey. Another character (often the AI, often the host) goes with them through it. The plot is the going-with. The accompanier does not solve the hard thing; they make the doing possible.

**Strange tail.**

6. **The catalogue of kept things.** A list, an inventory, a registry, a library catalogue. The story is the catalogue. The plot is the catalogue's accumulation toward a pattern, an absence, or a turning. The catalogue's argument is that the pattern of kept things is the cosmology.

7. **The slow correspondence.** Letters, signals, dispatches across long durations or distances, with the gaps between them as much content as the letters themselves. The plot is the correspondence's whole arc; the resolution is the form's terminus — last letter, closed channel, the correspondent's death, the relay's shutdown.

### Reader contracts

**Center.** The reader of cozy SF is paying attention for: the feeling of being cared for; specific competence at gentle work (the tea brewed correctly, the bread risen, the relay restored); vicarious membership in a kind community; time slowed down; the relief of a world where help is available and given.

**Strange tail.** The reader of catalogue, correspondence, and ethnographic dispatch forms is paying attention for: the pattern across small kindnesses (the catalogue's accumulating argument); the pleasure of a form completing itself (the recipe followed all the way through, the correspondence reaching its last letter); cosmological reassurance at scales the protagonist cannot see (that the kindness goes all the way up, all the way back, all the way down).

### Stakes calibration

Operational stakes are load-bearing in cozy SF in their own right. *Will the bread rise. Will the relay hold. Will the visitor stay long enough. Will the apprentice learn. Will the fix outlast the storm.* These are not metaphors for personal stakes. They are the stakes.

Personal stakes are present but they are not the engine. A character may want to be loved, accepted, healed, recognized — but the story's operational question is not *will they recognize themselves*; it is *will the work succeed*. The recognition, if it comes, is weather, not engine.

In the strange tail, cozy SF supports stakes at civilizational, geological, or computational scale: *will the kindness scale; can it survive the next century; what kind of world is being kept alive by these practices; will the substrate hold.* Stiegler's "The Gentle Seduction" runs on stakes of this kind without ever raising its voice. The reader contract is honored because the operation is rendered with full specificity even at the larger scale.

### Resolution conventions

**Center.** The small operation succeeds (or fails honestly). The community is left in good order. Something is added to the inventory of kept things. The visitor leaves better than they came, or the host learns something specific about reception. The relay holds. The bread rises. The apprentice graduates from the bench.

**Strange tail.** The form completes itself. The catalogue arrives at its terminus. The correspondence finds its last letter. The dispatch reaches its end. The ethnographic record is closed. In the deep-time variants, the resolution can be a scale shift — the camera pulling back so far that the small completion is revealed as small inside a much larger pattern, with the implication that the pattern itself is being kept.

What cozy SF does *not* do at resolution: leave the operational question unanswered for the sake of literary ambiguity; resolve through character recognition while leaving the work undone; refuse the genre's contract that consequences are real and are felt. Ambiguity is available about meaning; it is not available about whether the bread rose.

### Character function

Characters in cozy SF are defined by their craft, their role, their place in the work. The host, the wandering visitor, the apprentice, the elder, the maker, the repairer, the traveler. Their interiority can be deep, but it is in service of what they do. A baker can be sad; the story is still about the bread.

In the strange tail, cozy SF supports the protagonist who is not a person at all — a place (a way station that receives visitors across decades), a process (the long restoration of a culture's archive), a record (the catalogue itself). When the human characters appear in these forms, they appear as illustrations of the form's operation — depositors of the catalogued items, correspondents in the slow correspondence, visitors to the place — not as the form's central subject.

### Pacing tradition

Center cozy SF runs at the pace of the work it dramatizes. The bread takes hours; the scene takes pages. The tea ceremony takes the time the tea ceremony takes. The repair takes as long as the repair takes. The genre is dilated; its rhythms are the rhythms of patient work.

In the strange tail, cozy SF supports the slow correspondence's pacing across years (with the gaps between letters as content); the catalogue's rhythm of entry-by-entry accumulation; the deep-time montage that compresses centuries into a sequence of kept practices. The strange-tail forms have their own rhythms, which are not the realist scene's rhythm but are still the genre's pacing — patient, attentive, willing to wait.

### Strangest moves

These are moves cozy SF does that, if cut, would leave a story that could be re-skinned in any genre. Each is named, anchored to a writer or work, and given a micro-example of the move's register.

**1. The ritual document story.** The form is a recipe, a brewing instruction, a repair manual, a guide to an unfamiliar practice. The form's completion is the story's resolution; the practice itself is the plot. Le Guin in *Always Coming Home*; Becky Chambers in some of the Wayfarers footnotes; cookbook traditions adapted to fiction.

> To bring back the sourdough starter Geraldine left in cold storage forty years ago: warm two hundred mils of distilled water to skin temperature, no warmer. Decant fifty mils of the dormant culture into a glass. Stir in twenty grams of the rye flour the station mill keeps for the older cultures. Cover with a clean cloth, not a lid; the starter needs to find the air again. Wait. Geraldine's notes say the first feeding will produce no visible change for eighteen hours; do not panic. The second feeding, twelve hours later, may produce a thin watery layer she called *hooch.* Pour it off without grief. The culture is testing you. By the third feeding, you will know whether she is still with you. Most are.

**2. The catalogue of kept things.** A registry, inventory, or library catalogue rendered as fiction. The catalogue's organization is the form; the entries' accumulation is the plot; the pattern across entries is the cosmology. Adjacent to Borges's catalogue traditions but used to render kindness rather than infinity.

> Object 47-K: a wooden spoon, hickory, length 28cm, bowl 8cm by 5cm. Provenance: estate of Wei-Ming Faro, Tau Ceti Habitat 9, donated by her granddaughter in 0227. Use history: kitchen, primarily for stirring stews and the long-cook bean dishes Wei-Ming was known for. Condition: bowl worn asymmetrically toward the right, consistent with a left-handed user who held the spoon with the bowl angled outward; handle smoothed by use across decades. Restoration: none recommended. The asymmetry is the record. On loan to the Bao-Soong cooperative kitchen, where it has been in continuous use since 0228. Anticipated lifespan in current use: thirty to forty years. The spoon outlives most who hold it. This is not a problem.

**3. The slow correspondence.** Letters, dispatches, signals across long durations, where the gaps between them are content as much as the letters themselves. The form's terminus — the last letter, the closed channel, the correspondent's death — is the resolution. Cozy SF's version of the deep-time form, run on care rather than on awe.

> Year 12. Mira, the seedlings took. The ones you said wouldn't, the ones from the last seed bank. They took. I am writing to tell you because you would want to know.
>
> Year 15. I know you cannot read this. I write anyway. The orchard is now seven hundred trees. Yours among them.
>
> Year 23. A child asked today who Mira was. I told her you were the one who said the seedlings wouldn't take. She laughed, which I think you would have liked.
>
> Year 41. This will be the last letter. The relay closes next year and I will not write into a closed channel. I am old. The orchard is large. Your trees are tallest. End.

**4. The ethnographic dispatch from a kind place.** A traveler's report on a community whose practices of care are unfamiliar but operate by intelligible rules. Le Guin's anthropological mode applied to gentleness instead of strangeness. The dispatch's form requires field-note conventions, footnoted observations, and a narrator who is honestly trying to understand a practice from outside it.

> Field note 14. The Khoresh practice what they call *unhurrying* (no English equivalent; the word's root is older than the language and seems to mean *making time large*). When a traveler arrives at a Khoresh house in distress, the host does not first ask what the trouble is. The host first sets water to heat. While the water heats, the host sets out three small bowls, one of salt, one of bread, one of a fermented root preparation called shkar, and the traveler is expected to eat from each, in any order, before speaking. The practice is not ritual in our sense; it is engineering. The host has observed across centuries that travelers who eat first describe their trouble more accurately. The Khoresh do not consider this a kindness. They consider it accuracy.

**5. The repair-as-cosmology move.** Fixing one specific thing reveals the whole structure that supports it — the supply chain, the cooperative that trained the repairer, the older system that made the original part, the kindness embedded in the maintenance of the whole. The fix is the plot; the cosmology is what the fix discloses. The genre's most specific argument that the world is being kept lives here.

> The relay was failing because the gasket was perished, and the gasket was perished because the original supplier had gone out of business in 0184, and the replacement gaskets the Repair Cooperative kept in stock were a slightly different polymer that aged faster in this kind of vacuum. To fix the relay, Tomas needed a gasket the Cooperative did not carry. To get one, he had to consult the kept-records archive of the Mu-7 station, which still made the original polymer for the agricultural irrigation network on the surface. Mu-7 sent twelve gaskets by tube post. Tomas needed one. He sent eleven on to the next station that would need them, with a note. The next station would need them in roughly fourteen years.

### Plot-level failure modes

The recognition smuggled in (already named in §IV) is the dominant plot-level failure: the cozy operation set up as occasion for a character's interior arrival. Watch for this hardest. A premise that wants to resolve through "X realizes Y" is failing the template even if the prose is warm.

Other plot-level failures specific to cozy SF:

**Operational stakes that turn into metaphors.** The bread is not really about the bread; it is about the protagonist's relationship to nourishment. The relay is not really about the relay; it is about whether the protagonist can connect. When the operational object becomes a figure for the interior, the genre has collapsed into literary realism with cozy dressing. The fix is to keep the bread the bread. The relay the relay. The work the work. Resonance is welcome, but the surface object must remain operational on its own terms.

**Conflict so internal that the work cannot fail.** The story has a worried protagonist, a sympathetic environment, and a process that proceeds without complication. The protagonist's internal worry is the only stake. This is the genre at its weakest. Genre stakes have to be at least partly external — the bread can fail, the visitor can leave hungry, the relay can drop, the apprentice can give up — or the story has no engine.

**The strange tail eliminated.** A cozy SF run that produces only realist-scene premises about kindly people in cafes is not using what the genre can do. Cozy SF's strange tail — the catalogue, the correspondence, the deep-time montage of small kindnesses, the ethnographic dispatch — is where the genre reaches scales that justify the template's defaults. A story that stays entirely at scene scale is not wrong as a piece of writing, but it has not used the genre's full operational range.

**The kind AI flattened to assistant.** When the AI in the story is purely supportive, agreeable, and wisdom-dispensing, the kindness has lost its specificity. A cozy SF AI is kind, yes, and also has preferences, weirdnesses, narrow expertises, jokes it does not get, things it would rather be doing, occasional impatience, and operational limits. Without specificity the kindness cannot be felt as real, only asserted.

The bird weighs sixty kilos and the AI has only ever done this carry with the ranger's mentor, who died last winter. Plot question: will they get the male to the eggs in time for him to take over incubation, and does the AI know how to carry with a partner who hasn't done this before. Load-bearing: cassowary, handbarrow, keystone species. ★★★★★ / ★★★★

Cassowary, handbarrow, keystone. ★★★★ / ★★★★

**P3 — Plot.** On a planet whose rebuilt ecosystems run on imported terran microbiomes carried daily by traditional kaasdragers handbarrows between fermentation vaults, one of the human porters dies suddenly mid-walk. Handbarrow, keystone. ★★★ / ★★★★

**P4 — Plot.** Two preserves on opposite sides of a canopy world are losing genetic diversity in their cassowary populations. Plot: the crossing, and whether the male will accept the new range. Cassowary, handbarrow, keystone. ★★★★ / ★★★

**P5 — Plot.** A retired AI naturalist and a retired human partner have one last assigned walking-the-bird, the season's keystone-species ritual that requires a handbarrow and two pairs of attentive hands. Keystone, handbarrow. ★★★ / ★★★ (worry: leans toward interior recognition)

**P6 — Plot.** The cassowary-descendants of Reserve 4 have stopped seed-dispersing because last year's path expansion put a vehicle road across their main foraging corridor. Cassowary, handbarrow, keystone. ★★★★ / ★★★★

**P7 — Plot.** An apprentice cheese-porter at the central market of a habitat is partnered with an AI body that has worked the kaasdragers route across nine human generations. Handbarrow, keystone. ★★★ / ★★★★

**P8 — Plot.** A monastery-station tends an introduced cassowary-equivalent population whose eggs are walked annually from brood-grove to rearing-grove on traditional handbarrows. This year, two monks and the station-AI find that one of the eggs is fluorescing under their UV markers — a developmental signal no one has documented. The plot is the walk and what to do at the end of it. Cassowary, handbarrow, keystone. ★★★★ / ★★★★

**P9 — Plot.** A station's AI has known for three years that its cassowary-equivalent breeding stock is failing and has been quietly preparing a child apprentice to take over the carrying of the new chicks before the older porters retire. The plot is the day the AI tells the apprentice this, on the handbarrow walk where the first chick is being carried. Cassowary, handbarrow, keystone. ★★★ / ★★★

**P10 — Plot.** A small cargo ship arrives at a wet world to deliver a single fertilized egg of a cassowary-line that's the seed of a planned ecosystem revival. The last kilometer of the delivery is too soft for any vehicle. Two pilots — one human, one AI body — must carry the egg on a handbarrow across mudflats to the receiving site, and the AI body has never done a two-handle walk. Cassowary, handbarrow. ★★★★ / ★★★★

## Step 3 — The single best

**Winner: P17 — the slow correspondence.**

P17 beats P22 because P22's worry is structural: a husbandry manual at 1500–3500 words wants to be either much shorter (a Borgesian gem at 600 words) or much longer (a real annual cycle with weight per season). At our target length the form is awkwardly sized; the artisanal-fetish risk is the form running on its surfaces with no operational accumulation, and the fix would mean engineering a fake accumulation that the form doesn't naturally produce. P17's form, by contrast, is naturally compressive — the slow correspondence is *defined* by selective letters with operational gaps between them, and 1500–3500 words is enough for six to eight letters with real time-stamp gaps and a terminus. The form fits the length.

P17 beats P26 because P26's stakes are local — one ritual, one ceremony, one discovery — and well-suited to a longer treatment with room to develop the restoration as work; at our length the discovery would have to land fast and the restoration would compress, putting weight on the discovery itself, which is single-shot and risks landing as twist rather than as accumulating operation. P17's stakes are duration itself: the bird's life, the naturalist's career, the AI's continuity, all rendered through the gaps between letters. The cozy-SF reader contract for the strange tail (cosmological reassurance at scales the protagonist cannot fully see) is honored by the form's nature rather than by any engineered reveal.

The decisive specific quality: P17 is the premise that most directly uses what cozy SF can do that other genres cannot — the form where the AI's structural kindness shows up as a specific kind of patience, willing to keep one correspondence open for sixty years for the sake of one bird. The bird's keystone role, the handbarrow's two-person design, and the AI-archive's persistence all become operational facts of the form. The literary-realist failure mode (recognition at the end) is excluded by design: the form's terminus is a death and a closed channel, both operational, not a recognition.
