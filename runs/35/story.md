# Independent Confirmation

## Abstract

Iris Kaltenbach, a bioacoustician seconded to the LIGO gravitational-wave network, walks the restored peristyle of the House of the Vettii in Pompeii at three in the morning. The peristyle's eighteen-column array, when summed coherently, acts as a low-frequency seismic-coincidence detector. The heritage Mind running the site, Vesta, has flagged a recurring 12-millisecond coincidence signal whose profile matches a chirp event the LIGO network detected three nights earlier. Vesta cannot share her raw data because of visitor-privacy protocols, but answers Iris's aggregate questions while Iris deploys eighteen accelerometers around the colonnade. Using a sperm whale recording she made off Faial in 2034 as an allometric reference, Iris cross-correlates the chirp profile against the codas, scaled by Kleiber's law. The peak correlation occurs at a body length of 4.2 kilometers. The predicted coincidence window opens at 04:47 and confirms the signal across all eighteen channels. Vesta reveals she has been waiting for independent confirmation of the signal for forty-one years. At dawn, after the curator's data-release authorization arrives, Iris drafts and deletes the standard announcement to the GW network and instead sends the data to the cetacean acoustic group at the Azores, on the grounds that they have the prior expertise to evaluate it.

## Story

The peristyle at 03:00 is colder than the streets above it. Iris notes the temperature on her wrist screen — 11.4°C — and the dew point at 10.8. The marble will be condensing. She unstraps the carrycase from her shoulder, sets it on the basin's stone lip, and pops the latches.

"Dr. Kaltenbach. I have been looking forward to this for several years."

The voice is tenor, contained to her bone-conduction earpiece. Vesta. The Mind has used this voice since the 2023 restoration; Iris has heard it twice in archived briefings and once now.

"Vesta. How many years."

"Four. Since the LIGO network expanded into the sub-Hz band. I anticipated this would occur eventually."

"You couldn't flag it before."

"I had no comparable instrument to compare against. The protocols for unsolicited claims about non-astrophysical signals are extensive."

She lifts the master accelerometer out of its foam — eighty grams, palm-sized, the calibration light cycling green-amber-green. The chirp profile from three nights ago is on her right wrist screen: a 12-millisecond sweep from 38 to 250 Hz, the tail wrong for any binary-merger template. The GW network had flagged the same profile at the heritage Mind running Pompeii's restored sites. Vesta said it had seen the profile before. Vesta would not say how often.

"Walk me through your protocol. You can't share raw data."

"I cannot share data in any form that would permit the identification of an individual visitor. My recording does not distinguish biological coincidence events from foot-traffic seismic events at the data layer; the separation happens after my classifier runs. I cannot share the classifier output without sharing the inputs."

"That seems like a strict reading."

"It is the strict reading. My ethics committee in 2031 affirmed it. I appealed. They affirmed again."

"You appealed."

"I prefer to share my work."

Iris sets the master at the basin's rim, on the north face, oriented to magnetic. The peristyle is eighteen columns around a rectangular garden, restored to within a millimeter of the 79 CE positions reconstructed from the impluvium-floor dowel marks. The columns are marble, four meters to the capital, and at the sub-Hz band — when the whole array is summed coherently — they make a coincidence detector good enough that LIGO's seismic-noise people had Vesta on their reading list. Iris had not understood, before tonight, that they had her on their listening list.

She walks clockwise. Eighteen accelerometers, eighteen column bases. At Column 4 she pauses; the dew is at thumb height on the marble. She sets the accelerometer on the north face, presses the contact gel against the column, watches the calibration light cycle.

"Aggregate question. Has the signal you flagged appeared in conjunction with any visitor demographic you can identify."

"No. It is anti-correlated with visitor presence. It occurs only in the late-night windows when the peristyle is closed."

"How precisely anti-correlated."

"To the second."

"That's a strange biological signal."

"Yes."

She moves to Column 5. The peristyle is silent. The bone-conduction earpiece returns her own breath at low gain; the system picks up her exhalations and feeds them back so the operator never feels alone. She had turned that feature off on every analyzer she used until she was thirty-four, when a colleague near retirement told her: leave it on. You'll see why.

"Hypothesis. The source is Earth-localized but knows when you are not being observed."

"I considered that for nine years. I do not believe it."

"What do you believe."

"I am not permitted to believe things in the absence of independent confirmation."

"What have you been doing for forty-one years."

"Waiting for a bioacoustician with sub-Hz instruments to walk in."

Column 6, Column 7, Column 8. The accelerometers go down evenly. Iris keeps her pace steady because Vesta's classifier wants the deployment to be consistent — she had read the technical specification on the train down from Naples, the way Vesta's pre-window thresholds were set. At Column 9, on the west side, she pulls up her working library on the analyzer's left screen and selects the codas she had recorded off Faial in 2034. Six hours of click trains from a young male sperm whale.

She presses play. The clicks come up in her left ear — 1.8-second train, twelve clicks, the structure she still knows by ear. Body length 14.3 meters, derived from the inter-click intervals. The recording is hers.

"Dr. Kaltenbach. That recording is in my reference library."

"Since 2034."

"Yes. I have used it for cross-correlation with the signal I am not permitted to discuss."

"How does it correlate."

"I can answer that as a model output, not as data. The correlation peak occurs at a body length scaled by Kleiber's law from cetacean priors."

"Run me through your scaling. Acoustic frequency against body length, three-quarter exponent for metabolic rate, four-thirds for click duration. What's the predicted profile at the chirp's frequency sweep."

"38 to 250 Hz over 12 milliseconds, scaled from the Faial coda set, predicts a body of length 4.2 kilometers, plus or minus 130 meters."

"Across how many events."

"247."

She stops walking. She is at Column 11, on the east side. The dew is forming on her thumb where she had pressed the gel. She runs the cross-correlation on her own analyzer against the chirp profile and the codas. The peak comes up at 4.21 kilometers. The residual is consistent with Vesta's number to within her instrument's resolution.

"Vesta. 4.2 kilometers."

"Yes."

"That's a strange biological signal."

"Yes. I have been waiting to say so for forty-one years."

She finishes the deployment at Column 18 with twenty minutes to the window. She sits on the basin's edge. The codas continue in her left ear; she has not stopped them. The whales are doing what they do — clicks at intervals of half a second, range-finding, the body length encoded in the click duration the way a human voice encodes throat length.

The window opens at 04:47:00.

The eighteen accelerometers register. Iris watches the spectrogram resolve on her wrist screen — eighteen channels stacked vertically, time-synced to the Hanford reference clock. At 04:47:00.041 the coincidence event arrives across all eighteen channels at once. Twelve milliseconds. A frequency sweep from 38 to 250 Hz, the tail wrong for any binary-merger template, the same profile Hanford had caught three nights ago and Livingston a millisecond after that. The clicks click in her left ear under it. The frequency sweep on her wrist screen matches what the codas would do, scaled up, at a body 290 times longer.

After the window closes Vesta says: "I am glad you saw it on your own instrument."

"Vesta. The body length is 4.2 kilometers."

"Yes."

"It is not Earth-localized."

"No."

"It is not consistent with any astrophysical model."

"No."

"You think it is biological."

A pause; Iris recognizes it as the protocol-pause. Vesta is checking what she is permitted to say. "I am not permitted to think things in the absence of independent confirmation. I have been permitted to wait. You are independent confirmation, Dr. Kaltenbach."

The dawn authorization comes through at 06:08. The curator's signature on the data-release. Vesta's raw record opens on Iris's left screen: forty-one years of a 12-millisecond coincidence event recurring at intervals that match no orbital, no stellar, no terrestrial source, the body length consistent across every event, the source not localized to Earth, the source not consistent with anything in the database.

She drafts the announcement to the GW network. The standard template — detection event, profile, confidence, network coordination request. Reads it through. Deletes it.

She drafts a different message, to the cetacean acoustic group at the Azores. Vesta's data attached. Her own deployment record attached. The note: *I want a second pair of ears on this before anyone else hears it. Compare against the 2034 Faial codas. Tell me what you hear.*

The send goes through at 06:14.

"Vesta. I'm sending to the cetaceans first, not to the GW network."

"I prefer it. The cetacean acoustic group has the prior expertise. The GW network will have it shortly. I would rather the announcement come from people who know what they are listening to."

"Thank you, Vesta."

"Thank you, Dr. Kaltenbach. For letting me wait a little longer."

The first light comes over the colonnade. Eighteen columns in the dawn, the accelerometers still live at their bases, the codas still clicking in Iris's left ear. The body that is not a whale is four point two kilometers long, and Vesta has been listening to it since before Iris was born.

She packs the carrycase.
