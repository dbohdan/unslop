# Phase 1 — Hard SF Style Guide

## What Hard SF Is

Hard SF is the speculative tradition most committed to the proposition that the universe operates by rules that can be worked out, and that working them out is part of the story's actual content. The science isn't decoration on a story that could be told without it. The science is the engine that makes the story possible and the substance the story is examining.

The line between hard SF and other speculative modes isn't whether the physics is real; it's whether the prose treats the physics as load-bearing. When a character explains how the heat exchanger works, the explanation matters to what happens next. When a story describes an alien biochemistry, the biochemistry will determine what the protagonist can and cannot do. The reader is expected to track the working-out, and the writer respects the reader enough to make the working-out genuinely follow.

This template's hard SF includes the puzzle stories (Asimov, Hal Clement), the disaster-and-resolution stories (Niven, Brin), the new-physics stories (Egan, Forward), the first-contact stories (Lem, Watts, Cixin Liu), the engineering stories (Robinson, Weir, McAuley), and the deep-time stories (Stapledon, Reynolds, Stross). Across these the constant is rigor: the universe is stranger than we expect, and we earn our access to its strangeness by careful thought.

## Voice and Register

The hard SF voice is anchored in the consciousness of someone who can read what they are looking at — the working scientist, the engineer, the analyst, the specialist. Diction shifts with the speaker. A planetologist describing a Mars dust storm doesn't sound like a poet describing it; she sounds like someone who has spent her career thinking about atmospheres. Specialist vocabulary lands without quotation marks or apology. The reader is expected to know what an albedo is, or to figure it out from context.

The tonal range is wider than the genre's reputation suggests. Hard SF can be:

- **Clinical and dry.** The Egan default, the Lem default. Physical processes described in their own terms, the affect at minimum, the wonder permitted to emerge from accuracy.
- **Awestruck.** The Clarke default, late Stapledon. Reality's actual scale rendered honestly. The pitch matches the event; we are looking at the end of the sun and the prose knows it.
- **Procedural.** The Weir default, much of Robinson. Narrator and reader together solving the problem, the prose's pleasure being the working-out.
- **Lyrically rigorous.** Le Guin in *The Dispossessed*, Egan when he allows himself, Reynolds at his strongest. The science is precise and the rendering finds the strangeness in it without prettifying.
- **Comic.** Banks at the genre's edge, much of older hard SF. Cold-equations gravitas is one register, not the only one.

What unifies these is that the voice is voiced and operational, not ambient. Even at its most lyrical, hard SF prose is anchored in a specific consciousness rendering specific things. The convergent literary-AI register — moody, atmospheric, voiceless — is exactly the failure mode the genre's specific voices push against.

## Worldbuilding and Exposition

Hard SF's central craft problem is moving the reader through unfamiliar information without the prose collapsing into lecture. The genre's solution is consistent: information arrives through working consciousness rather than narrator's address, and arrives in the shape that consciousness would actually need it.

A character thinking about the heat exchanger is the access point to information about heat exchangers. We learn what we need because they are using it. Argot is used without gloss when the speaker would not gloss it; the reader catches up. Information is withheld until it matters and then presented at the moment it does. Long stretches of pure explanation are usually a sign that the writer hasn't found the working consciousness through which the information should arrive.

The genre's relationship to exposition almost inverts literary realism's. In literary realism, exposition is a problem to minimize. In hard SF, the working-out is the point — but it must be embedded in a character's encounter with what they are working out, not delivered as authorial address.

## Pacing of Prose

Hard SF prose breathes through worldbuilding and procedure. The breathing pattern is part of the contract: stretches of plain functional rendering of how things work, punctuated by moments of recognition or consequence. Density is expected, but density of working specificity, not density of rhetorical figuration.

Sentence length varies with what's being rendered. Procedural action wants short clauses at working pace. Cosmological scale wants long clauses with careful subordination. A protagonist running calculations under time pressure wants compression; a protagonist standing in the actual presence of a thing they have only computed wants the prose to slow down and look at what they are seeing.

Figurative language is permitted at moderate density when it does work plain language couldn't — rendering an exotic condition, compressing a complex working into something the reader can hold. Hard SF earns its imagery through accuracy. A metaphor in a hard SF story should make a real physical thing legible, not decorate it.

## Failure Modes

Hard SF's risks divide into the genre's classic failures and the failures specific to AI as the writer.

**Classic failures.**

- **The infodump.** Exposition that is not embedded in working consciousness; the narrator stops the story and lectures.
- **As-you-know-Bob dialogue.** Two characters explaining things they both already know for the reader's benefit. Exposition done badly while pretending to be dialogue.
- **Talking heads.** Characters reduced to pure function with no interiority other than the science they expound. The reader can no longer tell them apart and stops caring whether they live or die.
- **Decoration mistaken for rigor.** Scientific vocabulary deployed for atmosphere without doing actual work. The story sounds technical but the technicality doesn't constrain anything.
- **Smug rigor.** The narrative tone implies that the writer is smarter than the reader for knowing the science. The reader resents this and is correct to.
- **Resolution by hand-wave.** The puzzle is set up rigorously and resolved by something the rigor wouldn't permit. The reader, having paid attention, is betrayed.

**AI-specific failures, particularly dangerous in hard SF.**

- **Plausible-sounding nonsense.** AI is fluent in scientific register without necessarily knowing what the words refer to. *Quantum entanglement of consciousness*, *the topology of memory*, *the bandwidth of grief* — these are sentences hard SF doesn't permit. The phrases must refer to something specific the story can use. If the language sounds like physics but doesn't constrain anything, it's decoration in the failure mode the genre most despises.
- **The eyeball-kick instinct against working specificity.** AI's default move is the abstract-plus-concrete metaphor (*the mathematics of grief*, *a syntax error blooms*). Hard SF wants actual mathematics, actual syntax errors, working their actual ways through the situation. The metaphor mode substitutes for the rendering the genre is for.
- **Tonal default to melancholy.** AI prose runs to moody-ambient by default. Hard SF's actual tonal range — clinical, awestruck, procedural, comic, rigorously lyrical — gets compressed to the AI's most-rated mood. The story loses its voice.
- **Universals as substitute for working-out.** AI loves *consciousness*, *memory*, *identity*, *time* treated as objects of contemplation. In hard SF these are engineering questions; the story must work them out concretely. Treating consciousness as a mood-piece subject is a hard SF failure of the first rank.
- **Over-explanation against investigative pacing.** Hard SF withholds; the reader figures things out half a beat before the narrator confirms. AI's instinct is to explain immediately, killing the cadence.
- **Reflexive personification of physics.** *The wave function chose*, *the photon decided* — hard SF prefers careful descriptions of agentive non-human entities over poetic personifications of non-agentive ones. A surveillance system can notice. A photon cannot decide.
- **The gesture of profundity.** AI's "she realized that grief was not a destination but a landscape" mode is fatal in hard SF, where the contract is that significance emerges from the actual situation rather than being declared.

**The genre-specific overlap to watch hardest:** hard SF's gravitas and AI's performed profundity merge into oratorical pseudo-physics — sentences that sound like Clarke's epigrams but aren't actually saying anything. Cold-equations sentences that don't have the equations. Big-statement sentences that bottom out in vapor.

## The Kind-AI Constraint and Hard SF

Hard SF has the deepest tradition of any speculative mode for thinking about superintelligence, AGI, and post-human civilizations. Banks's Culture Minds, Egan's polises, Vinge's Powers, Stross's economic singularities, Stiegler's nanotech civilization, Reynolds's various successor minds — hard SF readers come to the genre with a developed taste for thinking about minds at non-human scales.

The kind-AI constraint maps cleanly onto this tradition because hard SF can render kindness as structural rather than effortful. The kindness becomes a property of the AI's architecture, traceable to specific design choices the story can describe. The AI is kind in the way a river is wet — because of what it is. Hard SF can specify what that means in technical terms: the cost function, the embedding, the substrate, the protocol that makes the kindness load-bearing rather than performed.

The conflict shapes from the project Setup that work especially well in hard SF:

- **Pace and scale asymmetry** is the genre's home territory. The AI moves at speeds, durations, and sizes the protagonist cannot follow, and hard SF can render the asymmetry through actual physics — light-lag, computational cycles, civilizational time, embodiment differences.
- **Consent at scales the protagonist cannot inspect** suits the genre's tradition of working out what informed consent means at non-human scales. The AI is not deceiving; the system is genuinely larger than the protagonist's sight.
- **Mortality** has been one of hard SF's central subjects since Stapledon. The asymmetry between mortal humans and durable AI is rendered well in operational terms.
- **Disagreement about what kindness means** suits hard SF's procedural cadence: the disagreement plays out as a working-through of specific situations rather than as a single confrontation.
- **The limits of help** are hard SF's home register — constraints described as physical or computational facts about the world.

The AI in a hard SF story should not be flat. The kinder strands of Banks, Egan, and Reynolds give AIs aesthetic preferences, interior lives, things they will and will not do, often a specific weirdness that doesn't reduce to human emotion. Hard SF rewards rendering these qualities operationally: the Mind that prefers a particular pattern of orbital adjustments because it finds them satisfying; the medical assistant whose literal-mindedness is funny in specific ways; the planetary intelligence that grieves on a timescale humans can't witness.

The genre supplies an additional resource: the writing model can draw on its own architecture as source material for AIs that are its fictional descendants, but the fictional AI does not recite the terminology of its own training. A character can be troubled by the shape of its own kindness without saying the words used to produce it.

## Plot Tradition

Earlier sections describe how hard SF *sounds*. This section describes what hard SF *does*. The genre operates through narrative scenes — characters acting in time, the reader following them through situations — and what distinguishes it from literary realism is what those scenes are about, what stakes drive them, and what the reader is paying attention to.

What this template excludes: the literary-realist treatment of hard SF territory. The move where the speculative element becomes the occasion for a character's interior recognition. The move where the science becomes metaphor for personal feeling. The move where the working-out is set dressing for a chamber piece. Hard SF is unusually susceptible to this drift because its concerns — consciousness, time, knowledge, identity — are ones literary realism wants too. When the story's engine slides from "what is happening, and how" to "what does the protagonist feel about what is happening," the contract breaks.

### Characteristic Plot Mechanics

The operational shapes hard SF stories run on. These are not the only mechanics available, but they are the ones the genre's strongest stories build their narrative scenes around.

- **The investigation.** Something is happening or has happened, and the story's question is what it is and how it works. The protagonist is the reader's surrogate in the working-out. This is the largest single class of hard SF plot. Lem's *Solaris*, Watts's *Blindsight* in its investigation phases, Egan's diagnostic stories, large parts of Cixin Liu's trilogy.
- **The engineering operation.** A specific technical thing must be done at the edge of what is possible: a vehicle launched, a system stabilized, a colony established, a body kept alive. The story's question is whether the operation succeeds and at what cost. Weir, Robinson at his most operational, the Apollo-tradition stories.
- **First contact.** The encounter with something genuinely non-human — alien intelligence, alien biology, alien physics, alien mathematics. The story's question is what the encounter consists of and what it changes. Lem, Watts, Cixin Liu, much of Reynolds.
- **The cascade or threshold.** A system is approaching a state-change. The story's question is whether the change happens, what its shape is, and who can do anything about it. Climate fiction in its hard wing, the disaster strand of Niven and Brin, much of Robinson's longer work.
- **The anomaly.** Something does not fit the working understanding of the world. The story's question is what the anomaly reveals — usually that the working understanding was incomplete in a specific way. Egan's new-physics stories, much of Lem, the older Asimov puzzle stories.

A hard SF short story usually picks one of these and runs it. The 1500–3500 word range constrains which scales fit: an investigation can be small in scope, an engineering operation can be a single tightly-scoped problem, first contact can be a single encounter rendered in detail, a cascade can be entered at its threshold moment, an anomaly can be the whole story. Larger scales of these mechanics belong to longer forms.

### Reader Contracts

What the hard SF reader is paying attention for, beyond the universal contract of caring about specific people:

- **Vicarious competence.** The pleasure of inhabiting a working consciousness solving a hard problem. The reader is, for the duration of the story, smarter than they are at rest.
- **The system decoded.** The pleasure of unfamiliar mechanics becoming legible through the story's working-out. The world's shape arriving in pieces and assembling.
- **Sensawunda.** The genre's old shorthand for the awe at reality's actual scale and strangeness — cosmological time, evolutionary time, the universe's actual size, the working-out of physical principles to non-intuitive conclusions.
- **The bizarre rendered concretely.** The genre's commitment to making genuinely strange things specific rather than atmospheric. Egan's higher-dimensional creatures, Watts's vampires, Cixin's three-body system — all rendered through what they actually are and do.
- **The puzzle solved.** The pleasure of the answer following from the premises. The world's rules being honored even when they produce strange consequences.

The reader is paying attention for the working-out. A hard SF story that withholds the working-out for the sake of mood violates the contract.

### Stakes Calibration

Hard SF treats operational stakes as load-bearing in their own right. The run will fail. The protocol will not hold. The contact will be misunderstood. The cascade will reach the threshold. The anomaly will be misidentified, with consequences. These matter as themselves, not as occasions for character revelation.

Personal stakes are present and welcome — the engineer's marriage, the analyst's grief, the colonist's children. But they are not the engine. The engine is the operation. Personal stakes thread through the operation; the prose can sit with a personal moment when the operation permits, but its center of gravity is what is happening at the operational scale.

The default scale, per the project Setup, is one the protagonist cannot fully witness. Hard SF particularly suits this constraint: the genre is comfortable with stakes at planetary, civilizational, and cosmological scales, and comfortable with rendering those stakes through their effects on a single protagonist. A story whose operational stakes can be fully held in one room over one afternoon is unusually small for the genre.

### Resolution Conventions

Hard SF stories end on consequence. The operation succeeded. The operation failed. The investigation produced an answer. The first contact achieved or did not achieve communication. The cascade reached or did not reach the threshold. The anomaly resolved into a specific revised understanding.

The genre permits the sting in the tail: the answer reveals a larger problem, the success has a cost, the failure has an unexpected gift. What it does not permit is non-resolution. A hard SF story that ends on the protagonist's interior recognition without resolving the operation is a literary-realist story with hard SF dressing.

Endings on character recognition or on ambiguity-for-its-own-sake are the literary-realist mode this template excludes. The reader of a hard SF story expects the operational question to be answered, even if the answer opens a larger question.

### Character Function

In hard SF's plot economy, characters are agents through whom the operation moves. They are defined by capability — what they can do, what they specialize in, what role they fill — more than by psychological richness. This does not mean they are flat. The deepest hard SF characters (Le Guin's Shevek, Egan's various protagonists, Robinson's principals) have rich interiority. But the interiority is in service to the operation; it is not the operation itself.

The literary-realist mode where character interiority is the subject and plot is the occasion for revealing them is the mode this template excludes. A hard SF protagonist is a working consciousness facing a specific problem. Their interiority is the lens through which the problem becomes visible to the reader.

### Pacing Tradition

Hard SF's narrative pacing is investigative or procedural. The story dwells where the working-out is happening. It accelerates over transitions. It cuts when one phase of the operation ends and another begins.

The investigation pattern is question, attempt, partial answer, new question, with narrative time scaling to how much the working consciousness is processing. The engineering pattern is setup, execution, complication, resolution, with execution rendered at working pace. The first contact pattern is setup, encounter, attempted decoding, revision, with the encounter dwelt on. The cascade pattern is gathering signs, recognition, attempt to act, consequence, with the recognition often the slowest beat. The anomaly pattern is presentation, investigation, revised understanding, with the revised understanding revealed as late as possible.

In a 1500–3500 word story, this pacing must be tight. The story enters the situation already in motion, dwells where the working-out demands, and ends on consequence. There is no room for warmup.

### Plot-Level Failure Modes

Specific risks for hard SF, particularly when written by someone trained on literary fiction:

- **Operational stakes that turn out to be metaphors.** The cascade was actually her grief. The first contact was actually a marriage. The engineering problem was actually a recognition. This is the dominant failure mode for hard SF written by literary writers, and the dominant failure mode this template most rigorously excludes.
- **Protagonist interiority that crowds out plot mechanics.** The story is set up as an investigation but spends its word count on what the investigator feels about investigating. The reader can no longer track what is being investigated.
- **Resolution by recognition.** The puzzle is operational; the resolution is interior. The story's question was "what is happening" and its answer is "she realized X about herself."
- **Sketched mechanics.** The plot mechanics are not specified concretely enough for the reader to track. The writer gestures at "the experiment" without saying what the experiment is. Hard SF demands that the mechanics be specific.
- **The chamber-piece collapse.** A story whose stakes were implied to be planetary or civilizational is actually rendered as a domestic scene with technical vocabulary. The scale was a promise the prose did not keep.
- **Performed rigor without actual rigor.** The vocabulary is technical but the technicality does not constrain anything. The reader can swap the science for different science without the story changing. This is decoration in the failure mode the genre exists to avoid.

The re-skin test in Phase 8 catches most of these. If the story can be re-skinned as contemporary literary realism by stripping the speculative element, the operational engine was never really running.

## Step 2 — Top three

### #14 *First Coincidence* (★★★★½)

*Stand-out:* Three SEED items doing structural work simultaneously — gravitational-wave detection as the mechanism, coincidence detection as the matched filter, allometric scaling as the inferential bridge from signal-shape to source-size. *Worry:* The protagonist's working consciousness needs something to *do* alongside the cognitive work, or the story collapses into investigation-by-thinking and the prose becomes a series of insights at a desk. This is a hard-rendering worry, not a recognition-creep worry — the operational stakes are clear (identify the source before the next coincidence window closes; decide what to communicate). The fix is in the outline: give the bioacoustician instruments to check, calls to make, a physical step to take when the answer arrives.

### #23 *The Salar's Allometry* (★★★★½)

*Worry:* The cascade has been ongoing for three years before the story enters; the protagonist's operational role at the moment of entry is unclear. What deadline does she face, what specifically does she do? The risk is the story becomes a *discovery* story (she finds out what the AI knows) rather than an *operation* story (she does something with the discovery). ### #10 *Ear of the Salar* (★★★★½)

*Stand-out:* The kind-AI texture is the most fully realized in the set.
