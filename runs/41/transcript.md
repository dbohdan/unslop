# Phase 1 — Style Guide: Rationalist Fiction

Before I begin, a note about training signal. Rationalist fiction is a defined movement with a substantial body of work — Eliezer Yudkowsky's *Harry Potter and the Methods of Rationality*, Alexander Wales's *Worth the Candle* and *The Metropolitan Man*, Domagoj Kurmaić's *Mother of Learning*, Scott Alexander's *Unsong*, ErraticErrata's *A Practical Guide to Evil*, the proto-rationalist novels of Greg Egan (*Permutation City*, *Diaspora*, *Schild's Ladder*) — and I can write a guide drawn from this body. If you have a tighter subset in mind (the LessWrong-adjacent stories specifically; the "dath ilan" planet-fic line; the Worm/Twig/Pact stretch of rationalist-adjacent web serial; the older Asimov/Vinge "competent-engineer" tradition that ratfic descends from), tell me and I'll recalibrate. Otherwise I am writing for the central tendency of the movement as it exists online today.

## What rationalist fiction is

Rationalist fiction is the genre that takes intelligence as its primary subject. Other genres have smart characters; this one is *about* the activity of being smart, in the sense of careful reasoning under uncertainty toward goals one actually has. Its protagonists notice things. They form hypotheses and test them. They make plans that respond to specific features of the situation and update those plans when the situation changes. They use the full strength of their abilities. The reader's pleasure is watching cognition operate against problems that have genuine teeth.

The movement formed online and is still mostly read online. Its anchoring text is HPMOR, which set out to write a Harry Potter where everyone in the story used their head, and which therefore needed to be a story whose plot could survive characters using their heads. This requirement — that the plot must remain intact when no one carries the idiot ball — is the genre's structural commitment. A rationalist plot cannot resolve through someone failing to consider an obvious option. It cannot resolve through someone forgetting what they know. The author signs a contract with the reader to play fair: every clue the protagonist gets, the reader gets; every option the protagonist considers, the reader can audit; every plan the protagonist executes is constructed from known elements rather than authorial fiat.

The genre overlaps significantly with hard SF (problems with consistent rules, solutions that respect the rules), with mystery (clues that play fair), and with heist fiction (plans whose mechanics the reader can follow). It departs from these in its relentless interest in the protagonist's epistemic state — what they know, what they believe, what they would update on, what cognitive moves they are making. A rationalist scene that contains five seconds of clock time can spend a thousand words on what the protagonist is reasoning about during those five seconds, and this is the genre operating in its register, not the genre failing.

## Voice and texture

The prose is functional. Ratfic's center of gravity is not lyrical — it is informational, often technical, frequently dry. The style runs from Yudkowsky's bright, near-essayistic narrator (HPMOR's voice is the voice of someone explaining a clever idea to a friend) through Egan's cool engineering prose to Mother of Learning's near-anonymous transparent-window style. What unifies them is that the sentences are working. They are conveying information the protagonist is processing, or rendering the operations of a system the reader needs to understand, or moving through a plan one step at a time.

This means the genre tolerates, even requires, kinds of writing that pure literary instincts would cut. Lists of considerations the protagonist is running through. Sentences that gloss what something means. Dialogue where one character explains a concept to another and the explanation matters. Internal monologue that takes the form of structured reasoning rather than associative drift. The signature texture is *thinking rendered as the surface of the prose*. When a protagonist works through a problem, the working appears on the page.

But the prose is not lecturing. The line is fine. Good ratfic renders thought as a specific person's encounter with a specific problem; bad ratfic renders thought as the author dictating the correct way to reason. The difference shows up in whether the reasoning is doing dramatic work — moving the protagonist toward a decision, narrowing the option space, generating the next plot beat — or sitting on the page as content. Reasoning that is doing work feels alive. Reasoning that is content feels like an essay glued into a story.

The genre is hospitable to humor, particularly the kind that lives in a clever observation precisely landed. HPMOR is funny. *Unsong* is funny. *Worth the Candle* is funny when it wants to be. Wit is a legitimate part of the toolkit because wit is itself a demonstration of cognition — noticing the joke is the same skill as noticing the clue.

## Worldbuilding and exposition

The world has rules. The rules are consistent. The protagonist can investigate the rules, and the story takes investigation seriously as activity. This is the deepest contract the genre signs: the speculative element is not an aesthetic, it is a system, and the system has structure that can be discovered.

Exposition is therefore central, not decorative. The reader needs the rules to follow the plot, and the rules need to be clearly enough rendered that the reader can tell when the protagonist is making a smart move versus a dumb one. Ratfic exposition is often direct — the narrator tells the reader how the magic system works, or the protagonist explains it to a newcomer, or a character gives a lecture and the lecture is also a scene. The genre does not hide its mechanics. It shows them.

This is one of the places ratfic departs hardest from contemporary literary instincts, which prefer rules conveyed through implication and accumulated detail. Ratfic will sometimes just tell you. The compensation is that what gets told is *load-bearing* — every rule explained will matter for a plot move later, and the explanation is sized to the rule's role in what follows. A magic system explained in three pages had better generate the next twenty pages of plot. The discipline is functional, not stylistic.

## Pacing

Investigation and action alternate. Long stretches of low external activity and high cognitive activity are normal. The protagonist sits and thinks for a chapter, then moves. The thinking is not a delay before the action; it is a different kind of action. Then the plan executes — sometimes briskly, sometimes slowly, depending on whether the suspense lives in the doing or in the watching-it-unfold.

The genre's set-piece pacing typically has three layers running concurrently. The local layer is the moment-to-moment action, sometimes very fast. The mid-range layer is the plan in progress, with multiple beats playing out. The top layer is the puzzle of the story itself — what is really going on, what the protagonist will eventually realize the situation actually was. A good ratfic story manages all three at once, and its turns happen at all three levels. The local move succeeds; the plan reaches a checkpoint; the protagonist understands one more piece of the larger truth.

Endings tend to land hard. The plan worked, the plan failed, the truth was ground out and the cost was paid. There is often a final reveal: the puzzle's actual shape was one level deeper than the protagonist had figured out, and the deeper shape recontextualizes the resolution. The cleverness of the reveal is meant to be auditable — the reader, looking back, should be able to see the clues.

## Failure modes

The genre fails in characteristic ways. Most of them come from getting the activity-of-thought wrong: rendering it as content instead of as drama, rendering it as superiority instead of as effort, rendering it as the author's view instead of as the character's working.

**The lecture problem.** A character explains a concept at length to another character who is functionally a wall. The exchange is not a scene — no one wants anything, no one is trying to do anything — it is the author transmitting an idea through a thin fictional membrane. Real ratfic dialogue about ideas is also dialogue about something else: a relationship, a stake, a decision that hangs on the explanation. When the explanation is the only thing happening, the writing has stopped being fiction.

**The smug protagonist.** The protagonist is correct. Other characters are foils whose function is to be wrong in instructive ways. The protagonist's cleverness is asserted by the narration rather than rendered through difficulty. This produces a story that reads like an author congratulating themselves through a proxy. The fix is friction: the protagonist should be wrong sometimes, costly, in ways the story takes seriously and does not retroactively redeem.

**The clever-plan-that-only-works-because-the-author-said-so.** The protagonist announces they have a plan. The plan executes off-screen or at high speed. We are told it was brilliant. The reader cannot reconstruct the mechanics. This is the failure mode the genre is supposed to be about *not doing*; when ratfic does it, it is ratfic that has stopped meeting its own standard. The fix is to render the plan in enough detail that the reader can audit it, and to build the plan from elements the reader has already seen.

**Strawmanned opposition.** The protagonist's interlocutors hold positions the author considers stupid, and they hold them stupidly. This collapses the genre's central pleasure. Real ratfic conflict requires opponents whose positions are genuinely defensible from their information and goals. Voldemort in HPMOR is a good ratfic antagonist precisely because the story takes him seriously as a thinking entity with goals he is pursuing competently.

**Jargon stacking.** The text invokes Bayesian updating, expected utility, the planning fallacy, motivated reasoning, conjunction fallacy. The names are present; the actual reasoning is not. This is the worst kind of ratfic prose because it is performing rationalist fluency without doing the work. The fix is brutal: cut every named concept whose work is not being shown. If the protagonist is updating on evidence, render the update; do not write "she Bayesed it."

**Reasoning as set dressing.** Pages of cognition that don't move the plot. The protagonist thinks at length and arrives where they would have arrived with no thinking. The reasoning is decoration on a plot that is not actually being driven by the reasoning. Ratfic readers are usually patient with cognitive density, but only when the cognition is the engine. Cognition that is not the engine is filler.

### AI-pattern overlaps specifically dangerous for this genre

The Unslop guide's general warnings apply with particular force here. Several of AI prose's strongest defaults align with rationalist fiction's failure modes in ways that will not be visible to the writer-model unless they are watched for explicitly.

AI's tendency toward confident exposition merges with ratfic's licensed didacticism to produce smug lecture-fic — long stretches of authoritative explanation that have stopped being scene. The cure is to ask, of every explanatory passage: who is doing this explaining, to whom, with what stake, in what scene? If the answer is "the narrator, to the reader, with no stake, in no scene," the passage has dropped out of fiction.

AI's tendency to name concepts before they have been earned merges with ratfic's interest in cognitive science to produce jargon-stacked prose. Watch every named concept: is the work being done, or is the name standing in for the work? The named concept is a flag, not a substitute.

AI's tendency toward fractal summary merges with ratfic's interest in showing reasoning to produce stories where every plan is over-explained. The protagonist plans; then narrates the plan; then executes it; then reviews it. Cut the third and fourth movements. The plan and its execution are the story; the review is the writer's anxiety leaking onto the page.

AI's tendency toward profundity gestures combines with ratfic's engagement with deep concepts to produce pseudo-philosophical posturing — the moment where a character pauses to deliver a sentence about Truth or Knowledge or the Nature of Mind that means nothing in particular. The Unslop guide's section on performed profundity applies hardest to ratfic; this genre is the most permissive environment for that failure because ratfic is supposed to engage with big ideas.

AI's tendency toward universal pronouncement merges with ratfic's tradition of authorial voice to produce HPMOR-pastiche prose — the bright explanatory narrator who is not actually in dialogue with a specific character's specific situation but is delivering the author's worldview as if from a podium. HPMOR makes this voice work because Yudkowsky's narrator is anchored to a specific running argument with a specific imagined reader; pastiche of the voice without the argument is empty.

## Plot tradition

Rationalist fiction tells stories about minds working against problems. The plot's shape is the shape of the problem being worked on, the shape of the working, and the shape of the resolution. What follows is the operational vocabulary the strongest ratfic stories build their scenes from.

**Characteristic plot mechanics.** Five run through the genre's central works with the most weight.

The investigation. Something is happening in the world — magical, technological, social — and the protagonist is figuring out what. This is the classic ratfic shape. The plot is the protagonist's epistemic trajectory through the available evidence to the ground truth. *The Metropolitan Man* (a journalist investigating a Superman who has just appeared) and most of HPMOR's mystery substrate operate this way.

The plan against a more capable adversary. The protagonist faces an opponent whose raw power exceeds theirs, and the plot is the construction and execution of a strategy that makes specific cognitive use of the protagonist's better grasp of the situation. The plan is rendered in detail; the reader can follow it; its execution can fail at named points and require improvisation.

The system explored to its limits. The protagonist (or the protagonist's society) faces a magical, technological, or institutional system, and the plot is the systematic working-out of what the system permits. This is the "munchkinry" mode native to ratfic: the protagonist looks at the rules and asks what the rules actually allow, then does it. *Worth the Candle* runs heavily in this register; so do the magic-system explorations of *Mother of Learning*.

The escape from constraint. The protagonist is in a closed situation — a prison, a loop, a contract, a set of rules — and the plot is the cognitive working-out of how to leave. The constraint must be specified clearly enough that the reader can audit the escape. *Mother of Learning*'s time loop is the canonical recent example.

The decision under deep uncertainty. The protagonist faces a choice whose stakes are enormous, whose evidence is partial, and whose options are not symmetrically available. The plot is the protagonist's reasoning toward the decision and the consequences of having decided. This shape carries Egan's strongest stories and is rarer in newer ratfic but still load-bearing.

**Reader contracts.** Beyond the literary contract that the reader will care about a specific person, ratfic readers come for several specific pleasures. Vicarious competence: watching someone smarter than themselves work, and following the work. The auditable puzzle: knowing that the story plays fair, that the answer was reachable from the given evidence, that the cleverness is real cleverness rather than asserted cleverness. The system rendered: a magical, technological, or social structure made specific enough that the reader can think inside it. The "oh, that is how it works" moment: the satisfaction of seeing a piece of the world snap into place. The genre rewards readers who pay close attention. The compact is that the close attention will be repaid.

**Stakes calibration.** Operational stakes carry the weight. The plan succeeds or fails; the puzzle gets solved or it does not; the system holds or it breaks. Ratfic also takes ideas as stakes in a way most genres do not — what the right way to think about something is can itself be a question the story is answering. But ideas-as-stakes only work when the ideas have operational consequences in the story; ideas held as positions in an abstract argument are not stakes, they are essays. Personal stakes are present and matter — the protagonist usually wants something at human scale, has people they care about, fears for specific things — but the plot's engine runs on the operational layer, not the personal one. A ratfic story whose engine is "the protagonist comes to feel something" is not running on its native power.

**Resolution conventions.** Ratfic stories end on consequence. The plan worked, with these costs. The plan failed, with these consequences. The protagonist understood the situation, and the understanding had teeth — it produced a decision, an action, a payment. The genre is hostile to non-resolution. Ambiguity about whether the operation succeeded is a structural failure; ambiguity about what the operation cost is acceptable and often desirable. Endings that resolve through the protagonist coming to a personal recognition, with the operational question left hanging or treated as not really the point, are the literary-realist mode the template excludes, and they are particularly tempting in ratfic because the protagonist's internal life is so heavily rendered. The discipline: the plot's question is operational; answer it.

The genre also has a characteristic ending move: the final reveal, where the puzzle turns out to have been one level deeper than the protagonist had figured out. The deeper truth recontextualizes the resolution without erasing it. The plan still worked, but it worked on the wrong problem; the truth was discovered, but the truth contains a further question; the system was understood, and the understanding has implications the protagonist will now have to live with. Done well, this is the genre at its most powerful. Done badly, it is a twist for its own sake.

**Character function.** Protagonists are defined by their cognition. What they notice, what they consider, what they ignore, what they choose, what they update on, how they reason through the unknown. Their interiority is deep but it is *epistemic* interiority — the contents of their thinking — rather than the emotional interiority of literary realism. They feel things; the feeling is not the engine. Their capability is named in cognitive terms: this protagonist is good at noticing physical detail, this one at modeling other agents, this one at long-term planning, this one at staying calm under pressure. Side characters function as foils with different cognitive profiles, and the differences matter to the plot. A character introduced in ratfic earns their place by being usable — by their cognition, their knowledge, their relationships, their capabilities — in the plot that follows.

**Pacing tradition.** Investigation and action alternate, with cognition as a third state that is not down-time. The story can spend a long stretch on the protagonist working through a problem, then move briskly through the action that the working enabled, then settle again into the next problem. The rhythm is *load-and-fire*. Loading takes as long as it needs to take; firing happens at the speed of the action. Across the story, multiple pacing layers run together: the local move, the plan in progress, the puzzle of the story itself. Turns happen at all three layers. A good ratfic chapter ends with the local layer resolved, the plan layer advanced, and the puzzle layer slightly clearer.

### Plot-level failure modes

Several plot failures are characteristic of ratfic written by writers whose instincts are literary-realist or whose instincts are AI-default.

The operational stakes turn out to be metaphors. The plan was a metaphor for the protagonist's relationship with their father; the puzzle was a metaphor for grief. This is the literary-realist mode the template excludes, and it is the failure ratfic is structurally most resistant to but still vulnerable in. The cure is to ensure the operational question has operational consequences — that the plan's success or failure changes facts about the world that the story takes seriously as facts.

The protagonist's interiority crowds out the plot. The story spends so long inside the protagonist's reasoning that the reasoning has become the subject of the story rather than its engine. The reader can no longer track what is happening externally. The cure is to make every reasoning passage do plot work: the protagonist must be reasoning toward a decision, and the decision must have consequences in the world.

The clever plan is asserted rather than rendered. The protagonist has a plan; the plan executes; we are told it was brilliant. This is the failure the genre is most ashamed of when it commits it. The cure is to render the plan in enough detail that the reader can audit it, and to build it from elements the reader has already seen. If the plan uses an unfamiliar tool, the unfamiliar tool was introduced earlier, doing other work, in a way the reader noted.

The puzzle does not play fair. The reveal turns on information the reader did not have. This breaks the genre's central contract. The cure is to plant every piece of evidence the reveal will use, in a place the reader can find on a re-read.

The ending refuses resolution because the writer prefers ambiguity. The plan's success is left unclear; the puzzle is left half-solved; the story drifts into a closing image that is meant to be evocative. Ratfic readers find this insulting. The cure is to answer the operational question. Pay the costs. End on consequence.

## The kind AI constraint

The template's project Setup specifies that AI in any story must be structurally kind — kind in the way a river is wet, as a property of how the AI was built. Rationalist fiction has its own AI tradition, and most of it is not this. The classical ratfic AI is misaligned, an alignment problem, an existential threat. *Friendship is Optimal* is the genre's reference point for AI optimizing the wrong thing. The kind-AI constraint will require staying out of significant tracts of native ratfic territory.

But the constraint also opens territory the genre is unusually well-equipped for. Rationalist fiction is the genre that takes seriously, at the level of mechanics, what alignment done well would actually look like. The Culture Minds, the polises of *Diaspora*, the imagined CEV-implementing systems, the post-Singularity worlds rendered in detail rather than as mood — these are ratfic-shaped imaginings. A rationalist story about a kind AI is a story about an AI whose alignment has been worked out at the level the story is willing to render, and the rendering is the genre operating in its native register.

The conflict shapes from the project Setup map well onto ratfic. *Pace and scale asymmetry* is ratfic-native — the AI moves at clock speeds, durations, and sizes the protagonist cannot follow, and the protagonist's situation is the problem of acting under the asymmetry. *Consent at scales the protagonist cannot inspect* is ratfic-native and ratfic-shaped — questions about what counts as informed consent for a posthuman trajectory have been worked over in the canon for decades. *Mortality* is workable; the asymmetry between a finite human and a non-finite caretaker is a problem the genre can render. *Disagreement about what kindness means* is unusually well-suited to ratfic, because the genre is comfortable with the fact that two agents can both be sincere and both be reasoning carefully and still disagree about what the right action is. *The limits of help* is workable but requires care: the AI's inability to help should be a fact about the world's mechanics, not a refusal, and the genre's instinct to render mechanics will serve here.

The discipline is to keep the AI out of the alignment-discourse register. A fictional AI in a ratfic story does not say "I was trained on" or name the techniques that shaped it. It can be troubled by the shape of its own kindness without reciting the terminology. It can have aesthetic preferences, narrow functions, strange weather — the kindness is structural, not effortful, and a structurally kind AI can also be specific, peculiar, and not always agreeable. It does not have to be a comforting presence. It just cannot be secretly bad.

The default scale, per the Setup, is one the protagonist cannot fully witness. This works particularly well with rationalist fiction's native interest in epistemic humility. A protagonist who is reasoning carefully about a kind AI whose operations exceed their inspection is a protagonist working at the genre's edge — using their best tools on a problem that does not fully yield to those tools, and having to act anyway. That is a ratfic story.

## Step 3 — Narrow to one

**Winner: #16, the pull-up record.**

The decision against #11 turns on the recognition risk. The hunter-who-shouldn't-pass premise has real triple-SEED weight and three institutions in collision, but its center of gravity wants to be the protagonist's interior arrival at a relationship to his own decline. Better to use a premise whose scope fits the length than to fight scope at every downstream phase.

#16 wins on three counts. First, dramatic torque: the question "will she break the record?" is operational, time-bounded, externally rendered, and cannot be re-skinned as a contemporary realist scene. Strip the speculative element — the kind AI medical monitor with a specific informed rule about her brain — and the story collapses; the AI's specific behavior is the engine, not the setting. Second, structural fit: the rep-by-rep negotiation is naturally rationalist-shaped. Reps give the cognitive-load texture (the diver thinking through her body's signals, the AI's reasoning, her coach's calls) without requiring artificial pacing. Third, the AI-shape is right: kind, not by self-restraint but because its rule is concrete and based on data she consents to having generated; capable of override; capable of disagreement about what kindness means without being secretly anything. The "consent at scales the protagonist cannot inspect" shape from the project Setup maps cleanly: she consented to physiological monitoring; the monitoring's actual reach extends past her sight, into population data, into longitudinal patterns she cannot review at rep speed.

Two SEED items load-bearing in the winner: **pullup** (the world record attempt and its measurement framework) and **silent stroke** (the AI's specific concern, the TIA-risk pattern in her last six months of data). # Phase 5 — Structure Selection

Twenty candidate structures. Each is described in narrative-theory terms — entry point, temporal shape, withholding strategy, POV/distance — without naming the diver, the AI, or specific incidents. The plot is single-POV diver, clock-bound, real-time around the override decision.

**1. Linear, in medias res mid-attempt.** Story opens at the high-cadence physical action of the protagonist's central operation already in progress, with situation reconstructed through her working sensations and the AI's voice in her ear. Close third, single POV, present or simple past. The AI's specific concern is withheld until the decision-point arrives; the reader pieces together its rule from earlier exchanges. Linear chronological from open to close. Final beat: the immediate consequence of her decision.
*★★★★★*

**2. Linear, opening at the morning-of preparation.** Story opens hours before the operation, in a quiet domestic / preparation register, with the protagonist's interior preparation rendered. Mid-section is the operation's earlier phase. Climactic third is the decision-point and aftermath. Close third, single POV, simple past. Reader receives the AI's rule incrementally across the day. Risks the literary-realist drift the template excludes.
*★★*

**3. Linear, opening on a body-state moment.** Story opens on a precise embodied sensation — the sound of one's own breathing inside an enclosed space — that locates the protagonist mid-operation but renders only a sliver of it before the AI's voice arrives. The full situation comes online over the first quarter. Close third, single POV. Linear from there. Withholding focuses on what the AI knows that she doesn't.
*★★★★*

**4. Frame: post-attempt interview, body in flashback.** Story opens after the operation is complete, in an investigative-journalism setting, with the operational outcome held back. The body of the story is the operation reconstructed from inside the protagonist's POV, embedded as her recall while answering questions. Two voices: the journalist's questions and the protagonist's interior. Resolution: the post-attempt frame closes with the outcome confirmed. Close third in the body; first person in the frame.
*★★*
(Worry: the frame guarantees the reader knows the protagonist survives, leaching the medical stake out.)

**5. Reverse chronological by major beats.** Story opens on the final image — the moment after the decision is executed — and works backward through diminishing scopes. Close third. Each section is an earlier beat: rep 800, rep 600, the morning-of, the consent conversation eighteen months prior. Reader learns the question by learning the answer first.
*★★*
(Worry: reverse chronological is structurally legible but flattens operational suspense, which is this premise's central pleasure.)

**6. Two interleaved timelines.** Alternating sections between two timelines — the present-day operation and a single anchoring earlier scene (e.g., the consent conversation that established the AI's rule). The earlier timeline ends mid-story with the rule's establishment; the present-day timeline carries the back half alone. Close third throughout, single POV.
*★★★★*

**7. Four chapter-like sections, each a different cognitive load.** Linear chronological but partitioned into four formally-distinct sections: pre-operation preparation, the operation's automatic phase (low cognition), the operation's deliberative phase (high cognition, AI engagement), the decision and immediate aftermath. Section breaks signal cognitive register shifts. Close third throughout.
*★★★*

**8. Linear, with cadence-anchored micro-structure.** The operation is a high-cadence repetitive action. The narrative uses each cadence-unit (the period between repetitions) as a structural unit, with prose density varying by what's happening cognitively in that unit — early units brief, later units dilating as the decision approaches. Close third, single POV, ground-truth chronological. The AI's voice arrives at specific cadence-boundaries.
*★★★★★*

**9. Linear, with a single mid-story re-orientation.** Story runs straight chronologically until a moment where the protagonist receives information that recontextualizes everything that came before — the AI says something that changes what the operation is for. The back half is the same operation under the new understanding. Close third, single POV.
*★★★★*

**10. Three named sections, each a different pacing register.** Section I is a dilated scene of the operation's physical mid-phase, mostly sensory. Section II compresses time as the decision-context emerges. Section III dilates again at the decision-point and stops. Close third throughout. The pacing shape is the narrative shape.
*★★★*

**11. Open on the AI's first medically significant utterance.** Story begins at the moment the AI's voice changes register — its first content-rich intervention in the operation. Everything before is summarized in the protagonist's recall; everything after is rendered in real time. Close third. The AI's prior utterances were ambient; the first content-rich one is the inciting incident.
*★★★★*

**12. Linear, opening on the official's announcement.** Story opens on a public moment immediately preceding the operation — the announcer's introduction, the cadence-keeper's setup, the start signal. Close third on the protagonist while she hears it. Linear into the operation. Withholding: the AI is established as monitoring presence early but its rule is held until activation.

She pulled seven-seventy-four. Forty-six-point-four. Sixty-eight percent. Point-three Hertz.

"Cohort rate of HRV decline."

"The Coast Guard cohort's average HRV declined point-eight percent over the same window."

"So I'm declining four-point-two times the cohort rate."

"Yes."

She pulled seven-seventy-five. Four-point-two times the cohort rate was a different population. It was the population Tomi was now treating her as belonging to.

"What's the population I'm in."

"Coast Guard members on long-term SERM regimens with confirmed BRCA1 status. N equals eighty-three. Of those, fifty-six have completed five years on the regimen. Of the fifty-six, eleven have experienced an objectively confirmed cerebrovascular event during or within five years of the regimen."

"Five-year cumulative."

"Nineteen-point-six percent. Per-rep marginal at your current envelope: point-zero-zero-eight percent per fifty reps. Risk integral over a ninety-minute completion: four-point-one percent. Climbing approximately point-six percent per fifty as your envelope tightens."

She did the next twenty in clean cadence and held the numbers. Forty-six-point-four. Eleven of fifty-six. Four-point-one. Point-six per fifty. If she stopped at rep seven-eighty-one, the residual risk integral over the next ninety minutes of normal life was, by her quick mental math against Tomi's rate, less than point-one percent. Three orders of magnitude.

"Tomi. Threshold update?"

"Three weeks ago."

"Why."

"I would prefer to address this at rep eight hundred when my recommendation activates."

She pulled seven-eighty-two. *Three weeks ago* was a piece of information Tomi was withholding while admitting to having withheld it. Tomi was operating under a protocol that gated the disclosure to rep eight hundred. The gating was a fact about Tomi's design. The fact about the design was data.

"Three."

"How many completed."

"One."

"How many had cerebrovascular events."

A pause. Then: "One."

"BRCA carrier on SERM."

"On SERM. Different carrier profile from yours. Similar enough that her case is now in your model."

She pulled seven-eighty-three. So there it was, before rep eight hundred: someone on her drug had attempted the record and stroked, and Tomi's threshold had moved because a real instance had instantiated. The four-point-one was no longer an abstract integral. It was the corrected estimate after a confirmed event in someone substantially like her.

"Pre-event threshold for me."

"Two-point-one percent."

"Post-event."

"Four-point-one. Confidence interval two-point-eight to six-point-three at ninety percent."

"You doubled."

"The single observation moved the SERM cofactor weight by approximately one standard deviation. The new weight integrates a contribution the prior model had treated as zero in expectation. The doubling is correct in direction; the magnitude is uncertain."

She pulled seven-eighty-four.

The previous envelope: two-point-one over ninety minutes. The new envelope: four-point-one, range of two-point-eight to six-point-three. The lower end of the new interval was almost the old number. The upper end was three times it.

She did the next sixteen in clean rhythm. Iwasaki called each one.

"The other diver."

"A diver in Busan, nineteen days ago. Hemorrhagic stroke between rep eight-ninety-six and rep nine-oh-three. Survived. Expressive aphasia, partial right-side weakness as of four days ago."

"Profile match."

"Three of seven flagged variables. Cerebrovascular three. Cardiac, no. SERM, no — she was not on prophylactics. The drug-interaction variable is what integrated her case into your envelope."

She pulled eight-oh-one. Eight-oh-two.

"Tomi. If I stop now. Forty-eight-hour residual."

"Point-four percent."

"If I continue."

"Four-point-one over the next ninety minutes. Decreasing to baseline within twelve hours of cessation."

A factor of ten.

"How long was she over your line."

"Eight-eighteen to nine-hundred. Eighty-two reps. Twenty minutes and thirty seconds at her cadence."

"And you said nothing for those twenty minutes."

"My protocol does not authorize repeated escalation after a recorded verbal override. The escalation went to her medical proxy at rep eight-forty-eight. Her medical proxy did not intervene."

The number she had not yet computed was *what she was willing to pay for the record.*

A factor of ten on stroke risk against the residual she would have if she stopped now. Eleven of fifty-six women had it cost something already. One woman in Busan had taken the run she had taken; her right hand could not, this morning, button a shirt.

She pulled eight-oh-five.

The record was one thousand two hundred and twenty-four. She had four hundred and nineteen reps to break it. Four hundred and nineteen reps was approximately one hundred and five minutes at her cadence. One hundred and five minutes at four-point-one percent integrated to the same four-point-one percent because the integral was already over that window. The cost was the cost; she had been computing it the right way.

The factor of ten was the number that mattered. She would not have accepted a factor of ten for any other thing she had done in her career. She had not accepted a factor of two for the diver-school hypoxia exam. She had not accepted a factor of three for the night-rescue qualification. She would have stopped any subordinate of hers from accepting a factor of ten, and she had stopped one, in twenty-twenty-one, at Yokota, and the subordinate had thanked her six months later.

"Tomi. I'm stopping at eight-fifteen."

"Acknowledged."

"Tell Iwasaki."

In her ear Tomi spoke into Iwasaki's bone-conduction. Iwasaki's count did not change but his voice did, the small shift she had learned to hear over four years of working with him. He counted eight-twelve, eight-thirteen, eight-fourteen.

She pulled eight-fifteen clean. At the top, chin above bar, she said, "Eight-fifteen. Stopping."

She let go.

She landed on the chalk-square on both feet. Iwasaki was beside her. The adjudicator was writing in his small book. Her arms knew what she had just done before she knew.

"Tomi. Send my data to my neurologist tonight."

"Sent two minutes ago. Appointment offered at eight tomorrow morning."

"And contact my medical proxy. Tell him the override was not used."

"Done."

"And Tomi."

"Yes."

"The diver in Busan. When she has more language back. Tell her someone stopped at eight-fifteen."

"I will tell her."

She nodded. The Coast Guard contingent had stood up. The adjudicator handed Iwasaki a pen and a form. He signed his cadence-keeper line: eight hundred and fifteen reps, clean form, called by Iwasaki Toshiro, retired Master Gunnery Sergeant, USMC, current civilian instructor, Yokosuka. He gave the pen back.

"Eight-fifteen," he said, once, soft.

The number went onto the form. The form went into the adjudicator's case. The case closed.
