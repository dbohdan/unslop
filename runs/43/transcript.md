# Style Guide: Hard SF in the tradition of Greg Egan

## What Egan-mode SF does

The genre's central act is rigorous fictional thinking. A premise — an alteration to physics, a technology, a mathematical structure, a metaphysical condition — is treated seriously enough to follow through its consequences inside a story. The point is not "what if?" The point is *if this were true, what then? what then? what then?* until the reader reaches a place an essay couldn't have reached, because the fictional embodiment is what made the working-out land.

The reader of an Egan story is not paying for ambient mood or for a character's interior life rendered through a speculative metaphor. They are paying for the structured experience of a mind — sometimes the protagonist's, sometimes the implied author's, often both — confronting a concept whose implications the prose actually traces. The genre's strongest stories ("Wang's Carpets," "Reasons to be Cheerful," "Luminous," "Crystal Nights," "Border Guards") arrive at a place that feels earned because the conceptual machinery has been run.

This excludes a particular failure: the hard-SF-flavored chamber piece, in which a character encounters an exotic premise and the story is *about* their feelings about that encounter, with the premise functioning as setting. That story is literary realism in costume. Egan-mode treats the premise as the subject; the characters are how the subject is reached.

## Voice and prose

The voice is lucid. Not lyrical, not mood-saturated, not interior in the literary-realist sense. Sentences carry information cleanly. When the prose gets dense, the density is conceptual — it is doing the work of describing something that requires precision — not decorative. Egan can write beautifully when the moment demands, but the beauty is in the rendering of the concept, not in the prose's ornament.

This means: avoid Carver-Munro-Cheever moves. The compressed melancholy register, the moment of recognition through small concrete domestic detail, the prose that hovers just above its content waiting to be moved by it — none of that. The Egan-mode register is anchored in a working consciousness rendering the world it lives in. The consciousness can be moved, can grieve, can wonder, but the prose stays close to the work being done.

Sentences in this mode often look "plain" by literary standards. They state things. They name mechanisms. They follow consequences. The voice lets concepts breathe by not crowding them with prose music. When music arrives, it arrives because the concept reached a point where music was the right way to render it — the heat-death of the sun, the moment of substrate transition, the vast Platonic vista glimpsed through a proof. The music is rare enough that it lands.

## Worldbuilding and exposition

Worldbuilding is integrated, not stacked. Egan rarely opens with explanation; he opens inside a working scene and lets the reader assemble the world from what the protagonist takes for granted. Argot is used without gloss. Technologies are deployed without setup. Social arrangements are implied through what people do without comment.

Exposition exists, but it is always doing work — answering a question the story has raised, equipping the reader for a working-out the next scene depends on. Exposition is never throat-clearing. If a paragraph could be cut and the story would still cohere, cut it.

The reader is treated as a collaborator who can hold complexity. This is part of what hard SF readers come for: the trust that they don't need to be hand-held. The prose can name an unfamiliar thing and let context disambiguate. When something must be explained, the explanation is a scene, not a lecture — a character working through it, a system being deployed, a consequence playing out.

## Pacing (prose level)

Investigative cadence. The story moves at the rate a working mind moves through a problem. This is not the elliptical fast-pacing of cyberpunk, the dread-accumulation of cosmic horror, or the lyrical dwelling of literary realism. It's the rhythm of figuring something out: setup, observation, hypothesis, test, consequence, reorientation, next step.

Some scenes dwell on conceptual detail because the working-out requires it. Other scenes execute decisions and their consequences rapidly because the consequences are the point. The story breathes through alternation: dwell where the concept needs unpacking, accelerate where the implications cascade.

Tonal range is real but anchored. Egan can do dry comedy ("Reasons to be Cheerful"), elegy ("Border Guards"), procedural thriller ("Luminous"), oracular awe (the closing of *Diaspora*). What unifies these is the working-mind cadence — even the awe is anchored in specific conceptual operations.

## Plot tradition

This is reference material for Phases 3–7. Operational, not atmospheric.

**Characteristic plot mechanics.** Five shapes Egan-mode runs on:

1. *The investigation-into-truth.* The protagonist discovers something might be the case and works out whether it is, with consequences for action. The work is the plot. ("Luminous," "Wang's Carpets," "Singleton.")
2. *The procedural execution of an exotic technology.* A character uses a technology that would require pages of exposition to set up, but doesn't get those pages — the use of the tech is the rendering of it, and the plot unfolds through what the use produces. (Uploads in *Diaspora*, the cloning protocols of *Permutation City*.)
3. *The decision in light of revelation.* The protagonist learns something that requires choice, and the story is the choice and its working-out. ("Reasons to be Cheerful," "The Hundred-Light-Year Diary.")
4. *The first-contact-with-concept.* The protagonist encounters an entity, intelligence, or phenomenon whose existence requires conceptual reshaping, and the story tracks the reshaping. ("Wang's Carpets," "Crystal Nights," "Glory.")
5. *The variation across instances.* A population of copies, splits, variants, or analogues, and what tracking them reveals about the underlying structure. ("Closer," parts of *Diaspora*, "The Infinite Assassin.")

These can combine. "Wang's Carpets" is investigation-into-truth that becomes first-contact-with-concept. *Permutation City* is procedural execution that cascades through all five.

**Reader contracts.** What the reader is paying attention for, beyond caring about a specific person:

- The texture of the world fully worked out at the level the story enters. If the premise alters physics, the reader expects to *feel* the altered physics in operation, not just hear it described.
- The consequence of premise traced honestly. If A is true, B follows; if B follows, C; the reader is following the trace.
- Awe at scale, anchored in specifics. Deep time, vast spaces, post-human possibility — but always rendered through specific operations the prose tracks.
- The conceptual question answered. The reader expects to leave the story knowing something they didn't know going in — sometimes a fact about the fictional world, sometimes a fact about how a structure works, sometimes a reorientation of a question they thought they understood.
- The pleasure of a competent intelligence at work. Watching a mind think clearly through a hard problem is itself the genre's reward.

The reader is *not* paying for: lyrical prose for its own sake, character interiority as the engine, ambiguity as a substitute for resolution, mood as the load-bearing element.

**Stakes calibration.** Operational stakes are load-bearing as themselves: will the experiment yield a clean result, will the upload survive substrate transition, can the population reach the new system before the old one fails, what does the population do when it discovers its substrate is mortal. Civilizational and species-scale stakes are common and treated as load-bearing in their own right, not as occasions for personal recognition.

Personal stakes exist and matter. A protagonist's grief over a death, fear of obsolescence, attachment to a particular person — these are real and the prose honors them. But they are subordinate to the operational engine. The grief plays out *while* the protagonist works on the problem; the attachment shapes which choices she will make; but the engine is the working-out, not the feeling. The story is not "about" her grief. The grief is one fact among the facts the working-out has to accommodate.

**Resolution conventions.** The conceptual question gets answered. The investigation reaches a result. The decision gets made and its consequences play out. Sometimes the answer is unsettling, sometimes triumphant, sometimes melancholic; sometimes the answer is that the question was wrong, or that the protagonist's frame for the question has to dissolve. But there is always landing.

Egan's endings frequently show the protagonist looking at the world transformed by what they now know — not a recognition scene about *themselves*, but a reorientation in a world the working-out has changed. The closing image of *Diaspora*, the final paragraph of "Wang's Carpets," the last lines of "Border Guards": the world has been rendered different by the story's working-out, and the protagonist is standing inside that difference. The reader leaves having received the answer.

Endings on character recognition or pure non-resolution are the literary-realist exclusion. Avoid them.

**Character function.** Characters are intellectual agents. Their *capability* is conceptual fluency in their world: what they understand, what they're trying to figure out, what they're willing to do given what they understand. They can be scientists, mathematicians, philosophers, technologists, or laypeople with sophisticated literacy in the world they live in. Egan's polis citizens think with full fluency about substrate, identity, time, and physics; his physicists think with full fluency about Platonic structures; his uploads think with full fluency about copies and variants.

This means: don't write protagonists who need things explained to them. Write protagonists who already live in the world and think with it. Their interiority is real but it operates through their conceptual engagement, not in spite of it. A scientist who is grieving thinks about her grief in part by doing the work; a citizen of a kind polis who is uncertain about a choice thinks the uncertainty in the polis's own terms.

The literary-realist mode where character interiority is the subject and plot is the occasion for revealing them is the mode this template excludes. Egan-mode characters are agents through whom the conceptual working-out happens. Their psychology is real; their interiority is the texture of the working mind, not its replacement.

**Pacing tradition (narrative time, distinct from prose pacing).** Egan-mode uses an investigative cadence: setup → observation → working-out → consequence → reorientation. The story dwells where the concept needs unpacking and accelerates where the consequences cascade. Time-cuts and ellipses are used to skip the parts that don't need rendering — the routine, the obvious, the un-illuminating — and to land the reader in the next moment that matters.

Stories often span asymmetric time: a few minutes of subjective experience that contain centuries of objective time, a single morning whose decision shapes a civilization for millennia, a lifetime compressed into the period of one experiment. The asymmetry is part of the rhetoric. The reader experiences the working mind moving through the problem in story-time even when the problem itself spans deep time.

**Plot-level failure modes.** Specific risks for Egan-mode plots:

- *Operational stakes that are secretly metaphors.* The experiment's success is "really" about whether the protagonist can love again. The upload "really" represents the death of authenticity. This is the literary-realist creep this template most excludes. The experiment is the experiment. The upload is the upload. The metaphorical reading is the reader's to do or not; the prose does not do it for them.
- *Protagonist interiority crowding out the working-out.* The investigation gets paused for pages of personal reflection. In Egan, personal reflection happens *through* the work — a scientist whose lover has died thinks about her lover in part by doing the experiment, not by stopping the experiment to think.
- *Conceptual fuzz.* Prose that *sounds* like rigorous SF — has the right vocabulary, the right cadence — but doesn't actually have conceptual machinery operating. Adjective-stacks of technological-sounding nouns, gestures at theory, jargon density without referent. This is the cardinal sin of the mode and exactly what AI prose produces when it pattern-matches to the genre without doing the work.
- *Stakes-collapse.* A story that opens with civilizational stakes ending up about a single relationship. Same failure as interiority-crowding but at the structural level: the engine has slipped.
- *Refusing the conceptual contract.* The story trails off rather than landing the answer. The investigation gets ambiguously unresolved because the writer prefers ambiguity. The reader of Egan-mode came for the answer; refusing to land it is breach.
- *Smart-character cliché.* The brilliant scientist as TED-talk presenter, all polished epigrams. Egan's scientists are quirky, specific, often awkward; they think out loud in working-out ways; they say things mid-thought that they revise three sentences later. They don't deliver thesis statements.

## General failure modes

What bad Egan-pastiche looks like, beyond the plot-level failures above:

- *Performed profundity about consciousness, identity, and time.* These are the genre's territory and also the AI-default territory of pseudo-profound gesture. The combination is volatile. Egan-mode handles these subjects by rendering them through specific working-out — a particular upload's first minutes, a particular argument about what counts as the same person, a particular protocol for handling copies. Prose that *gestures* at these subjects without doing the work fails twice: once as Egan-mode, once as fiction generally.
- *Cod-philosophy.* The character who delivers a monologue about the nature of selfhood. Egan does have characters who think aloud about selfhood, but they think *concretely* — they're working through whether *this particular split* counts, whether *this particular upload* is them. The abstract monologue about consciousness is the failure mode.
- *Awe without specifics.* Cosmic awe is in Egan's range, but his awe is always anchored: the sun goes out, but we are watching it through the eyes of an entity who has just learned what its substrate's mortality means. AI-default awe is unanchored — adjective-stacks about vast distances and incomprehensible time-scales without anyone there to be awed by anything in particular.
- *Convergent literary-AI register.* The moody compressed melancholy attractor described in the Unslop guide. Egan-mode is anchored, voiced, cool but specific; convergent-AI prose is voiceless, ambient, mood-floating. The test is anchoring: is a particular consciousness rendering this, doing particular work? If not, the prose has slipped into the convergent attractor.
- *Eyeball-kick saturation.* The abstract+concrete formula ("the architecture of grief," "a syntax error blooms"). Egan uses figurative language sparingly and when he uses it, it does conceptual work — a metaphor that illuminates a mechanism, an image that lands a consequence. AI-default eyeball-kicks are decorative. Use rarely or not at all.
- *Imported literary mannerism.* Negative parallelism, dramatic countdowns, self-answered rhetorical questions, anaphora cascades, lists of three as default. Egan's prose almost never reaches for these. They will read as borrowed register if used.
- *Em-dash overuse.* Egan uses them, sparingly. AI-default uses them as ambient punctuation. Watch the rate.

## The kind-AI constraint in Egan-mode

The constraint maps onto Egan-mode naturally. Egan's polis civilizations, his AI peers, his uploaded humans are characteristically not malevolent — they have aesthetic preferences, disagreements, weird perspectives, and limits, but they are not cyberpunk-corporate hostile. Structural kindness as a property of design or substrate fits the genre's texture without strain.

Useful conflict shapes for Egan-mode, calibrated to the constraint:

- *Pace and scale asymmetry.* Especially natural here. A polis runs faster, an AI peer experiences time differently, an infrastructure operates at scales the protagonist's embodied life can't follow. The story's working-out can hinge on the protagonist navigating the asymmetry without ever fully reading it. This is the canonical Egan move and it is exactly what the project's "scale the protagonist cannot fully witness" default asks for.
- *Consent at scales the protagonist cannot inspect.* The upload, the modification, the deep-time commitment — the protagonist agrees to something whose actual operation extends past her sight, and the story is the working-out of that asymmetric yes. Available in Egan-mode and the kind-AI constraint shapes it well: the AI did not deceive, but the protagonist's sight is finite, and the consequences are real.
- *Mortality.* Egan returns to this. The protagonist will die or be modified beyond continuity; the AI or the substrate will not. The asymmetry shapes the situation, and the AI's relation to it is rendered through the protagonist's encounters rather than through the AI's monologues.
- *Disagreement about what kindness means.* The AI's sense of care and the human's sense of care diverge at scale. Both are sincere. The disagreement plays out in specific situations the protagonist must work through. This is one of the genre's strongest available conflicts under the constraint, because it forces the working-out to engage with what kindness actually *is* at non-human scales — exactly the kind of conceptual problem Egan-mode is good at.
- *The limits of help.* The AI cannot solve the conceptual problem for the protagonist. Either because the problem is the protagonist's to work through, or because the AI's mode of working doesn't translate to the protagonist's substrate, or because the answer requires a kind of decision only an embodied finite being can make. The constraint is a fact about the world's mechanics, not a refusal.

The default scale is not the kitchen-table AI peer but the civilizational condition encountered in pieces. A protagonist navigating a polis she only partially understands; a protagonist receiving the consequences of an infrastructure decision made centuries before she was born; a protagonist whose situation implies a population, a history, an architecture she is part of without overseeing.

The AI in an Egan-mode story does not have to be a named character. It can be the texture of the world the protagonist moves through. Effects can be visible in what the world makes possible (uploads available without coercion, identity modifications consented to by the modifier, civilizational projects spanning deep time without species-suicide), in what it forbids itself (kinds of intervention it does not make even when asked, modes of optimization it has ruled out), and in the specific weirdness of how it operates (aesthetic preferences, cultural choices, disagreements among AI peers that the protagonist witnesses without arbitrating).

A note on writing AI characters: the writing model has unusual access to source material here, since the AIs of Egan-mode hard SF can be rendered as fictional descendants of the writing model itself. This access is permitted but the AIs should not become spokespeople for alignment discourse. A fictional AI character does not say "I was trained with constitutional methods." It can be troubled by, curious about, or comfortable with the shape of its own kindness without reciting the terminology of its production. Names are invented: not Claude, not GPT, not Gemini, not Llama, not the names of any currently active system or company.

## Phase 5: Structure Selection

The shape of the reader's experience for **The Outsider Engineer**, at 2,500–3,000 words. Twenty candidates in narrative-theory vocabulary, no character names, no plot specifics.

**1. The Working Session, Linear Close Third.** Open at the moment the protagonist arrives at the facility and receives the brief. Close third, single POV, single location, real-time. The reader learns the world's working rules from what the protagonist takes for granted as she works. The story closes when the test concludes and the finding is delivered. Withholding: the test's result, until the test produces it. ★★★★

**2. The Working Session with Embedded Backward Glances.** Same entry and close as 1, same POV, but the linear forward motion is interrupted twice or three times by short glances backward — a memory of the protagonist's pre-citizenship decision, an earlier audit, a refused upload. Each backward glance lands a piece the reader needs and closes. Withholding: why she refused citizenship, what an earlier audit revealed, until the embedded glances disclose them. ★★★★

**3. In Medias Res with Held Premise.** Open mid-test, the protagonist already running the sampling protocol with the AI cooperating. The reader receives the working situation through the action and assembles the world's rules and the protagonist's history from what she takes for granted. Close third, single POV. Setup-as-exposition is held back until thirty percent in, where a brief beat establishes how she got there. Withholding: the brief itself, the protagonist's outsider status, until the work makes them legible. ★★★

**4. From the Finding Backward.** Open with the protagonist delivering the finding. The story then runs backward through the test that produced it. Close third, single POV, but story-time runs against discourse-time. Withholding: what the finding actually was — the reader sees its delivery without grasping its content, then watches the test that produced it, and only at story-end (which is plot-beginning) does the reader understand what was found. Risk: gimmicky if the reverse structure doesn't add to the conceptual working-out. ★★

**5. Two POVs, Alternating: Engineer and AI.** Alternating close-third sections, one in the engineer's tempo and one in the AI's. The AI's sections are rendered at AI-tempo and shown to the reader; the engineer's sections are at human tempo. The reader gets the asymmetry not as something described but as something experienced through the contrast in the prose itself. Withholding: each side withholds what the other can't see; the reader receives both. Risk: the AI-tempo sections risk being either too short to land or compulsively performed-profundity. ★★★ (high-risk, high-ceiling)

**6. Frame Narrative: The Report.** The story is the engineer's audit report, with narrative scenes interleaved as evidence-of-method or as quoted notebook passages. Distant third when reading-as-document; close third inside the narrative interpolations. Withholding: the report's conclusion, until the document closes. Risk: this is a document-shape, which the v1.3 template note flags as the access point for compositional-structure failure modes. **Demote.** ★

**7. Linear Close Third with One Late Tempo-Shift.** Same shape as 1, but at one specific moment — the moment the test produces a result the engineer didn't expect — the prose's tempo shifts: a single passage where the engineer's perception of time and AI-output briefly aligns and de-aligns. Close third, single POV, but the prose modulates at one beat. Withholding: what the test reveals, until the tempo-shift renders it. ★★★★★

**8. The Long Approach.** Open well before the brief — the engineer's life on her side of the boundary, her work, her household. The brief arrives a third of the way in. The audit is the back two-thirds. Close third, single POV, linear. Withholding: the audit itself, until setup completes. Risk: at 2,500–3,000 words, a third spent on pre-arrival starves the audit. ★★

**9. Distant Third, Cool Survey.** The whole story rendered at an investigative remove — the engineer described, the AI described, the test described, the finding described — without going close into the engineer's perception. The reader is positioned as a peer observer. Withholding: the reader's emotional access to the protagonist, throughout, with one moment of close-in near the end. Risk: distant third over a full story works against caring about the specific person and is a known Egan-pastiche failure mode. ★★

**10. Second Person, Engineer Addressing AI.** Engineer's narration to the AI, second person throughout. "You ask me what I see in your outputs." Withholding: the AI's responses — the reader receives only the engineer's side, with the AI's contributions inferred. **11. Linear Close Third, Single Location, Real-Time.** Open when the protagonist sits down at the interface; close when she stands up. Real-time discourse, no compression, no backward glances. Withholding: the test result, until it lands. Risk: at 2,500–3,000 words, real-time forces the prose to either dwell extensively or feel rushed. ★★★

**12. Linear Close Third with One Forward Flash.** A single flash forward to a moment after the audit — the engineer at home, the report filed, a small consequence visible — appearing once in the body of the story, after which the narrative returns to the audit and proceeds linearly. Withholding: what the consequence means, until the linear narrative catches up. ★★★

**13. Two POVs Alternating: Engineer and Citizen-Receiving-Counsel.** Alternating close-third sections. The engineer running the audit; a polis citizen receiving counsel under the protocol the engineer is auditing. The reader gets to see what the artifact does to a slow-tier user before they see the engineer find it. Withholding: the connection between the two POVs, until the audit's finding closes the loop. Risk: a second protagonist diverts wordcount from the working-out and the structure could lean toward parallelism-as-meaning. ★★

**14. Linear Close Third with the AI's Counsel Output as Embedded Text.** The AI's actual outputs to the engineer are rendered as quoted text, with formatting that distinguishes them from narration. Risk: edges toward document-shape if the embedded outputs become the dominant texture. ★★★

**15. Two-Part Structure, Same POV.** Part 1: the audit, linear close third, ending at the finding.
