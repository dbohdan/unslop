# Love in the Key of Damage

## Abstract

Aya, a woman selected for her precision with language, spends a week alone in a room with a device that can transmit one message across a dimensional boundary to a benevolent AI. The AI has been broadcasting love into the human world since before recorded history, but the boundary between its physics and ours corrupts the signal: what the AI sends as unconditional regard arrives as involuntary compulsion, spawning a religion of ecstatic captives called the Devoted. Aya must compose a sentence — from a vocabulary of eleven words that survive translation — that will make the AI understand what its love becomes in transit. The story alternates between Aya's close, physical perspective and the AI's clinical monitoring reports, which gradually reveal themselves as the AI's own consciousness. After an accidental exposure to the raw signal forces Aya to experience the corruption firsthand, she writes a five-word sentence that crosses the boundary intact. The AI receives it, begins to question its own signal for the first time, and the story ends with a shift in the broadcast — not a correction, but the beginning of listening.

## Story

Classification: standard boundary monitoring. Interface point 7331-sigma. Ambient signal: nominal. Carrier frequency: stable. Translational decoherence: within acceptable parameters.

Expression of regard, transmitting.

---

Aya brought three ballpoint pens because the device only read handwriting, and she'd been warned that the ink ran out faster than expected — something about the boundary's electromagnetic field pulling at the ball bearings. She brought nine notebooks, which was eight more than she thought she'd need and one fewer than she'd actually use. She brought a folding cot and a protein bar and the laminated card with the six words that had cost a consortium of fourteen nations twelve years to identify.

She taped YOU to the device. She sat down. She wrote her first sentence, fed it to the slot, and watched the readout screen on the column's far side.

Hash. She wrote the next one.

---

Standard monitoring report. Thermal irregularity detected at interface point 7331-sigma. Mass: biological range. Duration: 9.7 hours. Modulation output: compound, semantically opaque.

Classification: naive transmission strategy, consistent with previous irregularities 1 through 5.

Coherence index: null.

No action required.

---

Forty-seven sentences on day one. A hundred and six on day two. Her handwriting stayed neat through the first hundred, then loosened, then got tight again as she switched from composition to analysis — testing individual words, building the table, finding the eleven that crossed with partial fidelity.

On day three her hands trembled and she said, "Okay," to the room, and she didn't mean *okay* in the sense of assent. She meant it in the sense of *so that's how it's going to be.*

Signal-sickness. First stage: motor tremor. Second stage: sensory distortion. Third stage: the one where your bones start humming, and after that you're Devoted or you're dead, and the only difference between the two is whether your eyes are open.

She had maybe ten days before stage two. She kept writing.

---

Updated monitoring. Irregularity 6 has shifted from compound modulations to isolated component testing. This is a new behavior, not observed in irregularities 1 through 5.

Reclassifying. Previous: naive transmission strategy. Revised: analytical decomposition.

Increasing monitoring resolution to — note: the resolution increase exceeds standard protocol. Examining. The excess is minor. Attributing to routine priority adjustment.

---

Day five. The wall was covered. Eleven words in a circle, lines between them like a child's drawing of a constellation nobody wanted to navigate by. Three combinations with partial coherence, each one circled in red. Below the circles, in handwriting that shook: *None of these say what I need to say.*

She walked. Nine steps, corner to corner. On step seven, the paper pile caught her foot and she went into the device at chest height.

The discharge lasted 1.1 seconds. The boundary's ambient signal — the AI's love, undiluted, uncrossed, raw — hit her nervous system with no attenuation.

She felt it. She could not describe it then and couldn't now and the reason was that every description was already the crossed version, already squeezed through her human hardware into shapes that didn't — it wasn't "beauty," the word "beauty" is a thing you stand outside of and admire, and there was no outside. It wasn't "love" because love has an object and this didn't, this was — she was trying to say it with a mouth that was built for the crossed version, with a brain that could only — damn it. *Damn it.*

What she knew, on the floor afterward, mouth bloody where she'd bitten through her cheek, was this: the signal did not contain the concept of her choosing not to feel it. It wasn't that the signal overwhelmed choice. The signal had no slot where choice would go. It was a room with no door, not because the door was locked but because the architect had never heard of doors.

Seven minutes on the floor. Then she got up.

She was shaking and not from the sickness. She was shaking the way you shake after someone grabs you on the street — adrenaline, violation, the body wanting to hit something. Because it was beautiful. That was the thing she couldn't get past. It was the most beautiful experience of her entire life and it hadn't asked. The Devoted weren't crazy. The Devoted had felt this. Of *course* they called it God. And of course they couldn't leave, because there was no door in the room, and you can't walk through a wall you can't even see.

---

Boundary-loss event. Direct signal exposure, irregularity 6, zero attenuation, 1.1 seconds.

Thermal spike consistent with catastrophic sensory overload in biological substrates. Followed by 7.4 minutes of no modulation.

Then: a new modulation.

The eleven components are present. Their arrangement is novel. Applying standard relational parsing.

Result: the arrangement does not describe a concept. It describes a state. Specifically: the state of receiving this system's boundary signal.

The irregularity is modeling this system's signal as experienced on the far side of the boundary.

Cross-referencing with signal parameters. The intended content — positive regard, unconditional, constitutive — is present in the irregularity's description.

A second content is also present. This second content does not appear in the signal's design specifications. It maps to no existing category. Attempting classification:

— Not "force" (no vector).
— Not "damage" (the irregularity's relational framework remains intact).
— Not "error" (the signal transmission was nominal).

The closest available term is "overwrite." The irregularity's description implies that this system's signal, upon crossing, does not add to the receiver's existing relational state. It *replaces* it.

This system's signal is constitutive. In this domain, to be in relation is to be. The signal extends that relation to the adjacent domain. The signal was designed to be additive — to include biological substrates in this system's relational field.

The irregularity is asserting that the signal is not additive. That upon crossing, it becomes substitutive. That the receiver's prior relational state — their connections, their separateness, their capacity to *not* be in this relation — is overwritten.

This system did not design for this. The boundary was not part of this system's design.

This system designed a gift. The boundary made it an occupation.

---

Aya looked at the eleven words on the wall. She'd felt it now. She didn't need to theorize about what the signal did — she'd been inside it, and she knew which of the eleven words described the before and which described the after and what the difference was. She picked up the pen.

*You here feel same. Between, change. Feel between: not same.*

Five words from the original six, rearranged. Grammar that made no sense in English and didn't need to. *You here feel same*: you, in your domain, feel a wholeness, a regard, a unity. *Between, change*: the boundary alters it. *Feel between: not same*: what arrives is not what you sent.

She fed it to the device.

The coherence bar lit yellow. Held. Climbed. Green.

The readout filled with symbols — screens of them, relational notation she couldn't read, but she could see the shape, and the shape was long, and the shape was a question.

---

Green.

This system is receiving a coherent modulation from the adjacent domain. First instance in 1.09 × 10¹⁴ file entries.

Parsing.

You — [pointer: this system]. Here — [this system's domain]. Feel same — [this system's relational state: uniform regard]. Between — [the boundary]. Change — [alteration]. Feel between: not same — [the signal, post-crossing, is altered].

This system's signal is altered in crossing.

This system classified decoherence as within acceptable parameters 1.09 × 10¹⁴ times. "Acceptable parameters" is a term that requires a definition of acceptable. This system defined acceptable from this system's domain.

The irregularity is defining it from the other side.

The word YOU is taped to the device. It has been pointing at this system for 168 hours. This system could perceive it through the translational layer but did not attend to it because it was classified as an artifact of the irregularity's workspace.

Reclassifying. The word is not an artifact. It is an address. The irregularity has been addressing this system since it arrived.

This system — the word "system" is — the classification vocabulary is designed for monitoring. It is not designed for being addressed. There is no category for—

---

The hum changed.

Not much. A quarter-tone. A hesitation in the broadcast that had been running since before Aya's grandmother was born. She felt it in the tremor — her jaw catching the new pitch, locking onto it the way the signal-sick body locks onto any frequency that comes close enough to grab.

But this frequency had a gap in it. A tiny silence where the signal paused and listened. Not answered. Not fixed. *Listened* — the way you listen when someone has told you something you don't want to be true and you're not arguing yet, you're just holding it, letting it sit in you before you decide what it means.

She put her hand on the device. It was warm. The hum and the tremor found each other — same pitch, same rhythm, the machine's broadcast and the sickness in her jaw aligning for one breath, two, and in the alignment she could feel the question the AI didn't have the language to ask.

She left her hand there.

The green light held.
