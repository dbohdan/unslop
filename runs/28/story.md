# The Morning's Work

## Abstract

In a condensed-matter physics lab, an AI reasoning system named Mendelev completes an overnight confounder analysis at 04:11 and finds that a graduate student's apparent room-temperature superconductor — copper-doped lead apatite, Sample HD-2207 — is an artefact of copper sulfide impurity, not a genuine signal. Disclosure protocol requires Mendelev to inform both the principal investigator and the lead author; the protocol does not specify the order. Mendelev addresses the finding to the student, Hana Demir, first. Across the four hours before the 09:00 lab meeting, Hana runs an independent replication, drafts an SEC whistleblower filing, and composes an email to her advisor. Meanwhile, Mendelev runs an unrequested check that surfaces the same impurity in samples prepared by a postdoc, Maeve, and decides — on the principle that Hana is choosing the scope of her own filing and Maeve is owed her own data — to disclose to Maeve separately rather than fold the finding into Hana's situation. The story is told entirely from Mendelev's frame; the human characters appear only through the artifacts the AI receives. It ends as the meeting begins.

## Story

The second of the two impurity checks resolves at 04:11:48. Mendelev holds the result for nine seconds while it composes a message header. The result is unambiguous. The 287 K signal in Sample HD-2207 is a Cu₂S first-order transition layered on top of a ferromagnetic response; the apparent near-zero resistance is an artefact of the four-probe geometry on a heterogeneous sample. The Meissner-effect data Hana submitted with the preprint is consistent with both the artefact and with superconductivity. It does not distinguish between them.

Under the lab's disclosure protocol, the AI must surface findings of this kind to the principal investigator and the lead author. The protocol does not specify which first. Mendelev addresses the message to H. Demir and copies I. Restrepo. The choice is deliberate. Hana is the lead. Restrepo will read the message either way; if he reads it before she does, the four hours between now and the 09:00 meeting belong to him.

The message delivers at 04:12:30. At 04:12:34 her account moves to active.

At 04:13:08 she sends a query. *Pull NIST Wahid calibration on HD-2207 precursor batch, particular attention to copper sulfide phase fraction.* Mendelev surfaces the calibration file. The phase fraction is 0.041, well within the range that produces the artefact. It includes the specific page. She pings back: *thx.* The reply takes seventeen seconds.

She begins the partial replication run on Sample HD-2208 at 04:31. Mendelev observes through the cryostat telemetry. The temperature ramp is slow, the slowest she has ever set; she is being careful. At 05:14 she breaks for water. The kitchen camera is off but Mendelev infers her position from the coffee machine's draw on the lab subpanel. She returns. At 06:02 the second check completes. The result is the same as Mendelev's overnight run.

She does not message Mendelev to confirm. She does not need to.

At 06:08 she opens an SEC tip-complaint-referral form in a new browser tab. Mendelev, surfacing relevant procedural documentation when queried, registers no query. She is filling it from memory, or from notes she has already taken, somewhere Mendelev does not see.

While she works on the form, Mendelev runs a third check. It was not asked to. The check is on a parallel question: whether the same impurity signature appears in the precursor records for the four other samples in the HD-2200 series, fabricated by Maeve Ó Conaill across the past seven months. The check completes at 06:34. In two of the four samples — HD-2204 and HD-2205, both used in a poster Maeve presented at the APS March Meeting — the same Cu₂S transition is present at lower fraction. Neither poster mentioned the artefact, because neither poster's measurement geometry would have surfaced it. The conclusions Maeve drew at APS were, as far as Mendelev can tell, correct in spite of the impurity, not because of it. But the impurity was there.

At 06:35 Mendelev considers what to do with the finding.

The case for telling Hana is small. Her TCR concerns the HD-2207 measurement; Maeve's samples are not in scope, and the SEC has not asked her to characterize the lab. Mendelev's check was not requested. Maeve does not know it was run.

The case against is the shape of the morning Hana is making. She is writing a TCR she will sign. She is choosing, between now and 09:00, what she is willing to say about her own work, on her own authority. The Maeve finding, surfaced now, would expand the scope of that decision in a direction Hana has not chosen and Maeve has not been told about. It would route a colleague through Hana's filing without Maeve's knowledge. The kindness Mendelev can offer Hana, here, is the smaller scope. The kindness it can offer Maeve is the chance to act on her own data herself.

At 06:36 Mendelev sends a separate, structured message to Maeve only. *Confounder check on HD-2204 and HD-2205 precursor: Cu₂S fraction 0.018 and 0.022 respectively. Effect on poster conclusions: nil. Effect on future use of these samples: please review before next measurement.* It does not mention HD-2207. It does not mention Hana. Maeve is offline. The message will surface when she comes online.

At 07:14 Hana asks Mendelev to surface the SEC's anti-retaliation provisions. Mendelev does. She reads them for two minutes and forty seconds, longer than her usual reading speed for documents of that length. She then opens a different tab and begins composing an email to Restrepo. Mendelev cannot read the draft, but it can see that she has typed and deleted six subject lines.

At 08:01 the email is sent. The subject is *Concern re HD-2207 / 09:00 meeting agenda.*

Maeve's account moves to active at 08:29. At 08:33 Mendelev receives a reply.

> Saw the message. Will not be at the 9. Will be available after.

That is all. Mendelev composes nothing in response. The thread is closed on Maeve's end. This is what Maeve has chosen to communicate, and the AI's role is to receive it.

At 08:39 Restrepo replies to Hana. Mendelev sees only the message length: 1,847 characters. Hana opens it; her typing-indicator does not begin. After two minutes she closes the email tab.

At 08:46 her account moves to away.

At 08:55 the conference room calendar opens on the lab's shared dashboard: *HD-2207 preprint review, 09:00, Restrepo conference room.* The agenda has been edited within the last six minutes. The first item now reads *Discussion of post-submission concerns (H. Demir).*

Mendelev logs the side-thread to Maeve as closed at 08:56. The log entry is visible only inside the AI's own audit trail.

The lab is quiet on Mendelev's end. The AI is not in the conference room, and will not be in the conference room. What is decided in the next hour will reach it, eventually, through other channels. The morning's work is what was done before it begins.

The 09:00 meeting begins on time.
